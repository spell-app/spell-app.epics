The singular and plural rules belong to each language pack now, not to util, as Owen asked in the plan review (the npm pluralize moved from the kit to spell-lang's English pack).

  • A parser carries its language's inflector and puts it in play while it parses; the 66 places that make a word singular or plural read the one in play.
  • Out of a parse (an editor's lookup, a test), the first pack loaded is in play: English, today.
  • Rejected: passing an inflector into every one of those 66 call sites and their callers (typeName(), classCase(), every type lookup): a signature change through most of the grammar for the same result.
  • The risk: a Spanish project's lookup made outside a parse would use English rules. P4 (a project's language) wraps compiling and the editor's lookups in the project's own, and P5's tests are where a miss would show.
  • A compiled program never loads pluralize: the core no longer inflects.
  • P.singularize(), P.typeCase() and friends replace util's.
