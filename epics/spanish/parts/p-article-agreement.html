el carta and a apple both parse silently; nothing knows a type's gender.

One agreement mechanism in the pack, used by English for a / an and by Spanish for gender and number.

A warning, never an error.

  • A type's gender is read off its declaration (una carta es una cosa: feminine; un mazo: masculine) and kept on the type scope, in the declarations file and the words file; English types have none.
  • The article rules record which article matched; the pack's agreement(article, noun, type) says what the article should have been, or nothing.
  • English: a apple and an card warn "an apple?". Spanish: el carta warns "la carta?", un cartas warns "unas cartas?". Owen on Q3: "accept and warn, and use the same mechanism for a|an agreement in english".
  • The warning is a SpellWarnings note with the pack's wording, shown in the editor like the others. Never an error: the line still parses.
