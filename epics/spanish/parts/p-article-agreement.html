el carta and a apple both parse silently; nothing knows a type's gender.

One agreement mechanism in the pack, used by English for a / an and by Spanish for gender and number.

A warning, never an error.

  • A type's gender is read off its declaration, and kept on the type scope, in the declarations file and the words file; English types have none.
    • una carta es una cosa: feminine
    • un mazo: masculine
  • The article rules record which article matched; the pack's agreement(article, noun, type) says what the article should have been, or nothing.
  • The warnings, by language:
    • English: a apple warns "an apple?"
    • English: an card warns too
    • Spanish: el carta warns "la carta?"
    • Spanish: un cartas warns "unas cartas?"
    Owen on Q3: "accept and warn, and use the same mechanism for a|an agreement in english".
  • The warning is a SpellWarnings note with the pack's wording, shown in the editor like the others. Never an error: the line still parses.
  • A type's gender read off its declaration: una carta feminine, un mazo masculine; kept in the declarations file only when set
  • One agreement check per pack:
    • English warns a apple (Did you mean "an apple"?) and an card
    • Spanish warns el carta (¿Querías decir "la carta"?) and un cartas
  • A warning in the editor, never an error; no new warnings in any test project
