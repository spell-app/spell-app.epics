What the plain layout box is called, and how it says its spacing (gap 2).

  • It lays its children out in a column or a row, with a gap between them, alignment and wrapping, and no look of its own: what a <div style="display: flex"> does in HTML.
  • The examples need it most: Solitaire, the task list and the calculator all lay themselves out with native <div>s today.
  • Spell UI already has utility classes for it, .ui-stack and .ui-cluster, and a spacing ladder of tokens (--ui-space-3xs to --ui-space-3xl), but no tag.
  • The attributes come from words Spell UI already uses: horizontal (lists, labels, statistics), alignment (menus), vertical-align (grids), wrapping (code).
  • SwiftUI's, Chakra's and Material UI's word, and the utility class's already.
  • A column by default; horizontal makes it a row.
  • gap takes the spacing words: none tiny small medium large huge.
<stack horizontal gap="small" alignment="center">
  <button>New game</button>
  <button>Undo</button>
</stack>
  • The plainest word: "a box".
  • Cost: says nothing about laying things out; needs row or column to say which, and those are the grid's words. 
<box direction="row" gap="small">
  • <stack> for a column, <cluster> for a row that wraps, as the utility classes split them.
  • Cost: two tags to learn and translate for one idea.
  • New: ui-stack; in a drawing <stack>.
  • Attributes: horizontal, gap, alignment, vertical-align, wrapping, fluid.
  • The .ui-stack utility class stays, for plain HTML.
