What the page frame is called: the tag that is a whole page, with its header, navigation, main content, side and footer.

Owen's comment: "What is this concept in HTML? body?"

  • HTML has no one element for it.
  • <body> holds the whole document, everything shown.
  • The regions are separate elements inside it: <header>, <nav>, <main>, <aside>, <footer>.
  • Screen readers list those regions as "landmarks", and jump between them: "skip to main content".
  • The page frame would be the body AND those regions in one tag: a ui-root that is the whole window, paints its background and follows the system's light or dark setting (the sketch in ). 
<page>
  <header slot="header" level="1">Solitaire</header>
  <menu slot="nav">…</menu>
  <stack>… the game …</stack>
  <card slot="aside">Best scores</card>
  <text slot="footer">Made with Spell</text>
</page>
  • Children with no slot are the main content.
  • How a drawing says slot= is elemental-spell's (its named parts, Q48).
  • What people call it: a page.
  • Free: Spell's own page template is <spell-page> (), so a Page template draws inside a <page>.
  • HTML's word, for people who know it.
  • Cost: <body> is the whole document in HTML, not its regions; "body" also means a card's or a modal's main text in Spell UI's own words.
  • New: ui-page, a ui-root that is the whole window; in a drawing <page>.
  • Slots header, nav, aside, footer, the rest the main content; each a landmark; a "Skip to content" link.
  • One per page: a second is reported in the console.
