r1's concept 11: "header -> title. Owen: 'HTML title => tooltip, global-ish attribute'". So a card's heading becomes title=, and the old title tooltip becomes tooltip=. But the browser still treats title as a tooltip.

  • On any element, the browser shows title's text in its own small tooltip when the pointer rests on it.
  • So <ui-card title="Hearts"> shows a "Hearts" tooltip over the whole card, its heading included, after about a second.
  • Screen readers may also read it a second time, as the card's description.
  • Families that take header today: about twelve (card, item, modal, flyout, message, section, step, statistic, list ...). 
<ui-card title="Hearts" tooltip="The red suit">   <!-- r1 -->
<!-- hovering anywhere on the card:  the browser's own "Hearts" box, and Spell UI's "The red suit" -->
  • Owen's word stays: <card title="Hearts"> in a drawing.
  • The component hides the browser's tooltip: first by a blank title inside its own drawing (the browser shows the nearest one), else by moving the text off the element once read.
  • Cost: a quick test in each browser first; if it has to move the text off the element, the attribute no longer shows in the page's markup, and the inspector reads the property instead.
  • <ui-card heading="Hearts">: no clash, nothing to hide.
  • title stays an old name for tooltip, as HTML has it.
  • Cost: not the word Owen picked; heading and header are easy to mix up.
  • Nothing to build.
  • Cost: every titled card, item and modal shows its heading again as a tooltip.
  • header becomes title on every family that has it; HTML's tooltip is tooltip.
  • The global attributes phase starts with a test: does a blank title inside the component hide the host's tooltip in Chrome, Safari and Firefox?
