A new page is written in two passes: first what it must DO, then how it LOOKS.

1. Its jobs

  • a short list, in Owen's words: "see an analysis's runs", "start a new run", "compare two runs"
  • each job names the data it needs (runs, of this analysis) and where it leads (a modal, another page)
  • the jobs are SAVED with the page and kept for good, through the mockup and into the real site: the mockup is checked against them, testing with people asks each one, and the finished page still lists them
  • a job may be written as an Agile story instead of a plain line: "As a lab manager, I want to compare two runs, so that I can see which settings changed", with its checks ("done when ...") under it

2. Its mockup

  • sections laid out in the page frame (header, side navigation, main, a side panel), from Spell UI's own components: tables, cards, forms, buttons
  • drawn in the MOCKUP theme: wobbly ink borders, a handwriting font, greys, so nobody mistakes it for the finished look
  • CLICK-THROUGH: a button opens a modal, a row opens a slide-out panel, a link goes to another page with its parameter filled in
  • filled with sample rows from 1.4

Because a mockup is built of real Spell UI components, switching the theme later (sketchy to the company's) changes its look and nothing else.
