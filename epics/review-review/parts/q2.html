You asked for green = decided "in this or the last commit", grey = decided earlier.

  • Example: D7 was decided yesterday in a /review; since then there were six commits (P4, P5, two fixes ...). By the commit rule D7 is already grey, though you haven't looked at the doc since.
  • The point of green, as I read it: "this changed since you last looked".
  • Each /review stamps the doc with when it started.
  • Green: decided or reviewed after the stamp before last, i.e. new since your previous review.
  • Survives any number of commits in between.
  • As you wrote it: the item's change is in HEAD or HEAD~1.
  • Simple, but a busy phase turns everything grey before you look.
  • Green for, say, 2 days after the decision.
  • Easy to reason about, but unrelated to when you actually read it.
  • A /bedtime run's changes stay green until you review them.
  • plan-doc stamps each change with its time, and the doc with the last commit's time, so the page can color without running git.
