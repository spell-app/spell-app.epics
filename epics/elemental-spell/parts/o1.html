Elemental spell is a second way to write the same Spell program: each piece of a template (a property, a phrase, an action, a drawing) wrapped in its own tag, with plain markdown between them.

  • Code spell, today's form, reads like an essay; it stays, in .spell files.
  • Elemental spell is the same program cut into chunks, in .spellx files ("spellx" is our flavour of JSX; Q9).
  • The two must be 100% equivalent, and switching between them must be instant.

Why

  • A novice who knows a little HTML can read it: each chunk says what it is in its tag.
  • A notebook editor, like Jupyter's or Observable's, can show it:
    • the markdown between tags is a prose cell
    • each tag is a code cell
  • Each kind of chunk can get its own editor: a property's tag is a natural fit for a <ui-property>.
  • An agent can walk the chunks one by one, and find one by a selector: spell-property[name=color].

The playing card, in both forms

Owen's card from the kickoff prompt, tidied a little.

  • It has one drawing per side: its parent, the library's two sided view (Q1), draws the front when it's face up and the back when it's face down.
  • The prompt's card had "is a face card" twice; this has it once.
  • The prompt's is the (rank) of (suits) is (suit) here: a blank names a property.
  • The prompt's alias="it is a (suit)" drops its "it", like the other phrases.
<spell-view name="playing card" aka="card" template="two sided view">
	A playing card has a suit and a rank.
	It shows its front when it's face up, and its back when it's face down.

	<spell-property name="suit" one-of="clubs, diamonds, hearts, spades"
		description="Which of the four suits it belongs to"
		alias="is a (suit)"
	/>
	<spell-property name="color" one-of="red, black"
		alias="is (color)"
		alias="is a (color) card"
	>
		red if it is a diamond or it is a heart
		black otherwise
	</spell-property>
	<spell-property name="rank" one-of="ace, 2 ... 10, jack, queen, king"
		description="Ace is low:  ace = 1 ... king = 13"
		alias="is a (rank)"
	/>

	<spell-alias name="is the (rank) of (suit)" />
	<spell-alias name="is a face card">
		if its rank is jack, queen or king
	</spell-alias>
	<spell-alias name="is a number card">
		if it is not a face card
	</spell-alias>

	<spell-action name="turn it over">
		<description>Flips the card:  face up becomes face down, and back.</description>
		if it is face up
			turn it face down
		otherwise
			turn it face up
	</spell-action>

	<spell-draw section="front">
		<ui-text color={its color}>{its picture}</ui-text>
	</spell-draw>
	<spell-draw section="back">
		<ui-image src="card-back.svg" />
	</spell-draw>
</spell-view>

Only the declaration lines change shape. Every body -- a property's value lines, an action's steps, a drawing's markup -- is the same code spell, word for word, in both forms.

What code spell has to grow

Owen's call (Q2): where the elemental form says something code spell can't, code spell grows. For this card, five things:

  1. A property that has a list of choices AND works out its value: - its "color" is one of red, black: with value lines under it.
  2. A property's own phrases, indented under it (today they're separate lines beside it).
  3. A drawing per side, - to draw its front: and - to draw its back:, for a two sided view. Front and back drawings were taken out on 2026-10-22 (epic ); they come back only for views whose template is two sided (Q15).
  4. A drawing whose body is just markup lines, with no return and no quotes around "draw it" (Q14).
  5. The two sided view itself: a template in Spell's library.

Today's grammar already has the rest: aka on a multi-word name, one of ... 2 ... 10 ..., phrases with blanks, to turn it over:.
