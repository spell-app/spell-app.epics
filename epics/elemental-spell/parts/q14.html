A is good, assume we can have a <description> or <comment> block for comments inside the draw method

Yes: both fit, and they mean two different things.

  • <description>, first in the drawing: what the whole drawing shows. It's the drawing's own description, as on any other chunk (Q12).
  • <comment>, anywhere among its lines: a note about the lines right after it. It draws nothing. 
<spell-draw>
	<description>
		The card's picture, in its suit's color;  the blue back when face down.
	</description>
	if it is face up
		<ui-text part="picture" color={its color}>{its picture}</ui-text>
	otherwise
		<comment>
			every card shares one back:  styles.css sets its pattern
		</comment>
		<ui-text part="picture" color="blue">🂠</ui-text>
</spell-draw>

  • In code spell they're both comment lines: right above - to draw it: is its description (today's docstring rule), and a comment line inside the body is a comment.
  • Neither may sit inside a markup tag: there, an HTML comment (<!-- ... -->) does the job, and it isn't drawn either.
  • A short one-line <comment>...</comment> on one line works too.
