Nothing reads a .spellx file: today's tokenizer would swallow a template tag as one piece of markup.

A new reader of our own turns a .spellx file into a tree of chunks, in English and Spanish.

An unknown word is an error that names it.

  • prefixed template tags outside, short words inside (Q37); tag heads may be spell phrases with spaces (Q45)
  • quoted names on every tag (Q39); repeatable attributes (Q6)
  • </> closes the innermost tag, a named closer is checked, /> self-closes (Q40, Q47)
  • bodies kept as raw spell, indented from their tag (Q7); loose text is a markdown prose cell (Q12)
  • the words are a closed set per language, from the words files: spell- / encanto-, accents kept (Q25, Q36, Q49)
  • our own reader, never an HTML or JSX parser (Q5, Q8)

A new spell-parser/src/spellx/, with its namespace and README-spellx.md; the tokenizer's JSX reading (parser/tokenizer/Tokenizer.ts, Tokens.ts) where drawings need it.

Reader tests: every o1 example reads to the expected chunk tree; bad input gives the named error.
