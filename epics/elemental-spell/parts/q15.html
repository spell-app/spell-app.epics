A two sided view (Q1) draws its front or its back; the card says what each one looks like. What does the view write for "put the front here", and what is it called?

The parent template decides WHERE each part goes; the child template says WHAT each part is. The two sided view, in both forms: 

<spell-view name="two sided view" template="view">
	A view with a front and a back:  it shows one at a time.

	<spell-property name="direction" one-of="up, down" alias="is face (direction)" />

	<spell-action name="turn it over">
		if it is face up
			set its direction to down
		otherwise
			set its direction to up
	</spell-action>

	<spell-draw>
		if it is face up
			<section name="front" />
		otherwise
			<section name="back" />
	</spell-draw>
</spell-view>
 
// A view with a front and a back:  it shows one at a time.
a two sided view is a view where:
	- its "direction" is one of up, down
		- it "is face (direction)"

	- to turn it over:
		if it is face up
			set its direction to down
		otherwise
			set its direction to up

	- to draw it:
		if it is face up
			<section name="front" />
		otherwise
			<section name="back" />

  • A section here is a part a template draws that its subclasses fill in: a "draw its front" method, called where <section name="front"/> stands.
  • Spell already has a near cousin, a slot: <children name="header"/> marks a place that a CHILD element fills, written <h3 in="header">. A slot is filled by what's put inside the element on the page; a section by the subclass's own drawing.
  • <section> is also an HTML tag, and Spell UI has <ui-section>. Since we compile it (Q4), we can take the name over, but a reader who knows HTML may be surprised.
  • <spell-section name="front"/> in the parent; <spell-draw section="front"> in the child.
  • Clearly Spell's, never mistaken for HTML's <section>; the spell- prefix is already Spell's own.
