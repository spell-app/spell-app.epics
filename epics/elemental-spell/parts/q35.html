When several actions answer the same key, the first one that's enabled runs and takes the key (Q33). The question is what "first" means.

The example for each option: a card sits in a pile, in the game. The card, the pile and the game each have an action on the delete key ("remove this card", "clear the pile", "end the game").

  • The thing with keyboard focus offers its actions first, then the thing around it, and so on up to the app (macOS calls this the responder chain).
  • Card focused: delete removes the card. Pile focused: it clears the pile.
  • A right click on a card that isn't focused still asks whatever has focus.
  • Keys follow the focus chain, as in A.
  • What the mouse starts (a right click, a context menu, a drag) starts its chain at the thing under the mouse: a right click on the card offers the card's menu even when the pile has focus.
  • Whatever the person touched last goes first, by mouse or by key.
  • Simple, but surprising: hovering over one card while typing to another doesn't change it, and nothing says which thing "last" is.

Why B: it's what people expect from desktop apps. Keys go where the focus is, and a click acts on what you clicked.

  • Focus only lands on focusable elements, so clicking a plain card moves no focus at all.
    • A view is focusable by default, so a click on it focuses it.
  • A shadow root hides what's focused inside it: the page only sees the outer element.
    • Spell keeps "the current thing" itself, as focus and clicks move, and doesn't ask the page.
  • The chain is Spell's own: each thing's container, up to the app. It doesn't depend on how the browser's elements nest.
  • Keys go to the focused thing first, then up through its containers to the app.
  • A right click or a drag starts at the thing under the mouse.
  • Every view can take focus, and Spell keeps track of the current thing itself.
