In Carta.es.spellx, <acción "voltearla boca arriba"> defines voltearBocaArriba(), but a call to it, voltear la carta boca arriba, compiles to turnFaceUp(): the built-in two-sided view's action of the same words. The same file as .es.spell calls its own.

  • Found by P12's examples test: Solitario converts, then its calls differ (6 lines); marked a known gap there.
  • Not the converter's: a hand-written .es.spellx does it too. A made-up action (saltarla alto) is called right, so it's the built-in's rule winning over the read-ahead one (MethodDefinition.callAhead()) in the spellx twin parser.
  • English hides it: Card's turn it face up and the built-in's have the same name, turnFaceUp.

What P13 found (not fixed; the Solitario pair test is marked as expected to fail):

  • Carta.es.spellx compiles turnFaceUp / isFaceDirection, the built-in two-sided view's methods. Carta.es.spell compiles the card's own, voltearBocaArriba / estáBocaDirección.
  • The skeleton pre-pass does declare the card's Spanish methods (para voltearla boca arriba ...), so their words are known.
  • Suspected cause: twinFor() copies the built-in templates' rules from their layers as the twin parser's own rules (addTwinsRule). So they tie with the card's own method rules, and in a tie the later rule wins. Or the thing's type is lost in the twin.
  • English hides it: there the card's words and the template's are the same.
