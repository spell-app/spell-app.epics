The brand panel moved into Spell UI under the plain tag <ui-panel>, with a vocabulary of its own.

  • Chosen over keeping a brand prefix (<ui-brand-panel>), or a variation of the section (<ui-section panel>):
    • Owen asked for ui-panel
    • a panel is a generic widget, and generic elements move into Spell UI (decision D2)
    • a tag of its own keeps the inspector look out of every plain section's sheet
  • It is still a subclass of the section (UISection).
  • Its OWN vocabulary is built on the section's (ui-panel.vocabulary.en.ts).
    • It spreads the section's (...sectionVocabulary), plus its tag, topics, aka and two descriptions.
    • The brand's trick was to define the section's vocabulary under another tag.
    • Now the class's description, the docs' API table and the root element's catalog all say ui-panel.
  • Page authors write <ui-panel>: the same attributes, slots, parts, events and look as before.
  • Class words in the shadow root: ui ... section panel, plus sub; they were section brand panel.
  • Its layers are ui.components.panel.*; its lib entry, @spell-app/ui/ui-panel.
  • A fallback (PanelFallback): the section's, in the panel's class grammar.
  • A docs page, /ui/components/ui-panel.html.
    • The brand's own page is gone, and the Brand index's row links Spell UI's.
  • A types example, and visual baselines (Chromium, Firefox, WebKit; local).
  • 15 tests: the element, and the fallback.
  • Fixed on the way, in Safari / WebKit: sub-head bands stopped short of the box's edges, and a folded last band was cut off.
    • The outer content always clipped, and WebKit has no overflow-clip-margin.
    • It now clips only while the section animates (as the section does), which WebKit never does.
    • Chromium is unchanged.
  • The brand field's tests now import spell-ui themselves.
    • They had passed only because the brand panel imported the whole barrel, as a side effect.
