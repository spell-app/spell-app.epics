The colour picker's big plane is now the HSL square for the current hue, not the OKLCH triangle.

  • Owen (2026-10-04), on the first picker: "that triangle just looks weird".
    • Its plane was OKLCH Lightness × Colourfulness, which shows only the triangle of each hue a screen can show.
    • He asked for an HSL picker.
  • The square:
    • saturation 0 -> 100%, left to right
    • lightness 100% at the top -> 0% at the bottom
    • every point is a real sRGB colour: no hatch, no gamut mapping

The alternative: the HSV / HSB square most design tools use (Figma, Photoshop, macOS).

  • Saturation across, brightness up: the top-right corner is the PURE hue, and the top edge runs white -> hue.
  • In HSL the pure hue sits in the MIDDLE of the right edge (L 50%), and the top edge is all white.
  • HSV makes vivid colours easier to hit.
  • HSL matches the HSL row's numbers, and CSS's hsl(), one to one.
  • Switching later means changing:
    • the square's two gradients
    • the pointer and key mapping
    • the conversions (Palette would need hsv)

The square is CSS, not a canvas, and it's exact.

  • HSL is linear in (gamma-encoded) sRGB along both axes.
  • So it's a grey -> pure-hue gradient (the L 50% row), under white and black fading over the top and bottom halves, all in srgb.
  • Measured against Palette.hslToRgb() at six points: within 1-2 / 255 per channel (8-bit rounding).
  • A hue change only repaints: nothing is computed per pixel.
  • The hue slider's rainbow is static CSS too: HSL is linear per 60°.
  • No triangle: a full square of real colours for every hue.
  • The HSL row's numbers and the square agree; a vivid colour is at the right edge's middle, not its top.
  • No canvas, no redraw cost.
