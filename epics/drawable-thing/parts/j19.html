A drawable list draws every one of its items, so a stacked pile in Drawable Solitaire now holds all its cards, the last one on top.

  • The original Solitaire drew only a stacked pile's bottom card, the one you see: {draw its bottom card}.
  • With a slot, the pile can't choose: every item is a child element, and the slot shows them all.
  • The pile's <css> stacks them: each card is placed at the same spot, so the last one covers the rest.
  • A tableau's own <css> lays its cards one below the other instead.
  • So the game always has 52 card elements on the page, wherever the cards are.
/* its cards, stacked:  the last on top */
::slotted(*) {
	position: absolute;
	left: 0;
	top: 0;
	width: 100%;
}

A pile that shows only its top card could be a plain property drawn in its frame, as the original did, rather than a drawable list. Nothing needs that today.

  • Every pile in Drawable Solitaire holds all its cards as elements: 52 in all.
  • Which card shows on a stacked pile is CSS's job (the last on top), not the pile's to draw.
