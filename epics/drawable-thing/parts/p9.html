A spell app's layout is all to draw code: it can't be laid out in HTML from ui-* tags and spell's own.

The <spell-app> tag takes its own markup as the layout (Owen's Klondike sketch, O6).

  • The holes in curly brackets, and the actions, are spell, read in the program's scope.
  • Spell's tags in the layout are bound to the program's things.
  • A <spell> block adds code to the element it sits in.
  • Owen's Klondike layout runs: it draws, plays, the score updates and AutoPlay works.
  • A <spell> block inside a tag adds methods, variables and handlers to that element; when clicked is its click handler.

  1. What you can try: a <spell-app>'s layout, written in plain HTML, now runs the spell written inside it, against its program.
    • The demo page, Owen's Klondike sketch on Drawable Solitaire: klondike-layout.html. How to open it: the Klondike layout test, V5; not yet tried by hand.
    • A small layout, from the browser test: 
      <spell-app>
  <p>Score {score of the game}</p>                  ... "Score 3", kept up to date
  <button action="{bump the score}">Bump</button>  ... a click:  "Score 4"
  <test-project-tile id="T1">
    <spell>
      when clicked
        set its count to its count + 1
    </spell>
  </test-project-tile>
  <p>{the count of T1} left</p>                    ... "2 left", then "3 left" after a click on the tile
</spell-app>
  2. How a name in the layout's spell is found, innermost first (Owen's answer to Q18: "the component, the class, the page, the program"):
    1. the component: it and its are the thing of the drawable tag the spell sits in
    2. its class: the thing's own properties and methods, through it
    3. the page: each drawable tag with an id is a name, like T1, typed by its thing
    4. the program: its types, functions and rules, and its top-level things, read from the running program (J28)
  3. What each kind of spell does:
    • A hole in text, Score {score of the game}, is its own piece of text, kept up to date as what it reads changes.
      • A thing shows as its label; an object as JSON.
      • A hole that doesn't read is tried again with the in front (J26).
      • A hole must use all its text; one that doesn't read shows ⚠, and the program's console says why.
    • A hole in an attribute, value="{...}", is kept up to date too.
      • An object or a function is set as a property; nothing or false takes the attribute off.
      • One with a space in it needs quotes (C15).
    • An action, action="{auto-play}", is spell statements run on a click; they may wait. The attribute is taken off.
    • A <spell> block holds when <event> handlers, listening on the element it sits in (J27).
      • clicked, double-clicked, hovered, focused, changed; any other word is the event's own name.
      • Indents are spaces or tabs; the block itself is hidden.
      • A method or variable in a block is an error, for now: less than Q12's answer.
    • On the next run, all of it is taken back: the text, the listeners and the attributes, as written.
  4. The parser loads only when it's needed:
    • The runner loads LayoutSpell, which holds spell's parser, as a separate piece, and only for a layout with spell in it.
    • Checking for spell needs no parser: layoutHasSpell().
    • So a <spell-app> without spell in its layout loads no more than before.
    • Core's new spellCore.things.exported hands the layout the program's things.
