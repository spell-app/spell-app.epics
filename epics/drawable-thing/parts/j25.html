In a <spell-app>'s layout, a drawable tag shows one of the program's things: the one of its type whose name is the tag's id. Owen would notice it as <solitaire-tableau id="T1"> showing the tableau the program named "T1".

  • "In a layout" means inside the closest <spell-app>.
    • Outside one, on a plain page (P8), a hand-written tag still makes its own thing.
  • The type must match EXACTLY, not a sub-type.
    • A <solitaire-pile> tag doesn't find a tableau, though a tableau is a kind of pile.
  • With no id, the tag shows the program's ONE thing of its type, when it made exactly one.
  • With no match, the tag shows a stand-in saying so, not a blank.
    • e.g. ⚠ no Tile named "T9"
    • with no id and several things of the type, it asks for an id
  • The lookup is tracked: a tag finds its thing as soon as the program makes it, even after the tag is drawn.
  • Whether a <spell-app> HAS a layout is read once, as its runner starts.
    • Markup added later doesn't switch a running app over to it.
  • Where:
    • core's thingFor()
    • boundThing, in app's SpellElement 
<spell-app>
  <div class="row">
    <layout-tile id="T1"></layout-tile>   <!-- the tile named T1 -->
    <layout-tile id="T2"></layout-tile>   <!-- the tile named T2 -->
    <layout-tile id="T9"></layout-tile>   <!-- ⚠ no Tile named "T9" -->
  </div>
</spell-app>
  • A layout's tag shows the program's thing of its exact type, named by its id.
  • A pile tag never shows a tableau: a sub-type needs its own tag.
  • A layout added to a running <spell-app> waits for the next run.
