A drawable element can't show or take a thing it holds: a card's pile, an element's own thing, a list's items are JavaScript properties only (J23).
  • A thing is named in text as Type#name (Tableau#T1) or Type#1 (the first of that type, counting from 1), and an attribute can hold one.
  • A tag's name drops the word drawable: <solitaire-tableau id="T1">.
  • Owen (picking A on J23): "the web component name would be <solitare-tableau id="T1"> -- no <drawable- prefix"; "Tableau#1 should be the first tableau on the page, using 1-based indexing"; "Pile#T1 should work to find tableaus because Pile is a superclass of Tableau".
  • A reference names one thing: Tableau#T1 by its name, Tableau#1 by its place, counting from 1.
  • A type finds its sub-types' things too: Pile#T1 finds tableau T1. (Pile#2, a place across sub-types, is a todo for later.)
  • A property holding a thing shows as its reference on the element: <solitaire-card pile="Tableau#T1">, and follows the thing (as P13's attributes do); setting the attribute sets the property.
  • A layout's tag can say which thing it draws with one: thing="Pile#stock", beside id="T1".
  • A drawable element's tag drops the word drawable from its project's name: the Drawable Solitaire example's tableau is <solitaire-tableau>.
