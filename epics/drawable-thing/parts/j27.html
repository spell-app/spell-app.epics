A <spell> block in the layout holds event handlers only, for now: when clicked and the lines indented under it. A method or variable written in one is an error.

  • This builds less than Owen's answer to Q12 (Q12, option A), which said:
    • "it" and "its" are the tag's thing: built.
    • any other name is looked up in the program: built (with the class and the page's ids between, Q18).
    • methods and variables the block makes belong to that one element's thing: NOT built.
  • How a handler works:
    • It listens on the element the block sits in.
    • Its word is the event:
      • clicked is a click
      • double-clicked a double click
      • hovered the mouse coming in
      • focused and changed as they say
      • any other word is used as the event's own name
    • Its lines are spell statements, and may wait (await).
    • Indents may be spaces or tabs.
    • The block itself is hidden.
  • Anything else in a block shows on the program's console as an error, e.g. a <spell> block holds "when <event>" handlers.
  • Where: LayoutSpell.bindBlock(). 
<drawable-solitaire-stock-pile>
  <spell>
    when double-clicked
      play from the stock pile        ... a handler:  built
  </spell>
</drawable-solitaire-stock-pile>

  <spell>
    to deal three cards ...           ... a method of this one pile:  an error, for now
  </spell>
  • Chosen and built.
  • Owen's examples need only handlers: the stock pile's click.
  • Each handler is compiled on its own, like a hole.
  • The cost: a block can't give its one element a method or a variable yet.
  • What Q12's answer described: the block's methods and variables belong to that element's thing alone.
  • It means compiling the block as a subclass of the thing's type, made in the page, and swapping the thing to it.
  • The cost: much bigger, and it touches how core makes things; no example needs it yet.
  • A <spell> block holds when <event> handlers; anything else is an error.
  • Q12's methods and variables per element wait for a later step, option B.
