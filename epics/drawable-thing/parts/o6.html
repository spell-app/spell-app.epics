The other way round: a spell app laid out in plain HTML, from Spell UI's ui-* tags and the app's own drawable tags, with spell written inside it. Owen's sketch of a Klondike solitaire app: 

<spell-app name="Solitaire" grid fluid columns="7">
  <ui-row>
    <ui-header columns="2">Klondike Solitaire</ui-header>
    <ui-text>Score {score of the game}</ui-text>
    <ui-button action={autoplay}>AutoPlay</ui-button>
  </ui-row>
  <ui-row>
    <solitaire-discards-pile></solitaire-discards-pile>
    <solitaire-stock-pile></solitaire-stock-pile>
    <ui-spacer></ui-spacer>
    <solitaire-foundations-clubs></solitaire-foundations-clubs>
    <!-- ... the other three foundations -->
  </ui-row>
  <ui-row>
    <solitaire-tableau id="T1"></solitaire-tableau>
    <!-- ... T2 to T7 -->
  </ui-row>
</spell-app>
  • The <spell-app> tag's own markup is the app's layout.
  • The holes are spell, read in the program's scope:
    • {score of the game} shows the score, and updates when it changes
    • action={autoplay} runs the program's autoplay when the button is pressed
  • The app's own tags (the piles, the tableaus) are bound to the program's things.

Owen's next idea: a <spell> block in the markup is spell code attached to the scope of the element it sits in. 

<solitaire-stock-pile>
  <spell>
    when clicked
      - deal three cards to the discards
  </spell>
</solitaire-stock-pile>
  • The block can add methods, variables and handlers to that element.
  • when clicked is the click handler for the whole stock pile.
  • It needs spell's events, from output-targets' P14 (see O3).
