The other way round: a spell app laid out in plain HTML, from Spell UI's ui-* tags and the app's own drawable tags, with spell written inside it. Owen's sketch of a Klondike solitaire app: 

<spell-app name="Solitaire" grid fluid columns="7">
  <ui-row>
    <ui-header columns="2">Klondike Solitaire</ui-header>
    <ui-text>Score {score of the game}</ui-text>
    <ui-button action={autoplay}>AutoPlay</ui-button>
  </ui-row>
  <ui-row>
    <solitaire-discards-pile></solitaire-discards-pile>
    <solitaire-stock-pile></solitaire-stock-pile>
    <ui-spacer></ui-spacer>
    <solitaire-foundations-clubs></solitaire-foundations-clubs>
    <!-- ... the other three foundations -->
  </ui-row>
  <ui-row>
    <solitaire-tableau id="T1"></solitaire-tableau>
    <!-- ... T2 to T7 -->
  </ui-row>
</spell-app>

Built (P9, P12): the demo page plays it, written a little differently.

<spell-app project="@examples/Drawable Solitaire" name="Solitaire">
  <div class="row">
    <div class="title">Klondike Solitaire</div>
    <div class="score">Score {the score of the game}</div>
    <button on-click="{auto-play}">AutoPlay</button>
  </div>
  <div class="row">
    <drawable-solitaire-discard-pile></drawable-solitaire-discard-pile>
    <drawable-solitaire-stock-pile>
      <spell>
        when double clicked
          play from the stock pile
      </spell>
    </drawable-solitaire-stock-pile>
    <drawable-solitaire-foundation id="clubs"></drawable-solitaire-foundation>
    <!-- ... the other three foundations, then T1 to T7 -->
  </div>
</spell-app>
  • The <spell-app> tag's own markup is the app's layout: the game's own to draw isn't drawn (Q10).
  • Each drawable tag is the program's thing named by its id, or its one thing of that type (Q9, J25).
  • The holes are spell, read in the page and live:
    • {the score of the game} shows the score, and updates when it changes
    • a hole is spell exactly, as everywhere else: {score of the game} shows ⚠ (J26, B)
    • names are looked up in order: the tag's own thing, its class, the page, then the program (Q18)
  • Any element handles events: a Spell UI tag, a plain one, or a drawable one (P12).
    • as an attribute, either spelling: on-click="{auto-play}" or when-clicked="{...}" (Q20, C)
    • or a <spell> block inside it: when clicked: bump the score on one line, or the statements indented under it
    • on as well as when, and events in several words: when double clicked
  • A <markdown> block draws formatted text, its holes live (P14); a < in it is written &lt; (J38).
  • A child written in="header" lands in a frame's named place, as in a drawing (J35).
  • The rough edges: a word used twice in an unquoted hole needs quotes (C15), a formatter flattens a <spell> block (C16), and a layout knows only its own project's names (C17).
