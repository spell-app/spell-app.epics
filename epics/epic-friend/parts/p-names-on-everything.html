About a dozen places decide "is this Claude's?" by testing the author against the literal "Owen", and every writer stamps "Owen" on what a person writes.

  • Claude's replies and status cards name whose session wrote them, "Claude (Ana)"; just "Claude" when it's your own. (Q7)
  • A person who calls themselves "Claude" stays a person: Claude's replies are marked by a role attribute, never by the name (O3).
  • Every reply, comment, mark, pick, test result and log line written for a person names them ("passed in Ana's run").
  • Your own words say "You" on the page; everyone else's show their name. All people share the ivory colour, Claude stays violet.
  • Old pages and inboxes still read: from="Owen" stays a person's reply, and a migration step adds the role to Claude's.

One check, isClaudes(), tells Claude's words by a role of their own, not by a name. Comments, marks, picks, test results and log lines carry the person's name; the page says "You" for your own, and "Claude (Ana)" for another person's Claude. A migration brings older pages along.

    • isClaudes(reply): either shape, a comment's data or a page's <epic-reply>

    • turnOf(), whose turn it is: "person" | "claude" | "done"

  3. Every test of "Owen" or of Claude calls it: the dozen tests listed in the decisions, PlanItem's Claude constant (coherence), and the four "last reply by Owen" lookups (the inbox commands, the comments tool twice, guide changes) through lastPersonReply().
  4. Writers stamp the person. The server stamps a comment's author as it saves it, from Phase 1's person; the CLI stamps replies, picks, test results and log lines the same way. With Q10 B, the id goes beside the name. Claude's replies get the marker, and the name of whose session wrote them.
  5. On the page. A person's reply shows "You" when it's yours, else their name, in the ivory colour; Claude's shows "Claude", or "Claude (Ana)" for another person's session. Status cards the same.
  6. Migration. A new step in Migrations adds the marker to Claude's old replies, so the fallback in step 2 can go one day.
