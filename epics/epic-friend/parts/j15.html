epic init makes the shared epics repo under the code repo's owner only when that owner is an organization (it asks GitHub).

  • Otherwise it goes under your own account: nobody can make a repo in someone else's personal account.
  • A team on a personal account shares it by adding the others as collaborators on GitHub.
the first person's epic init makes the content repo on GitHub (asks its name, <repo>-epics; --content-repo), private, pushes, and writes its address in .epic/config.json5 (contentRemote); a later person's epic init clones it; --remote <address>, --no-remote; without gh, or logged out, it says how to get it and finishes the rest
