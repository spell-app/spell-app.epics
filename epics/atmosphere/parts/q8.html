Spell's server has to run somewhere people's phones can reach, and send the sign-in codes by email. Neither is settled, and both cost money or a new dependency, so they're yours to pick.

  • Where it matters: the sign-in phase sends mail through a small seam, and the cloud line phase puts the server on a real address.
  • Until it's picked: everything is built and tested on your machine, the mail written to a file.
  • A single small server at a hosting company, with its disk backed up, and a mail service for the codes.
  • Enough for a handful of invited groups.
  • Its cost: a monthly bill, an account at each, and one new dependency for the mail.
  • No new bill, and the data stays on hardware you own.
  • Its cost: it has to stay up and be reachable from outside, and backups are yours to watch.
  • Build and test everything on your machine; going live is a later epic.
  • Its cost: Ana's phone can't reach it, so the first version stops short of the cloud design page's step 3.
