Private data can't reach anyone without being copied by hand: there's no way to publish only some of a sheet.

  • A program says, in a line or two, that the published sheet is a projection of the sign-up sheet, into the event's public space, showing each row's person and shift.
  • Only the fields named are shown: a new field stays private until someone adds it.
  • It can always be rebuilt from the private data, so a failed write is tried again, and nothing is lost.
  • Three kinds of reader: everyone in the org (a public space), an audience (its own space), one person (their inbox).

A projection: a line or two of Spell that says what view of private data to write where its readers can see it (the wording is Q16), kept up to date by Spell's server.

  1. First: your answer to Q16, the sentence's wording. The steps below fit any of its options.
  2. The language: a rule in the places rules, compiled to a projection's description: its source, its target space, its fields, its filter.
    • Its named fields are read the way a program's reads already are (P.ReadGathering, and the select it gathers), not a new field list.
  3. The server, from what's there:
    • the trigger: PlaceEvents.watch() on the source, as live pages are told of a change
    • the write: PlaceCopy, given a field list and a filter, and taught to remove a thing that leaves the view; written as the org, into the target
    • a failed write: retried as Q19 settles (its recommended option, the save queue on the server's disk; its option B needs none, since a rebuild fixes it)
  4. Rebuild: a command to rebuild a projection from scratch: PlaceCopy.copy() over the whole source.
  5. Who sees it: whoever the target space's list says; Spell never decides a read.
  6. Checked by: parser and server tests; PlaceCopy's own tests unchanged.
  7. Left out: printing, a phone pass and checking at the door (later; the guide's "The same data can go outside an ATSpace too").
