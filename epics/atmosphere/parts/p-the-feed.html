There's no feed: nothing shows what's new across an org's spaces, and nobody can change what they see.

  • Bo opens RS and sees what's new across every space he can read, newest first.
  • He edits his feed's program: more of Ana's art posts, none of her politics.
  • Opening it again fetches only what changed.
  • Quiet events are left out.

A feed that runs in the browser: it reads the spaces a person may read, keeps them on the device, fetches only what changed, and its ranking is a Spell program each person can change.

  1. Reading: each space Bo may read, with his own credentials, kept on his device by DeviceCopy, so only changes are fetched.
  2. The ranking: a Spell function over the posts, in the feed program; changing it is editing that program, in his own copy.
  3. Drawing it: Spell UI's <ui-feed>, with a long list drawn a window at a time as every list already is (drawnInWindow(), a <for>'s), not a feed view of its own.
  4. How far it goes: tried with a few hundred people's posts in a test; the guide's "communities of hundreds" checked.
  5. Checked by: headless runs and a timing.
  6. Left out: a server-side feed for millions.
  • Where to see it: the built-in Feed, @spell/examples/OrgFeed, on the examples page with its picture; Bo's feed in a browser: the epic folder's tests/org-feed.png.
  • One address for everyone's own feed: @rs/__feed is whoever's asking's, as the inbox's is; Spell's server puts it together, reading each space's posts as that person, so each space's own read check holds (J159).
  • What's in it: every post in every space of the org they can read, private ones they manage too; never an inbox, never a quiet event's spaces (J161).
  • The ranking is the program's: a weight on each post, 2 more of it, 1 as it comes, 0 none; favourites first, then the rest, newest first. Bo's own: more of Ana's art, none of her politics (J160, J162).
  • Only what changed: the server answers what changed since a version, each space asked for its own changes, a space gone from the feed removed. Not yet on the page: a page still reads the whole feed each time (J158, T51).
  • How far it goes: 300 posts read in under a second in its test; not a feed for millions.
  • Checked: 6 server tests (who sees what, read only, a second read's changes, a quiet event leaving, the 300); a browser test where Bo signs in, sees Ana's painting on top, her politics nowhere, the picnic below, and Cy's new post arrive.
