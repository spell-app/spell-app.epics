  1. One answer to "who's asking": a new sign-in check, read by every place that decides it today.
    • Today five places decide it on their own:
      • the page server's run token
      • a room's socket token
      • the prefs routes' prefsPersonFor()
      • the app's LOCAL_PERSON (about 20 uses)
      • the page's own idea of its person
    • The new check, in the kit's server beside Guard, reads the signed-in session from a cookie and puts the person on the request; all five read it there.
    • PlaceServer fills in the place's idea of who's asking (PlaceContext.identity), which it never did.
    • Guard stays for the local page server.
  2. Rooms: a room's socket goes through the same check, so its token no longer rides in its address.
  3. Mail: one small seam, "send this mail", and two mails written for it, an invite and a sign-in, each with the link and the code. Tests and your machine send to a local mail catcher, Spell's own, in the kit's server: it keeps every mail and lists them on a page, with no new dependency. The real sender, a mail service's API from your machine, is added once Q12 is answered and you've given the machine, the domain's three DNS records (written out for you to paste) and the service's key.
  4. Invites and requests: an invite makes a Spell account, ana@spell, through the people store's existing first-file step (PeopleStore.me()), its email in the person's existing emails; no new store. Signing in makes a one-time sign-in request, good for ten minutes: the asking page keeps its id, and the email carries a link to it and a six-digit code. Typing the code on the asking device, or confirming on the link's page, completes it, once; wrong tries are limited.
  5. The link, on any device: its page shows where the request came from (the browser, the system, and a rough place from its address if that needs no new dependency, else said so) and one confirm button; confirming signs that device in. The asking page checks the request by its id every few seconds, and signs in too once it's confirmed. Spell runs the sign-in server, so the link signs in there, and each device then starts its own fresh sign-in: no device needs another's keys (T4).
  6. The sign-in bar: "email me a code", a step added to the sign-in shape places already have: "check your email", with the code box, while the page waits; the atmosphere account ignores the new step. The bar names the two kinds apart: a Spell account, and an atmosphere account (a Bluesky one).
  7. Checked by: tests of the request's rules (expired, wrong code, used twice, a link confirmed on a second device signing both in, a link confirmed twice or late refused, the confirm page naming where the request came from), and that all five readers see the signed-in person; a headless sign-in on the recipe box, reading the mail from the local catcher: once by code, once by the link in a second browser.
  8. Left out: passkeys, Google or GitHub, an atmosphere account as a sign-in (a later epic); real mail, until Q12 and your three things; the public address (P8).
