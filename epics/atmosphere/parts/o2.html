The proposal: is saved in the cloud is a data folder, plainly said, on a server that speaks Spell's own way of saving.

This is your answer on the cloud design page, with one change: the folder sits behind a seam, so Spaces can be added without touching a program. 

family cookbook is saved in the cloud
family cookbook is a data folder at "ana@spell/family-cookbook"

For Ana, who has never heard of a server:

  1. She writes the line, and adds a recipe. It stays in her browser, and the page says so.
  2. She signs in with an email link, and has ana@spell.
  3. Her recipe goes up, as a plain file in a folder.
  4. She opens it on her phone, signs in, and it's there.
  5. She hits a limit: the save is refused, says why, and nothing's lost.
  6. She leaves: she downloads a folder of plain JSON, and one changed line runs her program on it.

What keeps this from becoming the service you don't want to own:

  • It's the same server anyone can run, so "a person's own server" comes with it.
  • It starts invite only, and export is in the first version.
  • A person is Spell's own id, with an email or an atmosphere account attached. A folder's address is Spell's own too.
  • Plain files can move to a Space later, or to anywhere else.

The cost, said plainly: until a person's own server or Spaces is real, Spell's server holds invited people's data, which is the thing you'd rather not do. Q1 is that decision.
