An org knows only guests, members and admins: there's no Board, no teams, no projects, and no Managers.

  • RS can say: the Board is Org Manager of everything, the Admins of everything but the org's private space, a Team Manager of their team and its projects, a Project Manager of one project.
  • Every role can end: a term with an end date, after which the person holds it no more.
  • A project names "the Events Team", not the people on it today, so a change to the team reaches every project at once.
  • An org can rename roles, add its own, and say who's eligible for one; Spell refuses anyone else.
  • Project Managers can't remove the Board, Admins or Team Managers above them.

An org's roles move into its Admin space: the Board, the Admins, standing teams and projects, each with its Managers, all temporary, always looked up and never copied.

  1. The shape: an org has teams and projects (a project under a team or the org, never under another project); a role is a person or a team holding Manager of something, from a date, until a date. Written as Spell types in People.spell, the people library, which this epic changes here (a change it causes).
  2. One role table: the table every role check reads, ROLE_FLAGS, holds a space's three roles, viewer, editor and manager (Q21 D), each with its rights; P23 adds a right per kind of thing and the right to make something new, P26 the right to publish. The org's roles (the Board, the Admins, Team Managers, Project Managers) say who someone is, and a Manager of something is its spaces' manager; everyone in a space, or in the org, is a member of it. Folded into it:
    • the list of roles, Role, split in two: SpaceRole (viewer, editor, manager), made from the table, and the org's Role (Board, Admins, Team Manager, Project Manager) beside it
    • ProjectDrafts.mayPublish()'s stand-in, read from it (P26 finishes it)
    • SpaceMembers.keeps(), a right in the table rather than a check of its own
    The words, as Q20 A settled them: a space's role "admin" is now "manager", as its flag (canManage) says; "admin" means only the org's technical Admins.
  3. Where it lives: the org's Admin space, a private space folder of its own (@rs/__admin, Q25 A: one of Spell's own names, which no project can take), with the rules for roles and a log of every change.
    • The log is in the takedown log's shape (who, why, when; like __takedowns.json5), so P21's recoveries and P25's page read the same entries.
  4. One look-up: "who manages this space, today?" asked of the Admin space, every time, as PeopleStore's role look-up is now. Nothing else keeps a list of people.
  5. The old roles: membership files saying guest, member or admin are still read, as viewer, editor (still allowed to make things, as members are today) and manager; orgs with no Admin space (the family cookbook) use the three space roles alone. A space kept in a real Space gets read for a viewer, read and write for an editor or manager; managing stays Spell's, done as the owner's account (Q6 C).
  6. Rules: who may name a Manager, eligibility, and who can't be removed, checked when a role changes.
  7. Checked by: unit tests over a small RS; today's role tests unchanged.
  8. Left out: the spaces themselves (P22 · Paired Spaces), what each role may write (P23 · Writes By Role), a page for it (P25 · Admin Page).
  • Who leads what, in the Admin space: each org may have an Admin space, @rs/__admin (Q25 A), holding its roster: its roles, standing teams, projects (each under a team or the org, never another project), and terms. A term is a person, or a whole team, holding a role from a day until a day.
    • The roster is the people library's own Roster; every change goes in a log beside it, who, when and why, as the takedown log does.
    • Always looked up: a project names "the Events Team", whose people are its Team Leads today, so a change to the team reaches every project at once.
  • One look-up: OrgRoles answers who leads a space today: the Board every one, the Admins the Admin space and the rest (J83), a team's Leads its spaces and its projects', a project's Leads its. Each Lead is that space's manager.
  • Its rules, as a role changes:
    • whoever leads what a role leads, or above it, names or ends a term; only the Board names the Board (J81). So a Project Lead can't remove a Team Lead, an Admin or the Board.
    • an org may rename a role, add its own, and say who's eligible; Spell refuses anyone else, and anyone outside the org
    • an ended term keeps its record, its end day set
  • One table of space roles: viewer, editor, manager, everyone on the list a member (Q21 D); "manager" for a space's role, "Admin" only the org's technical Admins (Q20 A). SPACE_ROLES, in spaces' models (J79).
    • Older files' guest, member and admin still read, as viewer, editor and manager; what's written now is the new word (J82).
    • An editor writes as a member did, until Writes By Role (J78).
    • Who may change anyone's rows is a right in the table now; Publish's stand-in reads it too.
  • The words list says member, manager and Admin.
