Signing in to Spell takes an emailed code; someone with a Bluesky account can't use it to sign in.

  • Ana links her Bluesky account to ana@spell once; after that "Sign in with Bluesky" signs her in on any device.
  • The rest of Spell can't tell which way she signed in: one sign-in check for pages, places and rooms.
  • Invite only still holds: a Bluesky account with no Spell account linked is told to ask for an invite.
  • Spell never writes to her Bluesky account: it's how she signs in and nothing more.

A person's own Bluesky (AT Protocol) account becomes a second way to sign in to their Spell account, checked by Spell's server, the same one check as the emailed code.

  1. Chosen: Q26 A, AT Protocol's server sign-in library, @atproto/oauth-client-node, added at the kit's root: the same makers and version line as the page's library.
  2. Who does the sign-in: Spell's server, not the page, since the server must trust it. Today's AtProtoSignIn is the page's own, for a program writing to your account; the server gets the same library's server version.
  3. What it asks for: only who you are (the narrowest sign-in AT Protocol offers), never the right to write.
  4. Linking: signed in by email, Ana's account page offers "Link a Bluesky account"; her account's file keeps the permanent id (DID) of that account, and the handle only for show.
  5. Signing in: a "Sign in with Bluesky" button beside the email field; coming back, the server finds the account by DID and starts the same session the emailed code does (SignIns).
  6. Refused: an unlinked account, a hidden (taken-down) account, in English and Spanish.
  7. Needs you: nothing on this machine (a local address needs no client file). On a public address, the client file must be served there: that waits on the cloud's real address (M7, M8).
  8. Checked by: tests against the fake network; a manual test for you with spell-test.bsky.social.
  9. Left out: making a Spell account from a Bluesky sign-in with no invite; passkeys, Google and GitHub (a later epic).
