From Owen's comment on the Spaces guide: all of an org's records are written by the org's own account, and only Spell holds the right to do that, so it can enforce any rule.

A person's space works the same way, as Owen added: "even in my 'personal space,' I might invite my partner to collaborate on a document".

  • What it removes: needing an account, shared rows being a problem, and handing strangers' programs your grant.
  • What it costs: Spell's server becomes required, and holds the right to write for every org on it. What a person wrote stays with the org.
  • The proposals agree: in AT Protocol Spaces, the account that owns a space can always read and write it, whatever its access rules say. So writing as the org's own account is exactly what Spaces allows.
  • One thing to add: the protocol says every such record was written by the org. So each record needs a "written by" field of Spell's own, naming the person.

Everything in that list works from the first version, and the server is the same code anyone can run. Its cost: a service that must stay up, and a "written by" field on every record.
