How the editor shows what a line means

Spell reads like English, so one word can do several jobs: is can compare, ask, set or declare. This page proposes giving bold, underline and italic ONE job each, so you can see how a line compiles at a glance, and scan a dense block for what matters.

Only the last one is easy to tell apart today. One line can hold two of them:

  • Bold: a name the first time it's mentioned (a loop's variable, a local, an action's parameter), and the name a head declares.
  • Semibold: every later use of a name, properties, kinds, and a list's values (ace). The two weights are close: hard to tell apart.
  • Dotted underline: a phrase your project declares (is a diamond), its words only, not what it's asked of; and a number with its unit (300 msec).
  • Italic: // notes only.

Bold is for names, so you can scan a block for them. Which names, and in how many weights?

Every name a reader scans for, yours or Spell's, whole with its 's: pile's, suit, droppable; never the, its, a. One bold, no semibold.

  • Names: things, kinds, properties, variables, an action's parameters, and the word a head declares (droppable, draggable, layout).
  • "First mention bold, later uses semibold" goes: the two weights were too close to read, and Q4 A says "being set" more plainly.

Keep first mention bold and later uses semibold; add droppable and the 's.
