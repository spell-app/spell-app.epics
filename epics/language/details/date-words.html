Date and time words

Every date and time word Spell has today, every one it could add, and a pick for each open call. Mockup's Weekend Countdown can't be written until these are settled.

agent language-datewords, for the session of epic language, while working the date words todo (T159) 2026-11-17
language -- the words Spell understands, in English and in Spanish (Encanto). Epic mockup asked for a "Weekend starts in DAYS : HR : MIN : SEC" example (request r26). Spell has no minute, second or day of the week for a date, and nothing that runs every second. You asked for a table of every date and time word, with options, following Moment.js and Temporal. The parts (year, short year, month as a name, short month, month number, day, day of the week, hour, minute, second, timestamp); the day of the week as a name plus a 1-7 number from each person's "first day of the week" setting; names in the app's language; the every block. The words for the hour, leading zeros, the week, AM / PM, the time zone, "2 days ago", rounding, and which extra date words to add. Nothing is built yet.
  • One table, a row per word, grouped. "Has today" rows work now; "new" rows are proposals.
  • Example values are for Tuesday, November 17, 2026, 9:05:07 PM, in New York.
  • Moment.js is the old, well-known date library. Temporal is the new date API built into JavaScript, and Intl is the browser's own wording of dates in each language.
  • Bold rows in the last column are clashes: a new word that already means something else, in English or Spanish.

  1. The day-of-the-week number changes from person to person. Mockup's draft tests the weekend with > 5. For someone whose week starts on Sunday, Friday is 6, so it would say "It's the weekend!" all day Friday. The countdown below uses words that don't depend on numbers instead (Q8, Q10).
  2. the second of already means "the 2nd item of" a list. Spanish el segundo de is the same. (Q9)
  3. long means "in full" (long date, long time). Using it for "with a leading zero" gives it a second meaning. (Q2)
  4. The hour is a number today, and code does math on it. Turning it into 9 for 9 PM breaks 23 - the hour of now. (Q1)
  5. The app's language doesn't decide the clock. Spain uses 24-hour time and Mexico 12-hour, both in Spanish; Britain uses 24-hour, in English. So "12- or 24-hour by language" needs the region too. (Q1)
  6. Today's formats are US English even in a Spanish app. The short date is always 11/17/26, and the time always ends in "PM". Your "names follow the app's language" changes what existing apps show.
  7. Spanish la hora is both "the time" (text) and "the hour of" (a number). mañana is both "tomorrow" and "morning". el número de already counts things.
  8. The month is a name in Spell, but a number in Moment and Temporal. Moment even counts January as 0.

At 9 PM, should the hour of now be 21 or 9? Today it's 21, and examples do math on it: Weather checks if the hour of its time is 0, and the countdown draft does 23 - the hour of its time.

Whichever you pick, the setting needs a name. Suggested: "12-hour clock" and "24-hour clock" (Spanish "reloj de 12 horas", "reloj de 24 horas"), as a switch "Use a 24-hour clock", the way phones say it. It starts from the person's region, not just the language.

the hour of now is 21 for everyone, so math never breaks. Times shown as text follow the person's clock.

  • the short time of now: "9:05 PM" or "21:05", by the setting.
  • its time as a 24-hour time: "21:05" always; as a 12-hour time, "9:05 PM".
  • The 1-12 number, if someone needs it alone: the clock hour of now, 9 (new word).
  • Same as Temporal (.hour is 0-23) and Moment's hour().
  • Cost: "the hour follows the language" holds for what people see, not for the number.

the hour of now is 9 on a 12-hour clock, 21 on a 24-hour one. Math needs the 24-hour hour of now.

  • Cost: 23 - the hour of now gives 14 at 9 PM for 12-hour people.
  • Cost: Weather's is 0 never matches (midnight is 12), so its "Day" column goes blank.
  • Cost: the same app gives different numbers to different people.
  • Like Moment's h and H.

The number is 21, but drawn on the page it shows "9". Math is safe; what you see isn't what you get.

  • {the hour of now} draws "9"; {the hour of now + 1} draws "22".
  • Cost: a number that shows differently from its value. Nothing else in Spell does that.

Numbers have no leading zeros: the minute of 9:05 is 5. A clock or a countdown wants "05". How does a beginner ask for it? Mockup's draft wrote its own two digits action for this.

the minute of now as two digits gives "05". Works on any number, so the countdown's days and seconds use it too.

  • Spanish: como dos dígitos.
  • The result is text.
  • Same shape as as upper case and as a short date.
  • Like Moment's mm, Intl's "2-digit", JavaScript's padStart.
  • Cost: one new as form; "three digits" would be asked for next.

the long minute of now gives "05"; the same for long hour, long second, long day. Your idea.

  • Cost: long date and long time mean "in full". Is long month "November" or "11"?
  • Cost: only works on date parts, not on a countdown's numbers.
  • Spanish: el minuto largo, which reads oddly ("the long minute").

the minute of now with a leading zero gives "05". Says exactly what happens.

  • Spanish: con un cero delante.
  • Cost: wordy, and "leading zero" is a programmer's phrase.

People write a small action, as mockup's draft does.

Cost: every clock or countdown writes the same three lines.

the week of now gives the week of the year: 47 for November 17, 2026. Countries count weeks differently, and the answers differ near New Year. January 1, 2027 is in week 53 the European way and week 1 the US way.

Weeks start Monday; week 1 is the one with the year's first Thursday. Everyone gets the same number.

  • What Europe, Latin America's business calendars and most work tools use.
  • Temporal's .weekOfYear and Moment's isoWeek().
  • Cost: near New Year, a US person may see a different number than their wall calendar.

Follows the person's first day of the week and their region's rule. Matches their calendar.

  • Moment's week(); the browser's Intl.Locale week info.
  • Cost: two people sharing a list "for week 53" see different weeks.
  • Cost: the browser's week info is newer than the rest of Intl.

No week number until an example needs one. The countdown doesn't.

How does an app show where the person is, by time? There are three ways to name a zone: its id (America/New_York), its city (New York), and its abbreviation (EST). A date in JavaScript has no zone, so these are the PERSON's zone, not the date's.

the time zone is "America/New_York", the short time zone is "EST", the long time zone is "Eastern Standard Time", the time zone city is "New York".

  • Same "short / long" pattern as the dates.
  • Spanish: la zona horaria, la zona horaria corta, la zona horaria larga ("hora estándar del este"), la ciudad de la zona horaria.
  • Cost: the short one is often "GMT+1" (Madrid, in English) or "GMT-5" (New York, in Spanish), not letters.
  • Cost: the city stays in English; the browser doesn't translate it.

the time zone name is "New York", the time zone abbreviation is "EST". The id isn't offered.

  • Spanish: el nombre de la zona horaria, la abreviatura de la zona horaria.
  • Cost: "name" is vague (city? "Eastern Time"?); and a program saving the zone needs the id.

the time zone of its time: each date keeps its zone, as Weather's data has one per city.

  • Matches Temporal's zoned dates.
  • Cost: Spell's dates would have to change underneath to carry a zone; a big change.
