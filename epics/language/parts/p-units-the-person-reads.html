  1. The reader's system (Q27 A). The core reads the browser's region (Intl.Locale): US, Liberia and Myanmar read US units, the rest metric. The browser names the region but not the measurement system, so that list is ours, written down once.
  2. A preference. Units: metric · US, kept where the colour scheme preference already is (the persist folder's preference store), and read by the core; it wins over the region.
  3. Showing. By default every measure shown on a page or put in text converts to the reader's units, from their region or their preference: lengths in feet and inches, weights in pounds, temperatures in °F, volumes in cups for a US reader; the metric unit nearest in size for everyone else (172 cm, not 1.72 m).
  4. <ui-dimension>. A Spell UI element that shows one measure: <ui-dimension value={its height}> in the reader's units, and in="feet and inches" to choose them, as its height in feet and inches does in code. Its dimension comes from the value's type, or an attribute when it's a bare number (<ui-dimension length="172" unit="cm">). Built in the ui folder with its vocabulary in both languages, and on the components page.
  5. Python. The same list against the system locale, and an override setting.
  6. Star Wars. its "height" is a length in cm, its "mass" is a weight in kg, its "diameter" is a length in km; its measure action goes, except for population, which isn't a measure. This epic caused the change, so it's done here, and 🚧 data is told.
  7. Checks. A test per region and the preference overriding both; <ui-dimension>'s browser tests; Star Wars looked at in the editor, light and dark, US and metric; content compare shows only Star Wars.
  8. Left out. Per-dimension preferences (metric lengths but °F); rounding settings.
