  • Your second form compiles to (isEmpty && card.rank === "king") || (this.color !== card.color && this.value === card.value + 1).
  • Today a one-line body already works (it is empty and the card is a king), and so do parentheses on one line; neither rule does.
  • The two rules:
    1. a line of just and or or joins the lines either side, each in parentheses
    2. a line ending in and or or goes on onto the next, deeper line, which the editor indents by itself

How it would be built, and the risks:

  • Rule 2 is the tokenizer's: it joins the lines into one before any rule sees them, so every rule and the incremental parse work as they do.
  • Rule 1 is a body's: a yes-or-no body of several lines, split by the lone lines. Lines with no and / or between them are an error saying which to write.
  • The editor: Enter after a trailing and / or indents the next line (Monaco and VS Code), and the bubbly look draws the joined lines as one.
  • Spanish: y and o.
  • Both rules work wherever a yes-or-no is read: if, while, a when rule, return, a question's body.
  • A continued line is any deeper indent; the editor puts it under the first word after the line's own indent.
  • Drop and drag rules (droppable when), and a question's body; not an if line.
  • and its value is ... on the next line continues the line above it, too.
