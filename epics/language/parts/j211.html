Word first (otherwise black), value_otherwise competes at else's priority instead of its own lower one:

  • a lone value else can't read as a statement (otherwise the value of its last card, - otherwise 0): else reads only its word, so the value line, being longer, wins
  • a statement (otherwise print 2, otherwise bar = 2): both read the whole line, and the tie goes to the earlier rule, else
  • outside a getter or method, the value line is refused at its own lower priority, so else wins as before

Rejected: making the ladder's body know it's a ladder (the line above is known only once the block has parsed), and a check in else that its inline statement isn't a lone value (the same answer, in two rules).
