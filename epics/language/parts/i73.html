In plain Spell, a <for film in its films> written on a line of its own at a drawing\x27s top (not inside an element) parses as the in-element loop, and the javascript writer throws no ASTJSXExpression() method instead of saying what to write. Plain Spell writes that loop as for film in its films lines; the tag form read the tag line. Found when the tag tests moved to plain: ForTag.test.ts and placeReadsInDrawings.test.ts now wrap their loops in an element. A <for> is the loop itself wherever it stands, and in braces only as an element's child.

  • Cause: an in-element <for> always made its loop a child value in braces, {core.drawEach(...)}. On a drawing's own line (or after return), the drawing returned those braces as they were, and no writer writes braces outside an element. With a sibling on the next line it worked, since the two lines read as one fragment, and the loop was its child.
  • Fix: the <for> rule (SpellJSX) gives the loop itself: core.drawEach(...), or core.drawRead(...) with its riders, the same as the plain for film in its films lines. An element's children put it in braces (SpellJSX.childAST(), also what <example>'s children use), as they already did for <film/>.
  • So a place read in a <for> on its own line holds the whole drawing again (static drawsWaiting), as the plain loop does.
  • Inside an element, a place read in a <for> shows its own "…" where the loop is (a child value that waits), and the rest of the element draws at once: kept as it is, a test says so.
  • Tests: ForTag.test.ts (alone, with riders, returned, in an if's branch, after a line that draws nothing) and placeReadsInDrawings.test.ts.
