  • choose a card from the cards with prompt "Which card?" asks the person to pick one card, in a modal. Afterwards it is the card picked, and the result is ok or cancel, as prompt answers since P64 · Modal Answers.
  • What it chooses from, your words (2026-11-18): "a string type "card suits" should work, as should colors ... List of strings should obviously work."
    • A kind's own values, with no from: choose a card suit (Spanish elegir un palo de carta) shows clubs, diamonds, hearts and spades. it is the suit picked. choose a card suit from ... still picks from a shorter list of suits.
    • Colors: choose a color (Spanish elegir un color) shows Spell UI's 13 colors, red to black, each as a color chip beside its name. it is the color's name, like red, which any color setting takes.
    • A list of text works like any list: choose one of the levels, where the levels is a list of words. Written in place, it's Spell's bracketed list, choose one of ["easy", "medium", "hard"].
  • What each row shows, your words (2026-11-18): "for list of things, show thing or name.  for list of views, show the views (?).  For JSON object with { [label|name|title]: <value>}` show label and choose value."
    • Drawn things show their title, label or name as text, not their drawing, like any thing.
    • A record: its keys are the rows, and it is the value under the key picked. A list of records: each row shows its title, label or name, and it is the record picked.
  • Picking several, your words: "yes, plural form or many or up to 2 or between 2 and 4". Then it is a list. All four are built: choose cards, choose many cards, choose up to 2 cards, choose between 2 and 4 cards. choose between "Easy" and "Hard" isn't: picking from named choices is a record's job.
  • How it looks, your words: "either, up to 20, have an attribute to specify max shown".

  • An empty list, your answer: no modal at all. the result is cancel, and it is nothing.

  1. First, your OK on each new phrase, as you asked for any new Spell. Nothing is built before it. The phrases, with the clash each has:
    • choose a card from the cards, Spanish elegir una carta de las cartas. It's worded like a random card from the cards, which Spell already has; no clash.
    • ... with prompt "Which card?", Spanish ... con el mensaje "¿Qué carta?". With no prompt, the modal asks "Choose a card". Clash: prompt is also a statement of its own, but here it only follows with, so it reads apart.
    • choose one of the cards, Spanish elegir una de las cartas, from the old sketch: for a list whose items have no one-word name, like a list of words. Written in place, it's Spell's bracketed list: choose one of ["easy", "medium", "hard"]. A bare "easy", "medium" and "hard" isn't a list in Spell today (that list takes only names and numbers), so it isn't proposed.
    • choose a card suit, Spanish elegir un palo de carta: a kind's own values, no from, worded like a random card suit, which Spell already has. choose a card suit from the suits left picks from a shorter list. Clash: choose a card suit against choose a card from the cards. card suit is one name once Solitaire declares it, and the longest name wins, so it's the suit kind, not a card. A thing type with no values of its own, choose a card alone, is refused with "say which cards: choose a card from the cards".
    • choose a color, Spanish elegir un color: Spell UI's 13 colors, the ones notify ... in red already takes (red, orange, yellow, olive, green, teal, blue, violet, purple, pink, brown, grey, black; Spanish rojo, naranja ...). Clash: a program's own kind wins. Solitaire's card says its "color" is one of red, black, so choose a card color offers red and black only; choose a color means Spell UI's colors unless the program declares a color of its own.
    • Several: the plural, many, up to 2, between 2 and 4; the wordings and their clashes are in Q74. All four are built; choose between 2 and 4 cards always means a count, since no choose between "Easy" and "Hard" is built.
    • The height: showing 10 at a time, Spanish mostrando 10 a la vez: how many rows show at once, before the list scrolls.
  2. The rule. One new Choose rule beside prompt's, replacing the old sketch in a comment there.
    • Like prompt, it makes a NEW it: a thing, or a list when several are picked.
    • It compiles to const it = await core.choose(...).
    • It is written to Python the same way.
  3. The core. core.choose() beside core.prompt().
    • Each row's words: its title, else its label, else its name, else itself as text: the menu's way of naming a list's items, shared rather than copied.
    • A record: its keys are the rows, and the value under the key picked is given back. A list of records names each by its title, label or name, and gives back the record.
    • Drawn things are named the same way, as text; the modal draws no view.
    • A kind with no from: its own list, as a random card suit reads it (Card.Suits). choose a color: the list notify's colors already use, shared, not copied; each row marked as a color, so the modal draws its chip.
    • An empty list: no modal; the result is cancel and it is nothing.
    • No Spell UI on the page, as in a test: it prints the question and picks the first row.
    • Python's core: choose asks its page, as prompt does.
  4. The modal. A choose dialog in Spell UI's modal family, beside prompt's.
    • Up to 20 rows: buttons. More: a list, reusing Spell UI's list, that scrolls, with a search box. showing N at a time sets how many rows show.
    • Several: each row checks on and off; ok stays off until the count fits.
    • Colors: each row is a button with a <ui-color-chip> (the theme editor's small rounded square) of that color, then its name. The chip stays read-only; the button around it is what's picked.
    • Escape and the close button cancel, as every dialog does.
