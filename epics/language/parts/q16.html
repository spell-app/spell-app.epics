Both of these are good English, and the 's belongs to different things in them:

  • the name of the character's friend: the name of the character's friend
  • the first card of the deck's suit: the suit of the first card of the deck

English doesn't decide by grammar (not subject or object): the 's ends a whole phrase or just the nearest name, and people pick the one that makes sense.

`the character's name` is `the name of the character`, in English (the curly ’ too): long names, built-in members, `the deck's first card`, chains, `its friend's name`, `set the character's name to ...`; after an `of` the types say whose `'s` it is, both possible: the nearest, warning; `it's name` is an error; a translation writes `el nombre del personaje`. One letter of text is a `letter` (`letra`, `char`), so a program may name its own type `character`: Star Wars says `a character`, and the importer names a `characters` collection so.
