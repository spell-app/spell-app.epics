For P67 · Drag Events. You wrote: "dragged-over could have a limit, e.g. only once every 100ms or something ... Spell should be a good citizen as far as the rest of the browser ecosystem is concerned."

What it limits:

  • A program's when a card is dragged over it: already runs once as the card comes over, not on every move. The limit keeps it that way for files from the desktop, whose browser events flicker in and out as the pointer crosses things inside the drop area.
  • The page's ui-dragged-over and ui-file-dragged-over, which repeat while the pointer moves.

Either way the first one comes at once, and the last place the pointer stopped is always sent.
  • Your number. Fast enough for a hint or a sound; a marker drawn from it moves a little choppily.
  • A program running in Python hears each one as a message across to it: 10 a second is easy, 120 a second isn't.
  • Built on Spell's existing "do this once, a little later" helper, the same one that redraws a fitted layout.
  • Tied to the browser's drawing: 60 a second on most screens, 120 on fast ones, none in a hidden tab.
  • Smooth for code that draws under the pointer, but 6 to 12 times as many, every one crossing to Python.
  • Say it, e.g. 50ms.
  • Dragged-over events and handlers come at most once every 100ms per list, the first at once, the last always sent
  • A desktop file leaving and coming back within 100ms counts as never having left
