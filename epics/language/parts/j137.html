Python writes the app's Undo and Redo state (can_undo, can_redo, undo_title, redo_title) on the app in every state and patch, under $history (a key of our own, like $id); the app is put in the next patch whenever the history changes. The page's startPage() reads it into core.remote.history, which the page's App getters read, live. The other way was a separate frame or a top-level entry beside the things, which every reader of a patch would have had to step around. JavaScript's own StateWriter doesn't write it: a JavaScript program's page has its history itself.
