Default waiting looks the same on screen as today, and it needs nothing new from Solid. Solitaire ran in headless Chromium, through the real <spell-viewer> and its built runtime, from hand-patched compiled output with the awaits Python's output has. Nothing shipped.

  • What a deal draws: every step, as today. A deal changed the board 30 times today, and 30 or 31 times with waiting, each ending about 1.85 seconds in with the same board. A stock flip and a card move drew their steps too (pictures: deal 1, 3, 4, flip, move).
  • A step with no real pause doesn't paint: so Solitaire keeps its pauses.
    • With await 0, or a call to an async action, the whole deal was one jump: 3 board changes.
    • A real 0 ms timer drew about half its steps: 15 board changes.
  • Waiting at the top level hides the deal: the first board came at 1856 ms instead of about 110 ms, and no deal was seen (picture at 0.9 s). So P10's top level must not wait.
  • Clicks during an action: the queue is what keeps the game right.
    • Waiting with no queue, two restarts mid-deal: 78 cards in the piles, not 52, and TypeError: ... reading 'turnFaceDown'.
    • With Q6's queue: 52 cards, the repeat restarts ignored, and clicks on the stock mid-deal queued, the latest kept and played once the deal ended.
    • A bug today: three stock clicks mid-deal leave the second column with 1 card, not 2. The queue fixes it.
  • A slow getter in a draw: the frozen deal is real, and only on a later change. On a second deal, with the value already answered:
    • createMemo froze the drawn counter at 28, then jumped to 56.
    • createDeferred kept the counter moving, 29, 30 ... 56, with the old slow value beside it.
    • A getter that lands between steps (20 ms) doesn't freeze either way. So core.waitedValue() must be createDeferred.
    • A value that changes faster than it answers lands only when the changes stop.
  • Where the "…" goes: each custom element is its own boundary.
    • With no <Loading> in a card's element, only that card waits. On the app's own draw, the whole board stayed blank for 1585 ms.
    • A <Loading> around a pile, outside its element, does not cover what's inside: Solid's warning still fired. So it belongs inside drawInBoundary(), as P12 plans.
    • A draw that reads a slow value itself, on the app, hides the whole board under one "…" until the first answer: 1587 ms here. Reading it in a small part keeps the deal in view, so P12 warns on a whole-app hold.
  • Reading a slow getter from an action: solved. Bring the writes in, read once, then wait for the latest promise the value started: core.flush(), a read, await that promise.
    • After a write it gave 50, where resolve() gives the old 30. After two writes, 70.
    • With no write, no extra run. A failure reaches the action.
    • The read matters for a lazy value nobody draws: without it, the old 30.
  • Reads after a wait: not followed (it stayed 4 when the bonus changed). A read before the first wait is (103). So P12 moves simple reads up where it safely can, and warns on the rest.
  • A remembered getter over a slow one: derive() stayed at 31 where 41 was right, so P12 leaves it off these.
  • How long each value lives: about 1.9 KB each, and 500 made in 6 ms. Unless lazy, each runs its slow work again on a change even with nobody reading it: 500 more runs. lazy: true stopped that, and a drawn card still redrew. So P12 uses lazy. Disposal stopped the runs, but this test held on to the values, so it didn't show the memory coming back.
