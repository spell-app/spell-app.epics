Ana has never heard of a server. This is a proposal, step by step, with each step's wording still to come.

  1. She writes one line. family cookbook is saved in the cloud, with no address and no key.
  2. She adds a recipe, not signed in. It's kept in her browser, and the page says so: "Saved on this device: sign in to keep it everywhere."
  3. She signs in. An email with a link, and she has a space, ana@spell.
  4. Her recipe goes up. The change that was waiting is sent, to a file on Spell's server: ana@spell/__data/family-cookbook/recipes/r-1.json.
  5. She opens it on her phone. She signs in, and there it is.
  6. She shares it with Bo. Bo can read it and, if she says so, write, with everyone's changes in one order; there's no word for sharing yet.
  7. She hits a limit. A save is refused, saying what the limit is, and nothing's lost.
  8. She leaves. She downloads a folder of plain JSON, and one changed line runs her program on it.

What's already built carries the data. What's missing is everything about people.

What these don't count:

  • Sending sign-in email, and backups: I didn't price them.
  • Someone on call when it's down, and support when a person loses data.
  • The law on holding people's data, and what to do with data that's abusive.
  • Keeping it running for as long as the data lasts, which is the part you can't undo.

Each step below works on its own, and you can stop after any of them. How far should the first release go?

  1. On your machine: a data folder in a space (the chunking review). Not hosted.
  2. One hosted server, invite only: sign-in, and each person's own data.
  3. Backups, export, delete and limits.
  4. Sharing a folder with named people.
  5. Live rooms on it, with the referee behind the sign-in.
  6. Open to everyone, with abuse handling.

The one-line first program works for a few invited people, and their data is safe. Its cost: no sharing yet.
