Spell has no one idea of a back-end: preferences and space files each have their own, and neither can query.

  • A new interface, apart from preferences: a place throws when it fails, which preferences never do. Preferences code doesn't change.
  • A place says what it can do, including writing that depends on the field or on who owns the thing.
  • Two parts showing one thing get one row, so a change appears in both.
  • A result says whether it's still waiting, complete, or from an earlier visit.
  • TypeScript only: nothing here needs epic language.
  • Try it: nothing to see yet. The tests are the proof, and the next phases are built on them.
  • A new interface, a place: say what you can do, and list a collection.
  • One cache of rows by type and id, and results that say what they know.
  1. Write the types first: Place, its capabilities and its results, as the Overview's Places section has them, with writing asked per thing and per field. A saved thing's identity is called its id, so the capability is idOf (the question on key and id).
  2. Add PlaceCache: rows by type and id, handing out one row per id.
  3. Add Places: places by name, and swapping the one behind a name.
  4. Add MemoryPlace, the place every later test runs against, and an adapter over PrefPlace, which is also "this device".
  5. Settle one id format (T2): saving.ts's Recipe#r-12 for anything saved. The mirror's counted $id stays what the Python page speaks, and browser-made ids must not hold an @, as saving already says.
  6. Add the folder's own entry to the kit's exports, not flattened into the util barrel, so no page pays for it until it uses it.
  7. A result is a Loadable, since one read is one resource: its contents, loaded, failed and loading state come from there, and only "still waiting, complete, or from an earlier visit" is new, so there's no third kind of load state. It gains an abort signal beside its old promise.cancel(), whose users don't change. If it doesn't fit, the phase says why, as a judgement call.
  8. Checked by: tests for each capability, one row per id, and the adapter reading back what preferences hold.
  9. Left out: fetching, sending, anything about Things or Spell words.
