why do we need "(a recipe)"?

You picked B, the one-line form, and asked: "why do we need "(a recipe)"?"

It says which thing the operation acts on and sends. Today's Spell needs the parentheses to tell that from the action's name, but this one line doesn't have to.

  • What the parentheses do today: in to clear (a calculator):, the calculator is what's handed in. In to deal a card:, "a card" is only part of the action's name, and nothing is handed in.
  • What it gives an operation: publish the recipe sends that recipe to the address, and works from any type. With no thing named, there's nothing to send.
  • Why it can go here: on a line under a place, the compiler knows recipe is a type, because it already reads the program's types at compile. So a recipe can only mean the thing sent. 
family cookbook is a web service at "https://api.example.com/v1":
	## B:  as you picked, with the parentheses
	- to publish (a recipe) is at "/recipes/publish"
	## C:  no parentheses, a type's name is enough
	- to publish a recipe is at "/recipes/publish"
	## Sending nothing, in both
	- to refresh is at "/recipes/refresh"

## Called the same way, whichever you pick
publish the recipe

The catch with C: a line under a place would read differently from an action anywhere else, so someone copying the line elsewhere gets an action named "publish a recipe" that takes nothing. A warning there can catch that.

Reads as a sentence. Only a line under a place learns this, so no existing program changes.
