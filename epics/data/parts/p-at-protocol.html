One person's data in their own account doesn't fit one address, one referee, or a place that can write everything.

  • AT Protocol is one of the seven back-ends.
  • No referee: your own records, with everyone's put together when read. Changing someone else's is refused.
  • A person signs in from the browser with no server of ours, and saves with a version check, so their own devices don't overwrite each other.
  • Other people's changes arrive from the public change stream.
  • Try it: sign in with your own account in the demo, add a recipe, and see it listed on your account's host. It's public, as everything there is today.
  • Reading one account, or everyone's put together.
  • Signing in from the browser, and writing only your own records.

Its sign-in needs PKCE, PAR and DPoP together, which is easy to get subtly wrong. It's a new runtime dependency, only for programs that use this place. The name and fit get checked first.

No dependency, but a security-sensitive protocol to keep up with.

  1. AtProtoPlace: one account's collection by pages of 100, filtered in the browser. A shared place is a list of accounts, each on its own host, combined.
  2. Writing is asked per thing: only the owner. Saving uses the version check; when it's refused, it heals as any refusal does.
  3. The record key is chosen by the browser, using the same ids as everywhere else.
  4. Sign-in from the browser, by the phase's choice, with a session of at most two weeks and a clear prompt when it ends.
  5. Live updates through the public change stream, checked from a browser first, since the survey couldn't confirm that.
  6. Checked by: tests against a stub for combining, refusal and paging; a read of a real public account.
  7. Left out: private data, since Spaces isn't final; renaming a field, which its schemas can't do; App Views and search; how the people here reads on it.
