Two people changing one field can overwrite each other with no warning, and polling is the only live option from a browser. The limit of 5 requests a second per base is shared by everyone using that base.

Strains: the healing model and polling. A busy app needs the small server side to cache and to pace requests.

Said honestly in P17 · Airtable. The docs read that day: the update page has no version, ETag or conflict field, no page states what two simultaneous writes do, and its plans also cap calls a month (the free plan 1,000).

  • What two people see: different fields both stand. One field: the last save wins it, with no notice, and the other person's value changes at their next read. Long text is lost that way too. Edits in Airtable's own window are the same, and arrive at the next read. The program's line says all this at compile time.
  • The shared limit: the server side keeps to 4 a second per base, under the 5, and tabs asking the same read in the same window are one request.
  • Behind a referee: it works, tested on the stub: a Referee with an AirtablePlace as its store orders saves, says who lost, and hears Airtable's window as steps. It shouldn't be the default: every step waits for Airtable's write, at most about 4 a second for the whole base, so a busy room would crawl, and it does nothing for edits in Airtable's own window, which still win by arriving last. Use it for a small group editing the same things, by serving a room with this as its store.
