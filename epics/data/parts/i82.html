Looping over a list whose items are a type named in two words reads each item as a type nobody declared.

  • PokéSpell's - its "types" is a list of pokemon types: <for one in its types> (and <for its types />) types one as Pokemontype, not PokemonType.
  • So draw the pill of one can't find the pill drawing on a pokemon type: it compiles to core.drawThing(one.pill), a property nobody has, not one.drawPill().
  • TypeScript says so already, Cannot find name 'Pokemontype' (PokeSpell's snapshot.tsc.txt, before this change too).
  • The cause, likely: typeName() (the parser's parser.types.ts) builds list of pokemontypes with listOf(), which runs the words together; listOfType() beside it keeps them, list of pokemon-types, so itemTypeOf() gives PokemonType back.

Worked round in PokéSpell: a Pokémon's kinds are drawn as a group of labels that sizes them (<for its types as="labels" size="small" />), so no loop item is named.
