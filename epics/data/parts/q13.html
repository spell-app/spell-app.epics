Several people at once is planned around one referee that puts every change in order (Q5). AT Protocol has no such thing: each person writes only to their own account, on their own host.

So two people never fight over one record, but a shared cookbook is everyone's own recipes put together, and one person can't fix another's typo. 

family cookbook is each person's own at protocol account:
	- recipes are at "com.example.recipe"

It uses AT Protocol as it was designed, and each person keeps their data. Reads combine accounts, a version check guards your own devices, and the change stream shows other people's changes.

Changing someone else's recipe is refused, at compile time where Spell can tell.

Sharing works as on any other place, and anyone can edit anything. But the data no longer belongs to each person, which is the reason to choose AT Protocol.

The smallest job: showing what's already there. It leaves the writing half of this back-end untested.

  • This place never uses the referee, so the people here means something different on it.
