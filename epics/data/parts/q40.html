Why the cry is slow and seems to need many clicks: Spell can't tell an element to play, so hear it sets the pokedex's hearing to nothing, pauses 50 msec, sets it to the Pokémon, and a NEW player is drawn with autoplay.

  • The sound then starts when that player is ready, not from the click itself.
  • Browsers let sound start without a click only by their own rules. Chrome as it comes allows it (reproduced: it plays, about 430 ms after the first click, 80 ms after later ones); Chrome with --autoplay-policy=user-gesture-required, like Safari's and phones' rules, refuses it every time (reproduced: never plays).
  • Safari also lets a play through only shortly after a click: so the first clicks, while the file comes from GitHub, likely do nothing, and later ones work once it's in the cache. That's the "click many times".
  • A click during the 50 msec pause is a repeat of a running action, so it's dropped.

What this branch does now, with nothing new: the Pokémon's page draws a silent player of its cry as it opens, so the sound is loaded before you press; the click's player plays it from the cache (about 80 ms after the click from the second one, 260 ms the first). Still refused where a browser wants the play to come from the click.

Each way below plays it from the click itself, at once:

  1. A statement that plays a sound: play its sound / stop its sound; Spanish reproducir su sonido / parar su sonido. Compiles to core.play(url): one player per sound file, loaded as the page first reads the sound, played inside the click. PokéSpell's hear it becomes one line, and hearing and the cry drawing go.
  2. Tell a drawn part what to do: <draw "cry"><audio src={its sound} /></draw>, then play its cry; Spanish reproducir su grito. Compiles to the drawn <ui-audio>'s play(). Also opens pause, focus ... on any drawn part.
  3. No new words, a Spell UI change: <ui-audio> and <ui-video> answer the platform's button commands, <button command="--play" commandfor="cry">. Reads as markup, not English, and needs ids.
