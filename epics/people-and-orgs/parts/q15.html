How each old project address maps to the new scheme. Now that this epic rewrites every old address once (Q12), the mapping has to be settled before the address phase (P4) starts.

The new scheme, from Owen's kickoff and the address decision (Q5):

  • A space is a person at an org, owen@spell, or an org alone, @spell.
  • The @spell space holds what every install has: examples, guides, the library.
  • A slash after the space opens a running app: owen@spell/solitaire.
  • Another slash opens a page in it: owen@spell/solitaire/rules.
  • A colon after the space names a file: matt@three-degrees:bubble-trouble/folder/file.json.

Two rules that go with every row:

  • Project names are lower-kebab in addresses: Solitaire becomes solitaire, "Drawable Solitaire" becomes drawable-solitaire.
  • An address finds its project by that kebab form, whatever its case: @spell/examples/Solitaire still opens Solitaire.
  • The folders on disk keep their names. Nothing moves on disk until the spaces epic.

The one real fork is what a slash after a space means:

  • In owen@spell/solitaire/rules, a slash after the project means a page in the running app.
  • So in @spell/examples/solitaire, is "examples" a folder grouping projects, or a project whose page is "solitaire"? The address alone can't say.
  • Three examples are also test fixtures today, under the same names: Solitaire, FizzBuzz and OutlineSolitaire. Dropping the grouping means renaming one of each pair.
  • "examples/solitaire" is one project's name.
  • An address is read against the projects that exist: the longest project name that matches wins, and the rest is a page.
  • Keeps the address decision's example, and Owen's guide example, exactly as he wrote them.
  • Anyone's space can group projects: owen@spell/games/klondike.
  • Cost: reading an address needs a lookup, and a project can't share a name with a group (both called "examples" is refused when made).
