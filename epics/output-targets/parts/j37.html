Some smaller choices about the shape of the new list methods.

  • A change returns the list, so calls chain: pile.append(card).reverse().
    • That's the house style for methods that change things.
    • The spellCore twins answer nothing.
    • The old add() stays as it was, and answers nothing.
  • map() answers a scratch list of the same class, as spellCore.map() does. The old List.map() answered a plain array.
    • TypeScript can't say "a Pile, holding numbers", so its type is a plain List of whatever the callback answers.
    • Nothing in the repo called the old one.
  • Asking about a value takes anything, not just an item. That covers:
    • includes(), remove()
    • startsWith(), endsWith()
    • startingWith()
    • the item that addBefore() / addAfter() look for
    • Why: a program often asks with a value that may be nothing, e.g. a card that may be missing.
    • Adding still takes only the list's own kind of item.
  • startsWith() / endsWith() take ONE thing, as their twins do.
  • merged() without a class makes a list of its first list's class, and answers nothing for an empty list of lists, as merge the piles does.
