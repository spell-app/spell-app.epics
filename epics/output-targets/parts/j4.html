P3 was to put SPELL NAMES in the tree (is a {suit}, not is_a_$suit), with each writer making its own legal names. Chose to leave names, and constants' and enumerations' spellings, as they are for now, because they aren't decided in the tree: they're baked into scope records that the language server, scope packs and other projects read.

  • Names: a method's javascript name (play_fizzbuzz, is_a_$suit) is its scope record's output, worked out while parsing and written into the project's declarations, which importing projects read back.
  • Constants: ScopeConstant.output defaults to 'up' (single quotes, ScopeConstant.ts); ASTConstantExpression writes it verbatim.
  • Enumerations: the rule keeps its values pre-quoted ("clubs") in its rule data (classes.ts).
  • Doing it properly means scope records hold spell names and values, and every reader (LSP, scope packs, declarations, imports) changes with them: a phase of its own. P5 (declarations into a file) touches the same records, so it's the natural place to start.

What P3 DID take out of the tree: operators (now meanings: equals, and ...), text values (plain text plus a quote), and this / undefined / null (now Self, Nothing, MissingExpression).
