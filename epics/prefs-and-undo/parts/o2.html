Undo and redo are the kit's UndoStack, in util's persist folder: steps, two stacks, and changes to the same thing folded into one step. It's generic: Spell's runtime builds its history on it (1.3), and spell-app may too (Q14).

An undo step

  • A name in a person's words, e.g. "Change Margin".
    • The Edit menu puts "Undo" or "Redo" in front of it: "Undo Change Margin".
    • Short and capitalized like a menu item: "Add Register", "Copy".
  • A list of records, each knowing how to undo and redo itself: what a record holds is up to whoever builds on the stack.
  • Optionally, the thing it changed.

Two stacks

  • Undo pops the newest step off the undo stack, puts it on the redo stack, then runs its undo.
  • Redo does the reverse.
  • A new change empties the redo stack: what was undone can't be redone after something else happens.
  • An optional limit drops the oldest steps once there are too many.
  • The step's undo or redo runs just after the stacks have changed, so whatever it triggers already sees the new state. Nothing records while it undoes or redoes.
  • Undo and redo put the records back at once: nothing is run again, nothing waited on, so redoing an action that waited is instant.
  • clear() empties both, e.g. when another document opens.

Folding changes into one step

A new step with the same name AND the same thing as the newest one folds into it: the newest step keeps both steps' changes.

  • So typing "hello" into one field is one undo step, not five.
    • Undo goes all the way back to the value before the first letter.
    • Redo goes forward to "hello".
  • A change with no thing named never folds.
  • A recorder that can merge records overrides how it folds (fold()).

The menu reads live state

  • Can undo, can redo, and the two titles ("Undo Change Margin", or just "Undo" when there's nothing).
  • They update by themselves as the stacks change, so a menu or button just shows them.
  • The titles can be worded in another language, the whole title at once (titleFor).

Transactions: one step for everything an action does

  • Everything recorded while a transaction is open is its ONE step: until its function returns, or its promise settles, waits and promise chains included (Q6).
  • Undoable work nested in it, or begun while it waits, joins it: never a step of its own. E.g. moving ten items is one "Move Items", not ten.
  • A throw still records what changed, and passes the error on; nothing recorded, no step.
  • It replaced the other project's "skip undo" option on each step that changes something.
