Asked in the planning modal, 2026-10-24, from Owen's Q6 note "sometimes things aren't undoable": some changes shouldn't be undone (a selection, a hover), and some actions CAN'T be (sent an email, deleted on the server). How does undo treat them?

Owen's answer: A, but an non-undoable action can say whether it resets undo queue or not.

  • A property declared not undoable is left out of every step.
  • An action declared as one that can't be undone says which:
    • it keeps the history: earlier steps stay undoable
    • it empties the history: undo never puts back a state the world has moved past (Finder's Empty Trash)
  • A string mode, not a boolean (WWOD), e.g. undo: "keeps" | "clears": the names settled in the phase.
