A Spell app can't undo: actions are plain method calls, and nothing records what they change.

The runtime's own history, built on UndoStack, records property and list changes; an action declared undoable is one transaction; every app has undo and redo, with ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z.

  • One history per app run, cleared on a new run and when the app loads saved state (its document changes: Q3).
  • It keeps the least it needs: for each thing and property, the value before the first change and after the last; for a list, what was added and taken out where.
  • An action declared undoable, e.g. declareAction("turnOver", { undoneAs: "Turn Card" }), is ONE step, whatever it changes and however long it waits.
  • Every property write and every list change records into the open transaction; the while handlers' put-it-back keeps working on the same recorder.
  • Opting out (Q10):
    • a property declared not undoable (a selection, a hover) is left out of every step
    • an action declared as one that can't be undone says whether it also empties the history
  • Every app: undo(), redo(), and live canUndo, canRedo, undoTitle, redoTitle for its Edit menu.
  • ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z while focus is in the app, through Spell UI's keyboard registry; a text field keeps its own ⌘Z.
  • The library compiled apps import exports what's new; the core's method list and Python's not-ported list follow.
  • The compiler writes nothing yet: the syntax waits for elemental-spell; tests use hand-compiled programs.

⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z are off inside a text field and inside a modal, as the other project's actions were (Q19): a text field keeps its own undo, a modal its own keys.
