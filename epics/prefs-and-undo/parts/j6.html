The plan said a list's undo step keeps only what changed: what went out and what came in, and where. Built instead: a step keeps the list's items before the step and after it, whole.

  • Both are the same cards, not copies: an array of references for each list the step changed.
    • A 3-card deal keeps the stock's items before and after, and the waste's.
  • Why not a splice: putting one list of an exclusive family back moves its items out of the others.
    • Undoing a deal puts the stock back first, which takes each card out of the waste: by the time the waste's own record runs, a splice's place in it would be wrong.
    • Whole lists end right in any order: each one is simply set back to what it held.
  • The cost: one array of references for each changed list in each step, and a run keeps at most 100 steps.
  • Properties are kept as planned: each one's value before its first change in the step, and after its last.
  • undo and redo always put every list of a step back exactly, owners included
  • a step of a long list keeps the whole list twice, as references, not just the few items that moved
