Prefs and Undo: analysis

Four big choices that shape remembered preferences and undo in Spell apps. Nothing is built yet: your answers shape the plan, whenever this epic is started.

session elemental-spell, writing down ideas from another project 2026-10-24
prefs-and-undo -- a FUTURE epic: preferences that last for each person, and undo / redo, for Spell apps. You showed the elemental-spell session a preference store and an undo stack you built in another project, and asked for their ideas to be kept, without their code. The ideas are in the epic page's Overview. how much Spell does for an app by itself: where its preferences are kept, and whether undo needs any code at all.

The problem

  • A Spell app forgets everything on reload: a person's theme, a panel's size, the last thing they opened.
  • A Spell app has no undo. A person who drags the wrong card has to put it back by hand.

What exists today

  • Spell's runtime keeps nothing between visits, and records no undo.
  • Spell UI has a few one-off stores, each for one job:
    • DismissalStore: a nag remembers it was dismissed
    • SavedTheme: the theme a viewer made and chose to use
    • the docs site's theme and navigation preferences
  • Each guards its own storage access and picks its own keys.

The shape proposed

A property marked remembered, and undo that records itself (the epic page's Overview, 1.3, has more):

a "solitaire" is an app where:
	- its remembered "theme" is one of light or dark  // kept for each person, across visits
	- to deal the cards, undone as "Deal":               // ONE undo step, however many cards move
		...

a "card" is a view where:
	- to turn it over, undone as "Turn Card":
		...
// Edit > Undo Turn Card   (⌘Z)   -- every app has undo and redo, with live titles

What goes away

  • Spell UI's one-off stores could move onto the one preference store, each keeping its own keys.

The catch

  • Undo that records itself has to know where one step ends. Dragging a card changes its pile, its position and its direction: that should undo as ONE step.
  • A preference kept on the server needs a signed-in person, and Spell apps have no sign-in today.
  • The browser's storage can be missing, blocked or full: every read and write has to survive that.

Rough size

About 1-2 days for both, once the words are settled: most of it is wiring into actions and properties.

Where does a remembered property keep its value, unless the app says otherwise?

Works today with no sign-in. An app with signed-in people names a server store, and nothing else in it changes.

  • One namespace per app, so apps on one site never share keys (the Overview, 1.1).
  • A value too big for the browser's quick storage goes to its database, which has to be waited on.

Follows a person to any device, but needs sign-in before any app can remember anything.

Nothing hidden, but every app has to say it before its first remembered property works.

Does every property change record its own undo, or only the actions that ask for it?

Undo works with no code. Changes made inside an action are one step under the action's name; typing into one field is one step.

  • A change outside any action is named after its property: "Change Title".
  • A change can opt out: a selection, a hover, a scroll position.

Nothing is recorded by surprise, but an app has no undo until each action is marked.

Full control, and the most code: what the other project did, before Spell's reactive things.

Is there one undo history for the whole app, or one per document or view?

What a person expects from ⌘Z in most apps. Simplest to build and to explain.

Each open document keeps its own history. Needed once an app holds several documents at once.

Each editor on the page has its own, like text fields in a browser. ⌘Z then depends on what's focused.

Does this wait for epic elemental-spell to settle its words (Template, Action) and its event model?

The syntax (remembered, undone as) and the place undo is recorded both come from elemental-spell's actions and events.

The preference store and the undo stack don't depend on words: they could land in the runtime first, and Spell UI's one-off stores move onto them.
