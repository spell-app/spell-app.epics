  • A drag, by pointer events, so a mouse and a finger work alike
    • the dragged card, and any cards below it, follow the pointer
    • dropped on a pile that can take them, they move there
    • dropped anywhere else, they go back where they were
  • Which pile can take them: the piles' own rules, as a click uses them
    • can take a card if and can give up a card if
    • the first pile in the piles where it can take the card
  • What can be dragged:
    • from a tableau: any face-up card, with the cards below it
    • from a stacked pile: its top card, if it's face up
    • from the discards: only its top card
    • from the stock: nothing (a click still turns a card over)
  • A click still plays a card, as today: a click is a press with no drag
  • What shows it can be clicked or dragged, by itself:
    • an element with an onClick: cursor: pointer and user-select: none
    • a draggable one: the browser's drag cursor, grab, then grabbing while dragged
    • where that's done, the drawing core or Spell UI: decided in this phase
  • Where to see it: run Solitaire or Solitario (VS Code, Spell: Run Project) and drag a card from pile to pile, with a mouse or a finger; the tests V15-V20 step through it.
  • Dragging, in the page's element for a drawable thing (SpellElement, with a new Dragging beside it):
    • anything its list will give up (the list's own can give up rule) can be dragged
    • by pointer events, so a mouse and a finger work alike; a finger drags rather than scrolls
    • it and its list's items after it follow the pointer, raised above the board (a tableau's face-up card, with the cards on it)
    • a press that moves less than 6 pixels is still a click; a real drag swallows the click after it
  • The drop: the page finds the drawable list under the pointer, and the program hears an event (J43):
    • its name from a new core message per language, drop-event: English card-drop, Spanish soltar-carta (J44)
    • its details name the thing by its type, and the list by each of its types: { card, tableau, pile }
    • with no handler, the thing just moves there by the lists' rules
    • cards that moved glide from where they were dropped (P27's glide); a drop nobody takes slides back
  • What changes in how you write Spell:
    • a drop handler: on card drop with a card and a pile (Spanish al soltar-carta con una carta y una pila)
    • Solitaire's new to put a card on a pile (Spanish poner una carta en una pila): checks both piles' rules, moves the card and the cards on it, turns the start tableau's new last card up, and scores; play a card finds the first pile that takes the card and calls it, and a drop calls it too
    • anything with an onClick shows a pointer and its text can't be selected, with no CSS: Spell writes an inline style, unless the element says its own style (J45); the cards' hand-written cursor in Solitaire's styles is gone
    • a draggable thing shows the browser's grab cursor, grabbing while dragged
  • Which cards can be dragged, as you listed them:
    • tableau: any face-up card, with the cards below it
    • discards: the top card only
    • stock: nothing; it now can never let go of a card, and a click on it still turns a card over (J47)
    • foundation: nothing either, since its rule never lets a card go (J46)
