Yes, it makes sense, and spell can be made to read this way. Types are needed for most of it, but they are not enough on their own: about half the sketch is new grammar whatever happens with types.

  • It reads well because it is a list, not a story. The outline names the subject once, in the heading line, and each bullet says one fact about it.
    • today, every line starts by naming its subject again
    • e.g. a card has ..., a card "is ..." for ...
    • and the color of a card is ...
  • Almost none of the MEANING is new. Today's Card.spell already says nearly every line of the sketch.
    • 1.2 has them side by side
    • even the "quotes teach new language" idea is there: a card "is a (suit)" for its suits
  • What's new is the LAYOUT and the words left out:
    • a type heading with a bulleted body
    • it and its meaning the type being defined
    • — headings, curly quotes
    • and the parser working out what the left-out words were:
    • it “is a suit” leaves out "(suit)" and "for its suits": the parser has to see that suit names one of the card's properties
    • (rank) in "images/(rank)-of-(suit).png" leaves out its
  • Types are the "magic". Every left-out word is found by asking "what kind of thing is this?": the parser knows its deck is a deck, so "a suit of its deck" can mean one of the deck's suits (1.3).
  • Today, none of the sketch parses. A probe ran every line through the real parser: 28 errors, one per line. Three things fail before any grammar runs:
    • curly quotes “ ” aren't quotes yet (the tokenizer takes only " and ')
    • — isn't a comment yet (only #, --, //)
    • a - bullet in front of a line breaks it
  • The sketch has a few slips and loose ends. Each is a question below, or fixed in the tidied sketch (1.6):
    1. “is is face up” / “is is face down”: doubled is, a typo
    2. - “rank” is a rank of its deck has no its; the suit line does (Q4)
    3. “directions” are up or down declares a KIND of value, but the next lines read its direction, a property (Q4)
    4. drawing a card is a heading with no —, so it can't be told from a statement (Q5)
    5. “is the rank of suits”: plural suits, where the other phrases use the singular
    6. source=“images/...png“: opens and closes with the same curly quote
    7. if it is jack, queen, king: no or / one of; and ace, 2, 3, 4 ... 10 is a range, which spell has no word for yet
    8. a two sided thing: no such type exists (Q6)
