A type's heading may end where with no colon, and its indented lines are its body; with still needs its colon. Built in P10 (Headings Without Colons).

  • Why with keeps it: a deck is a list of cards with jokers could be the start of a name, so a bare with at the end of a heading would be a guess.
  • Why where can go without: it's a word a heading ends with, never one a name starts with, so the tests found nothing it could be misread as.
  • How: the rule that ends a heading, with_nested_statements, is registered a second time, with the syntax where.
    • the parser can't put a symbol inside a word choice, so (where :|with :) isn't possible in one syntax
  • Since the heading needs no colon at all now (P10), this matters only to someone who writes where out of habit and leaves off the colon.
  • a card is a thing where works like a card is a thing where:
  • a deck is a list of cards with, no colon, stays an error
