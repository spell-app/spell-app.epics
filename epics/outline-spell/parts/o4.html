The marks that make the outline, what each means, and how hard it is to teach the parser.

Mark Means Cost
A “card” is a ... where: what follows, indented, is all about a card medium: a new scope that knows its subject; the bullets' rules learn to take it / its as their subject
- (a bullet) one fact about the subject easy: stripped before the line is read
— Definition of a card — a heading (dashes on both ends) easy: joins ##; outline, folding and the Type Explorer follow
— suit and rank a sub-heading, or a comment easy: — joins -- and //
— e.g. “the card is a spade” at a line's end an example: becomes the hover text easy: trailing comments already work
“ ” the same as ": text, or a new word or phrase where one is declared easy, see Q3
  • Typing them: nothing in the editor helps type — or “ ” today. Proposal: the plain forms always work (--, "), and the formatter can turn them into the typographic ones (Q3).
  • Indents: the sketch indents with 4 spaces. The parser counts each space as a level, so 4 spaces is 4 levels. It still parses, and the formatter turns them into tabs, as it does today.
  • Free gift: the editor's outline (Cmd-Shift-O) already nests a declaration's body under it, so a type's bullets show under the type with no extra work.
