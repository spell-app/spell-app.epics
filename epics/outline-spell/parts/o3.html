"Types" here means the parser knowing what kind of thing each word is while it reads: that its deck is a deck, that its suit is a suit. The precedence-and-types epic (finished 2026-10-05) built most of that. Two gaps matter here.

  • A list of values isn't a kind of thing yet.
    • as one of clubs, diamonds ... makes a list on the card, Card.Suits
    • each value is plain text: 'clubs'
    • so its suit has no type, and "the color of a suit" has nowhere to live
    • that epic left it open: its caveat C10 and its todo T7, "ad-hoc enumerations as named types"
    • the values are also global: two lists sharing a value (red as a color and as a team) would collide
  • Bare words don't mean "its". Inside a method, rank alone isn't looked up on the card; you write its rank. Easy to add, since the parser already knows it is a card there.

What types would make possible, once those gaps close:

  • it “is a suit”: suit is a kind of thing, and the card has exactly one property of that kind, so the slot and "for its suits" are found, not written
  • a suit of its deck: its deck is a deck, and a deck has suits
  • the color of a suit is:: a suit is a kind of thing, so it can have a color
  • (rank) in text: rank is a property of the card being drawn

New grammar, whatever happens with types:

  • a type heading with a body (where: / with:), and it / its meaning that type inside it
  • properties declared by a quoted name (its “suit” is ...)
  • a value-per-line body: red if ... then black otherwise
  • is jack, queen, king as "is one of", and ranges (2 ... 10)
  • (name) inside text
  • a phrase whose first word isn't is (“draw its front”): today's quoted phrases must start with is and answer yes / no
  • "if we get types working fully" is half the answer: the outline layer is its own phase
