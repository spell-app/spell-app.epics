In a type's body a property still needs its quotes or its its: - suit is a suit of its deck is "Don't understand", and - x is 1 quietly sets a variable beside the class.

Directly in a type's body, a bare name declares the type's own property or getter: any bare name (T8, point 2, option A). Two guards: a line starting with a statement's first word stays that statement, and a getter that never reads it warns.

  • A property: - suit is a suit of its deck, - score is a number, - face is up or down.
  • A getter: - state is: with a block under it, or - name is: its rank + ....
  • are as well as is: - foundations are a list of piles.
  • - if x is 1 stays an if: a line starting with if, set, for, return ... is never a name; a property named so keeps its quotes.
  • - score is 0 warns: "score is always 0: did you mean a number?"
  • A kind keeps its quotes: - "suits" as one of clubs, ...; a bare suits as one of ... stays an error.
