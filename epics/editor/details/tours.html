Tours: a quick tour for a page

A new Spell UI tag, <ui-tour>, walks a person through a feature, one numbered step at a time. Before anything is built, this page settles how a tour is written, and above all the new Spell words that start it and move through it.

agent editor-tour, while writing up the new tour phase 2026-11-19
editor -- the Spell editor, and the help it gives people. You asked for a new phase, a "quicktour" for a page, and a page to discuss it first. The phase is on the epic page, last, still planning. What a tour looks like to write, in HTML and in Spell, the words that drive it, when one starts by itself, and which tours we write first. Nothing is built until you've answered.

A tour is a short list of steps that leads someone through one feature.

  • Steps are numbered 1, 2, 3 ...; a step can also have a name ("Run it").
  • Each step can:
    • point at something on the page: a button, a pane, a part of the app
    • dim the rest with a scrim, a dark layer with a hole cut around what it points at
    • say something in a small popup beside it, with Back, Next and Skip
    • wait for the person to do something ("press this button"): everything else is masked off, and the tour goes on once they've done it
  • A tour is started by a person (a Help menu, the tour gallery), by code, or by a condition ("the first time someone opens Design mode").
  • An app can have many tours: the tour gallery is a popup that lists them.

What Spell UI already has that a tour would reuse:

Piece Already in Spell UI What the tour uses it for
The step's popup <ui-popup>, anchored to a target, placed by the browser the step's text, beside what it points at
The scrim <ui-dimmer page>, which dims everything and blocks clicks the dark layer; the cut-out hole is new
The ring the editor's Inspect ring (Inspector.ring()) the outline around what a step points at
Step numbers <ui-steps ordered> "Step 2 of 5" dots in the popup, and the gallery
"Seen it already" <ui-nag>'s dismissal memory remembering a tour was taken, so it doesn't start again

A tour is a tag holding its steps, in order. It draws nothing until it's started.

On Attribute What it does
<ui-tour> name the title the gallery and the popup show
scrim every step dims the rest, unless a step says scrim="none"
remember where "taken already" is kept (Q8)
<ui-tour-step> target what it points at: a CSS selector, or an element set by script; none: a popup in the middle
name optional; a step can be jumped to by its number or its name
placement where the popup sits: top, bottom, left, right; <ui-popup>'s words
scrim dim (default when the tour has one), none
waits-for an event on the target (click, input, ui-opened): Next is hidden, everything else is masked, the tour goes on when it happens

Its script side and events, named the way every Spell UI event is now (opening / opened):

  • Escape, or Skip, ends the tour; the arrow keys go back and next.
  • A target that isn't on the page (a pane folded away) skips that step, and the console says why.
  • Spanish: <es-recorrido> and <es-paso-del-recorrido>, by the usual vocabularies.

Every new phrase has to sit beside these, which Spell already reads. Each is where a tour word could be misread:

Spell today What it does A tour word it could swallow
start animation "spin", stop process "deal" starts or stops a named animation none: start tour "x" is the same shape, a good sign
stop the deal stops a running action named deal stop the tour reads as "stop an action named tour"
go to the settings page moves to another page of the site go to the next step, if a page were named "next step"
show "help", show the settings page shows a named part, or a page show the tour gallery, show step 3
wait for the answer waits for a result wait for a click on ...

A tour written in a Spell app: what shape should it take? Each turns into the <ui-tour> above.

A tour is a thing on the app, its steps a list, as an app's properties are. Reads like the rest of a .spell file.

New words: is a tour, step, point at, say, dim the rest, wait until it's clicked.

Each tour its own .md: a heading per step, its text below. Easy for anyone to write; a second way to say the same thing.

No new Spell words yet: apps write <ui-tour> in their HTML. Cheapest; apps written in plain Spell can't have one.

The words that start a tour and end it.

start tour "first game", stop tour. Your words, and the same shape as start animation "spin".

  • stop tour stops whichever tour is showing; only one shows at a time.
  • stop the tour also works: the parser has to prefer it over "stop an action named tour". If an app has its own action named tour, the app's wins, as play does today.
  • Spanish: empieza el recorrido "primera partida", para el recorrido.

start the "first game" tour, end the tour. More like English; the name in the middle could read as a property, "the first game tour".

show tour "first game", hide the tour. Reuses a word people know; show already means three things, this makes four.

Going straight to one step of the tour that's showing, by its number or its name.

go to step 3, go to step "drag". HyperTalk's go to card 3. The word step tells it apart from going to a page.

jump tour to step 3, your first wording. Clear, but jump is a verb Spell has nowhere else.

show step 3. Short; one more meaning for show, and "step" could be a part's name.

Moving one step on, or one step back, from code: a button of the app's own, a key, or a step that moves on by itself. You weren't sure of the words.

Matches Q3's go to step 3, and HyperTalk's go next card / go back.

  • Also read: go to the previous step.
  • Ambiguity: go back alone, in HyperTalk, is the browser's Back. So "a step" is required here, never a bare go back.
  • Ambiguity: go to the next step could be read as a page named "next step"; the tour wins while one is showing.

Two words each. Shortest; a statement that starts with a noun, which Spell has nowhere else.

Your other wording. Says "tour" every time, so nothing else can match; long, and advance is new.

"Press this button": everything else masked, the tour goes on once it's pressed. How does a step say that?

point at the new game button and wait until it's clicked. "it" is what the step points at; other events read the same way: until it's typed in, until it's open.

  • Waiting always masks the rest, whether the step dims or not: only the target can be clicked.
  • Next is hidden; Back and Skip stay.

wait for a click on the new game button. Plain, but wait for already waits for an answer, so the parser must tell the two apart.

say "Press New Game to go on", and the tour works out from press that it waits. Shortest; the step's text and its behaviour get tangled.

In Spell, how does a step name the button or pane it points at?

point at the stock, point at its score: the names the app already gives its parts, the ones the Inspector's Views tree shows. Survives a translation and a restyle.

point at the "New game" button: what people see. Breaks when the label changes, or in Spanish.

A part's name, else the words on it. Most forgiving; two ways to write one step.

You called the scrim optional. When a step says nothing, is the rest of the page dimmed?

Steps just point, and the page stays usable. A step says dim the rest when it matters; a waiting step (Q5) masks anyway. A whole tour can say it once.

Every step dims, as Shepherd and driver.js do. Focused, but the person can't try anything until the tour ends.

"Start the Design tour the first time someone opens Design mode." Where is "they've had it already" kept?

Kept in the browser, beside the editor's own settings, as <ui-nag> remembers being closed. Works with no sign-in; a second computer shows it once more.

  • Remembered once the tour is finished OR skipped: skipping counts as "seen".
  • The gallery can always start it again.
  • One switch in settings, "Show tours by themselves", turns every automatic start off.

Kept with the person's account, so it follows them. Needs them signed in; a later step.

Forgotten when the tab closes. Simplest; the tour comes back every day, which grates.

A tour that starts by itself can annoy people: that's the "tour fatigue" most tour libraries get blamed for. What does an automatic start look like?

A note by the feature: "New here? Take the 4-step tour." Start or No thanks. At most one a visit.

Step 1 just appears. Fastest to the help; the most interrupting.

A small dot on the feature, as react-joyride's "beacon" does; a click starts the tour. Calmest; easy to miss.

An app with several tours lists them in a popup. How do people reach it, and what does it show?

A card per tour: its name, one line, how many steps, a tick once taken. A click starts it. In Spell: show the tour gallery.

  • A tag of its own, <ui-tour-gallery>, listing every <ui-tour> on the page, so any app gets one for free.
  • The editor's Help menu gets "Tours…"; an app puts the gallery wherever it likes.

Each tour a menu item, no popup. Fine for three tours; no room for a line about each, or a tick.
