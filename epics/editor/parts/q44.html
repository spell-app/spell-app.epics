For P34 · App Anatomy. A view draws with Spell UI's parts inside it: a card is a <solitaire-card>, and inside it a <segment>, a <text>, the card's picture. Which of these get a floor of their own decides how busy the stack is.

React Anatomy has the same question: it merges wrappers that draw the same box into one plane, and its Wrappers switch splits them again.

  • A, your views, with a switch for the parts (recommended): a floor for each thing the program draws (solitaire, each pile, each card, each card image); a Parts switch adds the Spell UI parts inside them.
    • The stack matches the program you wrote, and the tree's top rows: the app, its 13 piles, and each card and its picture in Solitaire.
    • The catch: a part with a look of its own (a toolbar, a menu) sits flat on its view's floor until the switch is on.
  • B, every element: views and parts alike, each its own floor.
    • Shows exactly how the page is built.
    • The catch: several hundred floors in Solitaire, most of them boxes inside boxes; hard to read without the Depth slider.
  • a floor per view the program draws, labelled with its type
  • a Parts switch in Anatomy's bar adds Spell UI's parts as floors of their own, remembered per project
