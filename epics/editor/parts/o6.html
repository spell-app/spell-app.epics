There is one selection, shared by the tree, the screen and the code, and it can hold many things.

Selecting:

  • Click selects one; shift-click takes a range; control or command-click adds and removes.
  • Dragging on the app's background draws a rubber band. Nothing like it exists yet, so it's new, in the Picker that draws the rings.
  • The click rule is lifted out of the running program's lists, so there is one copy.
  • With several selected, the properties card shows what they share, and "Mixed" where they differ. Whether the property editor can show "Mixed" is still to check.

The Views tree becomes a proper widget (Q4), since three hand-built trees exist and none takes arrow keys:

  • many selected, and arrow keys
  • a filter, behind a search icon in the header: no new row until you ask for it
  • an icon for each kind of thing: each component's vocabulary gets an icon, and a type can name its own
  • rows in plain words: "Moves: 0", not <ui-item "Moves: 0" />

Things and Templates mostly serve someone debugging a program, and they overlap with Views: a click in Things already inspects the thing in the properties card. They fold in now, stay available in the Program panel for developers, and get reimagined in a later phase.

  • Things not drawn on screen join the one tree, in a group of their own.
  • A template is reached from what's selected, through "All cards" and a link to its code.
