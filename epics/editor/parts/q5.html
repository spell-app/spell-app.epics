Your hunch was that they should fold into the rest. What they do today:

  • Things: a live list of what the running program made (67 in Solitaire), with a details pane. A click also inspects the thing in the properties card, so it overlaps.
  • Templates: the language's scope tree, built-ins too (App, View, Page, Date), labelled "Scopes" inside.

Both help someone debugging a program, and neither helps a newcomer.

  • Inspector: one tree, with undrawn things in a group of their own.
  • A template is reached from the selection ("All cards", and a link to its code).
  • The two views stay available in the Program panel, off by default.
  • Less code to keep.
  • The scope tree is the only view of the language's built-ins, and it would be gone.
  • No work, and the tab row stays.
  • A newcomer still meets two tabs that aren't for them.
  • The Inspector has no tabs on its tree, which saves the choosing and leaves room for the filter and actions.
