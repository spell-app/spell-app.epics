For P34 · App Anatomy, the 3D view that pulls the running app apart into layers. It decides what each floor actually is, and so what you can do with it.

React Anatomy, its inspiration, takes a copy of the page and rebuilds it as pictures on planes. Spell could do the same, or something simpler or livelier, because each view is already its own element on the page.

The three ways, each for Solitaire with a tableau pile in focus:

  • A, the live app, tilted apart (recommended): the real game's elements are turned and lifted in 3D, each view a floor above the one that holds it.
    • The game keeps running on its floors: a card moved, or a live edit, shows at once.
    • Clicks on a floor are the app's own elements, so selecting reuses what Inspect App does today.
    • The catch: a view whose drawing clips what's inside it (a scrolling list, a rounded box) keeps its children flat in it, so those floors don't lift. Such a view is lifted whole, its tag saying so.
  • B, outlines only: each view's box is measured, and drawn as an empty labelled frame, the floors stacked.
    • Small to build, never clipped, and clear about nesting.
    • The catch: no pictures, so a card's floor is a grey frame, not a card; you have to read the tags.
  • C, a still copy: the app is copied once into pictures, one per view, laid on floors, as React Anatomy does.
    • Looks the most like React Anatomy, and nothing clips.
    • The catch: it's a snapshot. A move in the game needs a fresh copy, and copying a view drawn in its own shadow root, with its own sheet, is the hardest of the three to get right.
  • Anatomy turns and lifts the app's own elements; turned off, it puts back every style it changed.
  • The floors are live: the game and live edits keep showing on them.
  • A floor's click and hover go through the app's existing selection and rings, not a new copy of them.
  • A view that clips its children is lifted as one floor, and its tag says why its children don't come apart.
  • live floors; a clipping view lifted whole
  • no pictures on the floors, only frames and tags; the smallest build
  • a snapshot, taken again by hand (or on each change); the biggest build
