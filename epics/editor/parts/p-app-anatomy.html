You can't see how a running app is put together: which view holds which, and how deep, without clicking through the tree a row at a time.

  • The idea comes from React Anatomy (Owen, 2026-11-17: "WANT ... Plan a phase for it"):
    • a browser developer-tools tab that takes a running React page and pulls it apart into layers, in 3D
    • each layer is one component, labelled with its name, stacked over its parent; you drag to turn the stack, scroll to zoom
    • beside it, the components' tree, and the selected one's props, live
    • a slider for how far apart the layers sit, another for how many levels deep; double-click a layer to show only it and what's inside it
  • Spell's version is easier and truer: every view is already its own element on the page (<solitaire-card>), and the editor already knows which thing each one draws, so nothing has to be guessed from the page.
  • What you'll see, in Solitaire:
    • Click Anatomy in the app pane's bar (beside Inspect App), or press its key.
    • The game tilts back, and comes apart into floors: the green table at the bottom; the stock, the discard pile, the four foundations and the seven tableau piles a floor above it; each pile's cards a floor above their pile; each card's picture above its card.
    • Each floor carries a small tag with its type, in the program's words: solitaire, tableau pile, card, card image. A tag is coloured like its type's band in the bubbly code, so the drawing and the code read alike.
    • Drag to turn the stack, scroll to zoom; Front looks straight at it again.
    • A Spacing slider pulls the floors apart or together; a Depth slider stops at the piles, or goes down to every card's picture.
    • Hover a floor and it lights, as the rings do today. Click it and it's selected, as a click on the app is: the tree's row, the Inspector's properties, and the code following along to its type (one selection, ).
    • Double-click a floor (or F) to show only it and what's inside it: one tableau pile and its cards. Esc goes back to the whole app.
    • The game keeps running underneath: a card moved, or a live edit to the code, shows on its floor at once.
    • Click Anatomy again, or Esc at the whole app, and it folds back flat.
  • The bar's words in English and Spanish, in the editor's words file.
  • Three choices to settle before it's built: how the floors are drawn (Q42), where it shows (Q43), and what gets a floor of its own (Q44). The picture above is each one's recommended answer.

  1. Settle Q42, Q43 and Q44 first. The steps below are for their recommended answers: the live app tilted apart, in the app pane, a floor per view with a Parts switch. Another answer changes steps 2-3 and keeps the rest.
  2. Finding the floors: walk the running app from its root element, through each view element and its shadow root, and keep the views (each knows the thing it draws, as Inspect App reads it). Each floor gets its depth, its type in the program's words and its type's colour from the bubbly look. With Parts on, Spell UI's elements inside a view count too. A view whose drawing clips its children is marked as lifted whole.
  3. Drawing the stack: a new AppAnatomy in the app pane turns the app with a CSS perspective, and lifts each floor by its depth times the Spacing; the Depth slider flattens floors below its level. Drag turns, scroll zooms, Front resets. Each floor's tag is drawn over the stack, not inside the app, so the app's own styles never reach it. Turning Anatomy off takes back every style it set.
  4. Selecting: a floor's hover and click go through the Inspector's existing ring and one selection (P11), so the tree, the properties and the code follow along as they do for a click in the app. Double-click or F shows one floor and what's in it, breadcrumbs above, Esc back out.
  5. Staying live: the stack is worked out again when the app draws something new (a card moved, a live edit), keeping the turn, zoom and focus.
  6. The switch beside Inspect App, the View menu's row and its key in EDITOR_HOTKEYS; the words in AppTexts, English and Spanish.
  7. A browser test over Solitaire (floors nested as the tree is, a click selects the card); yarn review in the app folder; check it live in Solitaire, and the console for Solid warnings; a picture of it for App UI's docs.
