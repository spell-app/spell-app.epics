Placing things in the app misfires: drops land in a page layout's panes, a click on a palette row often adds nothing you can see, and a view can't be worked on by itself.

Your words (Owen, 2026-11-18):

"Drag and drop should ignore panes in the <page-layout>. Also, clicking to add something doesn't always work or is often hidden. Also, there should be a way to drag and drop/edit the HTML for a component and its editor (e.g. card.spell) in isolation within the project that defined it."

Three outcomes, one per ask:

  • Drops land in the page, never in the layout around it.
    • In a site with a page layout, dragging from the palette (or moving a part) over the page shows the drop line inside the page you're looking at.
    • The layout's panes are passed over: its app menu, page menu, drawer, side bars and footer. They're written in the layout's own drawing, so a drop there today lands in the layout, and shows on every page.
    • Over empty space in the page, the drop goes at the end of the page's own drawing, not after the layout's footer.
    • The panes can still be changed when you mean to: select one first (in the app or the Views tree), then drag into it, or drop on its row in the tree.
  • A click on a palette row always adds, and you always see what it added.
    • With something selected, it goes beside it (or inside, if it holds things), as now.
    • With nothing selected, it goes at the end of the page shown, by the same rule as a drop on empty space (above): never into a layout pane, a hidden layout, or a page that isn't shown.
    • Once drawn, the new part is selected and scrolled into view, its ring showing.
    • When it can't be added, a notice says why, in plain words, rather than nothing happening.
    • When it's added but draws nothing you can see (an empty box, zero size), it's still selected, its row lit in the Views tree, and a short notice says it's there but empty.
  • A view on its own: its drawing and its property editor, edited by themselves, inside its project.
    • Open a view's file (Solitaire's Card.spell) and the app pane can show just that view, drawn for one thing: one card, not the whole game.
    • Beside it, its property editor, as the Inspector shows it (Card.editor.spell).
    • Both take the palette's drops and clicks, moves, cut, copy and paste, as the app does today: each written into that view's own file.
    • The thing it draws can be changed in the Inspector (rank, suit, face), and the view redraws, so you can see each case.
    • A live edit to the code redraws it in place, as the app does.
    • Where it shows, and what thing it draws, are two choices to settle first: where it shows (Q45) and which thing it draws (Q46).
  • Drops and clicks look for their place in the page shown, passing over its layout's panes.
  • What a click adds is selected and scrolled into view, or a notice says why it couldn't be added.
  • A view (a card) can be shown on its own, with its property editor beside it, both editable by drag and drop.
  1. See each failure first. On a site with a main layout (a drawer, a footer) and on Solitaire, drag and click palette rows with Playwright, and note where each lands:
    • a drop on the page's empty space: expected today to land after the layout's last pane, since the "end of the drawing" is found from the outermost parts drawn, and the layout's panes are among them
    • a click with nothing selected: the same rule, plus a hidden layout (kept drawn once shown) or a narrow window's closed drawer can take it
    • an add whose part draws with no size, or below the fold: nothing scrolls it into view
    Each finding goes in the phase's Done, so the fix answers what was seen, not what was guessed.
  2. Pass over the layout's panes. Teach the part finder which parts belong to the page shown: inside the page layout's default slot, and in the layout that's showing.
    • The drop finder skips a pane's parts (app menu, page menu, drawer, side bars, footer), unless the pane or something in it is selected.
    • "The end of the drawing" is the end of the page's own drawing, in the page's file, not of everything drawn.
    • A drop on a pane's row in the Views tree still goes there: the tree is where you pick on purpose.
  3. Make a click's add land, and show.
    • A palette click uses the same place as step 2.
    • Once drawn, the new part is selected (as now) and scrolled into view in the app pane, its ring showing.
    • Drawn with no size: its row in the Views tree is lit, and a short notice says it's there but empty.
    • No place at all: a notice says why, never nothing. New words in English and Spanish, checked with the novice-voice check.
  4. A view on its own (for Q45 A and Q46 A, the recommended answers; another answer changes this step's first two bullets).
    • With a view's file open, the app pane's bar gets an App | Card alone switch, remembered per file.
    • Card alone draws the view for one thing, in the pane's middle, on the programs' runtime as the editor's zones do: the card selected in the app, else a new one with its starting values, never added to the game.
    • Its property editor page, if it has one, beside it, drawn as the Inspector draws it.
    • Both are the pane's parts for the part finder, so the palette's drops and clicks, moves, cut, copy and paste write into the view's file and its editor's file, as they do for the app.
    • The Inspector changes the thing shown, and the view redraws; a live edit redraws in place.
  5. Tests.
    • A site-with-layout test in the integration tests: a drop and a click both write into the page's drawing, and what's added is inside the pane's visible box.
    • A Solitaire test: Card alone, a drop into the card's front lands in its drawing, a suit change redraws it.
    • The app's unit tests and the add-and-move tests as before.
  6. Finish. The app folder's review pass, the editor's console checked for Solid warnings, the phase's Done written, merged into main.
