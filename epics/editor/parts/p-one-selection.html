  • Click selects one; shift-click takes a range; control or command-click adds and removes.
  • Dragging on the app's background draws a rubber band.
  • The click rule has one copy, lifted out of the running program's lists.
  • The rings show every selected thing.
  • With several selected, the properties card shows what they share, and "Mixed" where they differ.

One selection, holding many, shared by the tree, the screen and the code. Click rules lifted out of the running program's lists.
  1. The rule, one copy. Lift the click, command-click and shift-range rule out of onClickToSelect() into a small plain class in Spell UI's runtime, handed any ordered list. The running program's lists then use it too. It lives in Spell UI because the new tree (P11) needs it, and a lower folder can't reach up to the app.
  2. The Inspector holds a set. picked becomes a list with a main one, addressed by the source places from P7 rather than by position, so a selection survives a move.
  3. Rings and boxes for all of them. The Picker stops clearing every other mark, and draws many fallback boxes instead of one.
  4. Rubber band. New: a drag from the app's background draws a band on a layer above the app, and selects what it touches. It uses the drag threshold already in the drag helper.
  5. Many in the properties card. Spell UI already does this: a form bound to a list edits them all, and shows blank with a mixed mark where they differ. So this only hands it the list, and shows "Mixed" as words if blank reads badly. An edit to several is one undo step, through P8's history.
  6. Words. "Select" goes in the word list (Q14), and Picked and the like are renamed. Parts or views is settled here too (T2). Grep first, and record what's found.
  7. Checked by: tests for the rule; a headless script doing shift-click, command-click and a band in Solitaire; one edit to several, undone in one ⌘Z.
  8. Left out: the tree's own selecting (P11); selection in the code (P15).
