The macOS column was from memory, so it was checked against Apple's own guidelines first. What they say, and what moved:

  • Text: macOS's default is 13pt, minimum 10pt (typography). Confirmed, and built as 13px on app pages.
  • Control height: macOS's default control is 28 by 28pt, minimum 20 by 20pt (accessibility). This replaced the memory of "24 or 28". Built as 28px.
  • Selection: a focused list item is white text on the accent colour; an unfocused one is standard text on grey (focus and selection). Confirmed for menus and lists, so segmented controls keep their raised white key.
  • Toolbars: buttons group on one piece of glass, while segmented controls, pop-up buttons and search each get their own, and titles get none (WWDC25, "Build an AppKit app with the new design"). Built as a capsule on the top bar's actions.
  • Shapes: mini to medium controls are rounded rectangles; large and extra large are capsules (same session). This moved a value: default controls stay 8px rounded, not pills.
  • Corners: a toolbar's controls are concentric with its corners (toolbars). Not changed here.
  • Inspectors: side bars float, but inspectors are edge-to-edge glass beside the content (same session). The editor's floating Inspector differs: left for Owen, as a call.
  • Contrast: 4.5:1 for text under 18pt, and a higher-contrast scheme under Increase Contrast (accessibility). Measured on real pixels: all chrome text passes.
  • Focus ring: Apple draws a ring round text fields and a highlight in lists, and gives no width or offset. So 3px and 1px are a judgement.

Apple's pages: developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines (materials, typography, accessibility, focus-and-selection, toolbars, sidebars, segmented-controls), and developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/310.
