For P32 · Spell Console (r4). A right click on a method in the code offers Debug in Console. It could fill the input with a call to it, its inputs left blank to type; or run it at once on the selected thing; or also pause inside it, a step at a time, which is a debugger and much bigger.

This is about one menu item in P32 · Spell Console, and what it should do when you pick it.

The Spell Console will be a new line at the bottom of the editor's console.

  • You type a line of Spell there and press Return.
  • It runs against the app that's running, as the browser's console does with a web page.
  • The answer shows under it: a value, or an error in red.

Your words, in the request it came from (r4): "Would be cool to right-click on a method in the running file and say 'debug in console'".

A "method" here is one of the program's own actions: a to ... block in a .spell file. Solitaire has three kinds, and each makes the menu item harder.

  • No inputs, and it belongs to the app: turn over the next card. The console line is just its name, and it can run as it is.
  • One input: play a card needs to know WHICH card.
  • Belongs to a thing, and takes an input: move a card to a pile is written inside "a pile". It needs WHICH pile it's for, and WHICH card to move. 
// Solitaire.spell:  no inputs
to "turn over the next card", undoable as "Turn Card"

// Solitaire.spell:  one input, a card
to "play a card"

// Pile.spell:  inside "a pile", so it belongs to one;  its input, a card
a "pile" is a viewable, droppable list of cards
	to move a card to a pile

That's why it's a question: you right-click the method's name in the code, which says nothing about which card or which pile. So "debug it" can't simply run it. Something has to fill in the missing parts: you, by typing, or the thing you've picked in the running app (the one the Inspector shows).

The three answers, each as what happens when you right-click move a card to a pile, having picked column 3 in the running game:

  • A, fill the input (recommended): the console's line fills with the call, and nothing runs yet.
    • What you'd see: move ‹a card› to ‹column 3›. The pile is the one you picked; the card is a blank, selected, for you to type over.
    • turn over the next card fills in whole: you press Return to run it.
    • The catch: it takes a key press and some typing, every time.
  • B, run it at once: the console runs the call straight away, on what you've picked.
    • turn over the next card runs at once, and the game turns a card.
    • The catch: move a card to a pile can't run. The pick gives the pile, but nothing says which card, so it would stop with an error.
    • And a stray right click changes the game in progress, if the question of where lines run (Q38) settles on the running app.
  • C, pause inside it, a step at a time: a real debugger.
    • It runs the call, stops on its first line, and you step through: the card, then each card on it, moving to the pile, with each value shown.
    • The catch: it's a debugger, much bigger than this phase. It needs a way to pause the running app, and a stepping view. It would be its own phase, after A.

Why A: it works for every method, with or without inputs, and you see the exact line before it runs. It's also what the console is for: trying a line out.

One thing to settle later, whichever you pick: how the line names the thing you picked (the "column 3" above). Spell may need a new way to say it, and that would come to you as its own question before it's built.

  • The code editor's right-click menu, over a method while the app runs, gets Debug in Console.
  • It fills the console's line with a call to that method, and runs nothing.
  • The thing you picked in the running app goes in as its owner or its input, where it fits.
  • Every other input is a selected blank to type over; Return runs it.
  • No stepping debugger in P32 · Spell Console.
