For P49 · One Repo Tooling: how a worktree cut BEFORE repo-split's P20 brings its child repos' work across, when it merges the new main.

  • Such a worktree has a worktree of each child repo inside it (spell-ui/ ...), each on its own branch.
  • The new main has those repos as folders (ui/ ...), their history rewritten by P20, so a child branch can't be merged in as it is.
  • Today, 3 of the 7 epic worktrees have child-repo commits main lacks: debut (spell-ui 1, spell-app 1), elemental-spell (spell-parser 2, spell-ui 1, spell-app 4), epic-engine (spell-epic 2).
  • epic worktree merge-main merges the kit first, then for each child repo replays the commits its branch has and its main lacks into the new folder (spell-ui's into ui/), one commit each, with their messages and authors.
  • Then the child worktree goes; its branch stays in the old clone, so nothing is lost.
  • A commit that won't apply stops the merge like any conflict: Claude resolves it, then --continue.
  • Cost: merge commits on a child branch (its own earlier merge-mains) aren't replayed, only their non-merge commits.
  • Each child branch's whole change against its main, as one commit in its folder, its message listing the original commits.
  • Simpler, and applies even where a replay would conflict commit by commit.
  • Cost: the branch's own history becomes one commit per repo.
  • No tool to write.
  • Cost: P20 waits on debut, elemental-spell and epic-engine finishing, or their child work is merged half-done.

Example: elemental-spell, after A: its kit branch gets main, then 7 replayed commits (2 under parser/, 1 under ui/, 4 under app/), and its child worktrees are gone.

  • an older worktree crosses over by its next epic worktree merge-main, with its commits
  • its child branches stay in the old clones until those are retired
