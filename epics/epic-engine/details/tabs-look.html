Page tabs: segmented or pills?

The Button page's tabs in the Fruity theme, as they are and as pills: a picture, so you can pick in the modal.

epic-engine, for debut's design re-check (its item I30). The designer review suggested separate pill tabs for the components pages' tabs (Examples / Usage / API / Theming). You asked: "need to see before/after, show me. What is impact?" Whether the page tabs and the row under them stay one joined, segmented box (your pick in r25), or become separate pills.
  • Before: today, on main's page server, in the Fruity theme. One grey pill track, the chosen tab a raised white key sliding along it, no lines between the tabs, in the rounded glass panel.
  • After: built for real in the epic-engine worktree, then put back.
    • Each tab is its own pill, with a 6px gap between them.
    • The tabs you're not on each get a faint fill of their own. The one you're on keeps Fruity's white key (a lighter glass in dark).
    • The glass panel round both rows stays. It's what hides the page scrolling under the tabs once they stick.
    • The row under the tabs (Types, Groups ...) follows the same idea.
  • All in the Fruity theme, the one you've been tuning. The Button page, at 1200px wide and at phone width (390px), in light and dark, at the top and scrolled so the tabs are stuck.

What draws the tabs in the pictures below. These pages are plain HTML: there's no .spellx source for them.

  • The markup doesn't change. Before and After are the same elements; only the CSS differs.
  • After adds one block of CSS: 10 lines, setting the segmented look's own settings.

  • It's CSS only. One block of about 10 lines in the site's own stylesheet, site.css: it sets the segmented look's own settings (gap, pill corners, no lines between, a faint fill on each tab).
  • No page needs touching. The 80 Spell UI components pages, the 10 brand pages and the page template keep their markup as it is.
  • Who else gets it: every page with these tabs, which is Spell UI's components pages and the brand's. Epic UI's and App UI's pages share the same site bundle, but they have no tabs, so nothing changes there.
  • Phone width: the four tabs still fit at 390px. Each one is a bit narrower, since the gaps take about 18px. The row under them still scrolls sideways, with its arrow.
  • The row under the tabs and the stuck tabs: nothing broke. The two rows still read as one panel, and the panel still sticks and hides the page under it.
    • Fruity draws no outline round the row under the tabs, so nothing is left over there. (Under the brand theme the row kept a thin outline from the toolbar's own sheet: one more line to drop it.)
  • A risk to watch: Fruity already sets these same settings (a pill track, pill tabs, no lines between). The block sets the gap, the corners and the unchosen tabs' fill in the site's stylesheet, which wins over any theme on these pages, so a theme can't give the page tabs another look. It leaves the chosen tab's key to the theme.

Pills. It's cheap, all in one stylesheet, and easy to undo.

  • The segmented box reads as one form control. Pills read as separate places to go, which is what these tabs are.
  • The row under the tabs stops looking like a second copy of the same box: both are now plain chips, told apart by size.
  • It takes Fruity's own pill track one step further: the pill shape the page header's icon group and the buttons already use.
  • The cost: each tab is a little narrower on a phone, and the glass panel now has some empty glass in the gaps.
