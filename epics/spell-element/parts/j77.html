The review controls are one family, <epic-review>, drawn three times, not one element holding all three pieces.

  • The three pieces, chosen by its shows attribute: the line's buttons, the note box, a marked note.
  • They sit in different places of their family's shadow root, which one element can't span:
    • the line
    • the end of the details, after Claude's status cards
    • the top of the details

How they work together:

  • Each <epic-review> keeps its own ReviewState, keyed by its of.
    • The family keeps its own one too: for its id chip, and to decide where the pieces show (ReviewState.noted(), new).
  • Revisit and Edit, in order:
    1. the element opens the box, in the inbox
    2. it sends epic-show-note; its family unfolds itself (an @E.on on its shadow root, for its own controls only)
    3. then it focuses the textarea of the note box beside it (<epic-review shows=note>)
  • Leaving the box (Escape, a button) is the element's own. For an item without details, under-line says it closes.
  • The texts moved to <epic-review>'s vocabulary.
    • <epic-item> keeps the buttons' NAMES (REVIEW_NAME_TEXTS), which its id chip's tooltip uses.
  • Its kind attribute is called buttons (item, todo or part).
    • A kind enum clashed with <epic-section kind>: the tags' shared data type became never, and the plan-doc tool's Markup.set() calls stopped compiling.
  • The controls look and behave as before.
    • The old 24 review tests pass, ported to step into the nested shadow roots.
    • check-review-epics.mjs passes too: its rootOf() now steps into them.
  • The docked note box's sticky ring is drawn on the box, through ::part(base).
