Chose inert on the top-level boxes of the shadow root (the flat tree carries it to what's slotted), over inert on the DOM element itself, because Q27 wants the element announced as disabled (aria-disabled), and an inert element leaves the accessibility tree.

  • the platform's inert has one more effect Q26 didn't spell out: inert content leaves the accessibility tree, so a screen reader doesn't read a disabled card's text either
  • the alternative, swallowing clicks and keys and taking each focusable out of the tab order, can't reach the shadow content of other elements inside
  • form controls keep their native disabled ("its own"), so they stay announced as disabled controls
  • families with a disabled of their own don't make their content inert (they keep what they did)
  • re-applied when the element first draws; NOT when a family swaps its top-level boxes later (none does today)
  • <ui-card disabled>'s buttons can't be clicked, focused or typed in, and are dimmed
  • its content is silent to assistive technology while disabled
