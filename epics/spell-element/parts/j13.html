When brand's this.attrs.x became this.x (One Attribute Store, P3), eight members of brand components had the same name as one of their attributes. The agent renamed the MEMBERS, so each attribute keeps its name and the member says what it is.

  • the eight renames:
    • font → shownFont
    • state → shownState
    • step → currentStep
    • the colour picker's value() → hex()
    • the composer's value() → text()
    • rows() → rowCount()
    • hint() → shownHint()
    • columns() → columnCount()
  • in packages/brand/components/: ui-brand-checklist, ui-brand-color, ui-brand-color-picker, ui-brand-color-set, ui-brand-composer and ui-brand-field
  • why: members are named for what they are (the "Member names" rule in UIComponent's doc, which packages/ui/AGENTS.md points to); this.step is the attribute, the member is the step being shown
  • attributes keep their names: no page markup changes
  • this.x is always the attribute; the member has its own, clearer name
  • ui's precedent: UIInput, UISelect and UIDropdown declare Omit<E.AttributeValues<...>, "value">, so their own value member wins
  • no renames, but this.font would not be the font attribute: easy to misread
  • eight brand members have new names; every attribute, and so every page, is unchanged
  • in brand, this.x always means the attribute x
