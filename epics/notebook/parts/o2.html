A notebook stops being a type of its own: it's a page with the notebook flag.

<spell-page "counter notebook" notebook>      <spell-notebook "counter notebook">
	# A counter                                  	# A counter
	...                                          	...
</spell-page>                                  </spell-notebook>
  • <spell-notebook> is only a spelling of <spell-page notebook> (Spanish: <encanto-cuaderno> and <encanto-página cuaderno>).
  • A page with no <draw> draws its body, flag or not: loose text drawn as markdown, a new block at each ##, its markup and examples where they sit.
    • A page WITH a <draw> keeps today's meaning: loose text a comment, the lines above a member its docstring.
    • The notebook flag then means one thing: the editor shows the page in place, editable (1.3).
  • Every page, flag or not, gets what makes sense for any page (Q1):
    • its own views inside it, written with the short word, <view counter> (or <view "one counter">), then drawn later in the page as <counter/>; lifted out to just before the page as it's read (where else it can be used: Q7)
    • locked
  • The core's Notebook class, its Notebook.spell template and Python's twin go; Page takes what they held.
  • The 360 notebook files in @spell/notebooks are rewritten to <spell-page "..." notebook> by a codemod; <spell-notebook> still reads, as the short spelling.
