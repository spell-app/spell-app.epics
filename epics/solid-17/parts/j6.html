You picked both (Q5): <ui-root> defining every catalog tag up front, and the page-wide setAttribute() patch with an off switch. Only the patch is built, with <ui-root pure> (Q8).

Why the early definitions stopped: "defined" means "its family has loaded" all through the code, and changing it reaches well past this epic.

  • About 30 files read it: RootLoader.undefinedTags() (how <ui-root> knows what to load), FormBinding's waiting, SlottedItems, ExampleSource, the site check and visual fixture, check-spell.js, spell-epic's pages, spell-app's runner and editor, and the :not(:defined) rules in native.css and utilities.css that hide an element until it's styled.
  • 25 family DOM classes (DOMNagElement, DOMTextBoxElement, DOMLoadableElement ...) carry script APIs. A tag defined early has none of them until its family loads, so their prototypes would have to be spliced in afterwards.
  • A tag's watched attributes and whether it's a form control are fixed when it's defined: the catalog generators (yarn gen:root, spell-pack) would have to write them, and hot reload (HotDefinitions) redefine around them.

What you have tonight:

  • The patch is on by default: on-ui-change={f} and tree={obj} work on every tag, loaded or not (tests: an element Solid draws before its tag is defined).
  • <ui-root pure> puts the browser's setAttribute() back; then a function or object given to a tag whose component hasn't loaded becomes text, as in plain Solid.

The patch covers every tag; pure is there for a page that wants the browser untouched and loads its components first.
