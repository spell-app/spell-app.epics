Found under I21, in Solitaire.spell, with a half-typed line (set t) in an if's body: laid out from the file's text alone (SP.BubblyLayout.of() with no project parse of that text), the lines ending control blocks (turn over the next card, pause for 500 msec, reset the stock) are 13-25px shorter than from the project's parse, 200ms later: their feet aren't drawn the same.

I21 no longer shows that layout while typing (it waits for the parse), but a slice or a file with no project parse still draws it.

Why the two differed: laid out from its text alone, a file has no idea what its other files declare. So a line naming one (if the stock is empty, for each card in the deck, after moving a card to a pile, a card) didn't parse at all. A line that doesn't parse was drawn as a plain step, so its block, its band and its foot were gone, and every line after it moved up.

Fixed in the parser's layout:

  • A line with lines under it that doesn't parse now takes its shape from its first word: if, for, repeat, otherwise start only control lines, so it's a control block; after, when, on start only actions, so it's an action.
  • The words come from the language's own rules (each statement rule's first word), so Spanish's si, para, cuando work the same.
  • A word that starts steps too (to, the, a) is left a step.
  • But a type's name alone, a card, is a thing's head: it reopens a type declared elsewhere.
  • And it "can go up" if ... (a pronoun, then a quoted name) is a property's head, as the two at the top of Solitaire.spell now are.
