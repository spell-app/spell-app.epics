Fifteen places write a local date or time stamp by hand, and three of them use the UTC date: a stamp written in the evening lands on tomorrow's date.

  • Probable bug: the design tool, the request writer and the voice tool take the date as toISOString().slice(0, 10).
    • That's the UTC date: in California after 5 pm (4 pm in winter), it's already tomorrow.
    • The review backfill tool documents exactly this trap, beside the code that avoids it, so it's known, just not followed.
  • Two homes for one job already: spell-epic's PlanTime (a day, a minute, a clock time) and PlanDates (a clock time).
  • The other copies each pad numbers with zeros themselves; the kit's command runs write their own too.

Only the format: a day, a minute, with the time-zone offset, or compact.

A local-time module in the kit's spell/util, since the kit's command runs need it too; PlanTime and PlanDates become its callers.

  • the three UTC stamps write the local date
  • the kit's spell/util gains a local-time module
  • PlanTime, PlanDates and the 15 copies call it

About 120 lines, 15 copies; and the evening stamps get the right day.
