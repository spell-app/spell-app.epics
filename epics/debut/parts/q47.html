How Spell writes clicks, drags and drops, in the outline (.spell) and in tags (.spellml): Owen settled it on the details page by typing over its examples, 2026-10-29. It builds on Q42 (when for conditions), Q43 (an on drag runs as the drag starts) and Q44 (an event implies its ability).

  1. Short forms beside the long ones: "it is" and the colon may be left out.
    • - clickable when it is face down = - it is clickable when: it is face down
    • - draggable when its pile lets it go
    • - lets go when the card is face up (a pile's)
  2. A one-line event needs no colon: - on click play it.
  3. Optional words: articles and "is" may be left out anywhere. The docs show them in brackets: - while (a) card (is) dragged over:.
  4. Shorter event names, "on it" understood:
    • on card dropped (was "a card dropped on it")
    • on card dragged out (was "a card dragged out of it")
    • on a card dragged over
  5. Tags take bare words, no quotes: a condition or an event name is plain words inside the tag.
    • <clickable when it is face down />
    • <droppable when the card is one lower ... />
    • <lets go when the card is face up />
    • <when its score changes>...</when>
  6. A one-line event in a tag closes itself: <on click play it />. Longer code goes between tags.
    • Code on new lines inside a self-closing tag is NOT allowed (one of Owen's boxes tried it, for on drag): it goes between an opening and a closing tag instead.
  7. Three closing tags, all the same: </>, </on> and </on click>.
    • </> is the one to prefer; the explicit forms where they help a reader find the start.
  8. The colon is optional, in the outline and in a tag: <while card dragged over: ... /> or the same without it.
  9. on and when mean the same for an event, since English reads better one way or the other: - on card dropped: = - when a card is dropped on it:.
    • An event's on / when STARTS the line (or the tag); a condition's when FOLLOWS an ability or a list's rule (clickable when ...). Position tells them apart.
    • This answers the details page's second question (on or when for a pile's drops): both.
  10. A new event, drag cancelled: - on drag cancelled: (or when drag is cancelled) runs on the dragged thing when its drag ends with no drop taken.
    • Dropped where nothing takes it, or Escape pressed.
    • The thing goes back where it came from, as today; the event runs then.
  11. "lets go" stays the pile's say over which of its cards may be picked up at all (today's can give up). It is not "drag cancelled".
  12. Still open: one tag per ability? In every example Owen wrote, an ability is a tag of its own (<clickable ... />), its events separate tags beside it, not nested inside it. The details page's first question (one tag per ability, holding its events) is NOT decided by this.
