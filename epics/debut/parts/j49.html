Chose a short program per guide that rebuilds the heart of its example, many of its lines copied as they are, over a guide that imports the example and talks about code in another file, because Q18 asks for the code inside the guide, and a reader can only change what's in front of them.

  • e.g. the Calculator's guide is an adding machine with 1, 2, 5, + and C; Tic-Tac-Toe's checks only the rows, and adding the columns is the reader's move.
  • Each ends by pointing at the full example.
  • No copied lines to drift, but the code being explained isn't on the page, and importing an example runs its own start.
  • Ten how-it-works guides, 60 to 110 lines each, every one compiling, running and passing its own check.
  • A change to an example doesn't reach its guide: the guides are tested on their own (their snapshots in tests/spell/__guides__/).
