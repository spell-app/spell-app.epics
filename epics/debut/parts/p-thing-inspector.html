  • Its computed values (Value, Color, Name, Picture, is a face card) don't show at all.

Owen, 2026-10-25, with a screenshot of the Inspector showing Card · king of hearts (Id, Direction, Rank, Suit, one flat list):

"For card inspector: would be cool to divide by group, then order in the file. Getters show up as non-editable fields which show the value. Acitons show up with lightning symbol and play button."

  • A thing's fields are grouped under its file's headings, each a band that folds, as an element's groups are (<ui-property-group>), in the order the file says them.
  • Computed values are fields you can't edit, showing their value, live as the program changes it.
  • Actions are rows with a lightning icon and a play button that does it; the app redraws by itself.
  • The compiler records which heading each member is under, and which members are actions, in the project's words file; what's stored, what's computed and how to do an action, the runtime knows already.
  1. What the compiler records: for each member of a type, the heading it's under in the type's outline (# card rank, ## turning the card over), and its kind.
    • Today a heading inside a type becomes only a comment in the compiled code: nothing at run time knows it.
    • The declarations note each member's heading; the words file (Solitaire.en.js) gains each type's headings, in the file's order, with the members under each.
    • It also says which members are actions (to turn it over:) and which are questions (it "is a (rank)"): the runtime can't tell is a (rank) from put a card on a pile, both one argument.
    • Why the words file: every runner loads it already (the editor's programWords), its members are already in the file's order, and a heading is words in the file's language (a Spanish file's in Spanish).
    • (coherence) A template's - group "direction" line (DefineGroup) is the same idea as a heading: both are recorded the same way, so the Inspector reads one shape.
  2. What the runtime already knows, nothing new to compile (ThingRegistry):
    • stored or computed: a getter with no setter is computed (propertiesOf())
    • its actions, and how many arguments each takes (actionsOf())
    • doing one (perform()), as the Things view's ▶ does
    • One change: perform() hands back the action's wait, so ▶ shows it's busy while turn it face up pauses.
  3. One list of a thing's members (coherence): a helper beside the Inspector puts the runtime's members and the words file's headings together: each band's members in order, each with its kind (stored, computed, a thing, an action) and its words.
    • The Things view's details read the same list, keeping their look, instead of a second walk of the classes.
  4. In the Inspector: a <ui-property-group> per heading, folding and remembered as an element's groups are.
    • Its title: the heading up to a colon; the rest is its description ("drawing", then "its picture face up ...").
    • Stored: editable, as today; a thing: a link to it (P15).
    • Computed: read only (<ui-property readonly>), its value read live, following what it reads as the stored ones do; a yes or no as a read-only check; one that fails shows its error, dimmed.
    • Action: a row with a lightning icon (bolt, as the Events card's), its words, then a play button.
      • Busy while it waits; an error goes to the Console, as the program's own would.
      • The app redraws by itself: the action changes the card, and its drawing follows.
    • Inherited members: under their own type's headings, after the type's; one it overrides shows once, in the type's place (a Joker's Color). The built-in Id last, in a folded band.
    • No words file, or no headings: one band named for the type, its members by name, as the Things view shows them.
  5. The example: Card.spell's its "picture" sits under ## turning the card over as written; its block moves under ## drawing, so the bands read right.
  6. Checked:
    • the words file: Card's headings and members, in order (the examples' and guides' snapshots re-blessed)
    • the members list: kinds, order, a Joker's own first then Card's, each once
    • the Inspector, in a browser test: a Solitaire card's bands, its read-only values, ▶ on turn it over turning it and its Picture changing
    • yarn review in spell-parser and spell-app; the parser's speed test on this branch and on main
  7. Left out:
    • Actions that take arguments (put a card on a pile): listed, dimmed, with no ▶. Asking for them (a pile picked in the app, a value typed) is for later.
    • Questions (is a (rank)) and drawing (to draw it): not shown.
    • Headings inside headings: every band is flat, a # and a ## alike.
  • what the compiler records: SpellDeclarations, the words file's SpellWords, a type's outline (TypeDeclaration, DefineGroup)
  • the runtime: ThingRegistry (perform() hands back its wait)
  • the Inspector: ThingFields, ClassSchemas, the Things view's ThingExplorer, the texts AppTexts.en.ts and AppTexts.es.ts
  • the example: Card.spell
  • tests: Inspector.browser.test.tsx, the examples' and guides' words snapshots (Solitaire.en.snapshot.js ...)
