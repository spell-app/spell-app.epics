Five examples, two of them card games.

  • Tic-Tac-Toe: a game on a grid, its rules written as sentences.
  • Bill Splitter: a form with live sums: people, amounts, tip, who owes what.
  • Flash Cards: a deck to quiz yourself on, with a score.
  • Habit Tracker: a week of checkboxes per habit, with streaks.
  • A word Spell doesn't have yet becomes an issue; the example works around it, or waits.
  • Each has a title, so the chooser shows a proper name.
  • Tic-Tac-Toe, Bill Splitter, Flash Cards and Habit Tracker (Q3).
  • Each checked against the language first.
  • Four new examples, each with a title, its own stylesheet and a test: Tic-Tac-Toe (its win written as eight sentences), Bill Splitter (a form of diners, a tip, and sums that follow every keystroke, in dollars and cents), Flash Cards (twelve Spanish words, turned over on a click, with a score) and Habit Tracker (a week of checkboxes per habit, its days and its streak).
  • All four are in the examples' compile test, with the Calculator: their JavaScript, TypeScript, words and printed output.
  • A Spell fix the examples needed: a phrase on a type of several words (tic tac toe) now belongs to its class.
  • README-examples: ten apps in its table, with pictures, and a short section on the four and the edges each one hit; the front page counts ten.
