Clicks and drags as events

The whole scheme for what people do to things -- clicking, dragging, dropping -- and what a program notices, in both the outline (.spell) and tags (.spellml), for you to comment on section by section.

session debut, while planning P27 (clicks and drags as events) 2026-10-29
debut -- Spell's public preview: the examples polished, the editor's Inspector showing a thing's events and actions. You picked: a drag's event runs as it starts (Q43 B); an event implies its ability (Q44 A); when: for conditions (Q42). You asked for the full scheme on one page, both forms, and floated a rule: on = done to this thing, when = something elsewhere I notice. How every example and guide says clicks, drags and drops once P27 is built. Comment on any section; the two questions at the bottom are the open calls.
  • on …: a person does something TO this thing -- clicks it, drags it, drops something on it.
  • when …: something happens ELSEWHERE, and this thing notices -- the score changes, a game is won.
  • An ability: what people may do to it -- clickable, draggable, selectable, droppable.
    • Writing an on event implies its ability (Q44 A): on click makes it clickable.
    • The ability's own line only adds a condition: it is draggable when: … (Q42). No condition: always.
  • while … stays as it is: set something for as long as it lasts, put back after.

You asked for the cluster as ONE tag. How does a card write an ability's condition and its events together in spellml?

One tag per ability: its condition an attribute, its events inside. The tables above show the parts on their own; this joins them.

<draggable when="its pile lets it go">
	<on drag>set the hint of the solitaire to ""</on>
</draggable>

<clickable when="it is face down">
	<on click>play it</on>
</clickable>

<droppable when="the card is one lower ...">
	<on card dropped>put the card on it</on>
</droppable>

No ability tag: the event carries the condition. Shortest, when an ability has one event.

<on click when="it is face down">play it</on>
<on drag when="its pile lets it go">...</on>

B for one event, A when an ability has several; two ways to write one thing.

Your note said "on drag into a pile should be when dragged". Is a card dropped on a pile done TO the pile (on), or something the pile notices (when)?

The person drops it on the pile's element: - on a card dropped on it:. The rule stays one sentence: a person does it here.

The card does it, the pile notices: - when a card is dropped on it:, as today. on then means only the thing being clicked or dragged.
