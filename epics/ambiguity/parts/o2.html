Before any page landed, a review asked what in the repos already does part of this. A lot does: the plan should grow these, not write new ones.

  • Every double meaning ends in one place. When several rules match the same words, the parser keeps one (the higher priority, then the longest, then whichever was registered first) and throws the others away. Nothing tells anyone a second reading existed.
    • A "collect every reading" mode beside the one the editor already uses for completions would find them, at no cost to normal parsing.
  • "Your own words win" is true only sometimes. An alias you write always wins; but your to draw a card loses to a built-in Spell prefers. So a new built-in can take over one of your actions today: your upgrade worry, already real.
  • A meaning check exists, for translations. It compiles one line, takes the names out, and compares, so a Spanish line that runs but means something else is caught. The same check could compare reading A with reading B, or old Spell with new.
  • "Which lines might read differently now" exists too, for the editor's quick re-parsing, and a test recompiles every example when a grammar word is dropped. Together, a check run when a library is added or Spell is upgraded.
  • Explain This already explains lines with no agent, from the editor's plain hovers, with spell explain for single words. The gap: most lines have no plain words yet.
  • agents-in-spell already plans one Spell guide, which makes the agents' files and a feature index that "how do I" help would read. This epic should agree with it, not add a second format.
  • The language epic's "Upgrading Old Spell" plans a project's Spell version and a spell upgrade rewriter, for wordings Spell REMOVES. A new reading that captures your words isn't one of those: this epic would add it, on that version record.

Three words already mean two things in Spell's own messages, the card worry in our own text:

  • Spell UI's pieces are a "component", an "element" or a "widget", depending on the message.
  • The hover says "template"; the errors say "type".
  • A to ... line is an "action" to people, a "phrase" in the translation dictionary, a "`to` rule" in your words; and "alias" names four different things.
