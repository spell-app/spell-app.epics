An epic page is the live record of one epic: what it's for, its phases, every open question, and what was built.

  • One per epic. /epic <name> makes it, at epics/<name>/<name>.plan.html, and keeps it current while the epic is planned and built.
  • Shared: every checkout (main and each worktree) sees the same file, through the epics/ link into the shared content repo. An edit shows everywhere at once.
  • The record: nothing is deleted. A closed item stays, closed; text Claude rewrites moves into the item's Original Discussion.
  • Read and review it in VS Code's right side bar, the Review tab (the checkbox icon). The page server serves it, so it updates in place as Claude edits it.
  • Claude edits it through epic ... commands (section 8), so ids, colours and counts stay right.
  • The airplane epic page has every part this guide describes.

An epic page has a header, a toolbar under it, then fixed sections, always in this order.

Part Its link What it holds
Header the title, where the epic lives, the current phase, Send and Review Now
Toolbar new one button per section, with badges for what waits on you
1. Overview #overview the summary, the kickoff prompt, then the design in sub-sections (O1 ...)
2. Phases #phases a progress bar, then one block per phase (P1 ...)
3. Questions #decisions what Claude asks you; an answered question is a decision
4. Judgement calls #judgements choices Claude made without you, open until you review them
5. Caveats #caveats limits and risks we accept
6. Todos #todos later work that isn't a caveat or an issue
7. Issues #issues problems found, open until fixed
8. To test #tests what you check by hand before merging: a step, and what should happen
9. Log #log one line per change to the plan, with its date and time

The header sticks to the top of the window, and draws itself from the doc's data: none of it is written by hand.

  • The crumbs (Docs › Epics › the title), and the title.
  • The meta lines: the branch, the worktree, when it started and was last updated, and the guide once the epic has one.
  • The step label: the active phase (P8 · Review Polish), else DONE once every phase is, else Next: the next phase, or FUTURE for an epic not planned yet.
  • The git toggle: shows or hides each phase's commits.
  • On the right, while the doc is being reviewed: (a new todo or question), Send, and Review Now (section 5).
  • The review line, which says what happens to your marks:
    • "To review this doc, type /epic review <name>", with a copy button: nobody is listening yet
    • "No Claude session is reviewing this doc", in orange: marks are waiting and nobody is listening
    • "Airplane mode: what you mark here waits for /airplane land": in airplane mode (section 7), with no warning
  • "Agents running": a panel under the header while any agent of the epic runs, a note box per agent to redirect it.
  • "Plan hung?": a folded notice while the epic has no phase yet, with how to restart it.

new A row of buttons across the bottom of the sticky header, one per section: it replaces the rail of icons on the window's right edge.

  • Each button: the section's icon and its name. A click lands on that section.
  • The section you're reading is a deeper ivory, and follows you as you scroll.
  • Badges: how many of the section's items wait on you.
    • 6 red: urgent items (state attention)
    • 2 orange: items Claude answered with options, waiting for your pick (state replied)
    • both kinds: both badges, red first; nothing waiting: no badge
  • In a narrow window (the side bar, a phone): first tighter, then icons and badges only, the current button keeping its name; the row scrolls sideways if it must.
  • Every section title, and an open item's line, sticks just under it.

Other docs pages, like this one, keep the rail on the right edge.

The Overview says what the epic is for, in three parts.

  • The summary: two sentences, drawn as the lede. It takes review marks, like a phase (section 5).
  • The kickoff prompt: what you typed to start the epic, folded, word for word.
  • The sub-sections, O1, O2 ...: the design, in prose. Each takes review marks too.
  • The total estimate: every phase's estimate added up, and what's left.

The chip's colour is the item's state. Claude's tool works it out from the item's status and your marks, on every edit; the first row that applies wins.

Colour State Means Live
blue progress Claude is working on it (an Underway card, or the spinner) I1
grey old canceled: made moot, struck through T6
green recent decided or done, however long ago; or settled by your review (approved, made a todo) Q8, J2
orange replied Claude answered with options, and nothing is picked yet: your turn Q5
red attention needs you: an open question; a judgement call or issue you haven't reviewed Q3, J7
yellow open open, not urgent: todos, caveats, tests; a reviewed issue or judgement call C2, I3
  • Red and orange are what waits on you: they're what the toolbar's badges count.
  • A judgement call or issue that simply follows the house style starts yellow ("calm"), not red. Click its chip while reviewing to flip it: urgent (red) or not (yellow).
  • While your mark on an item is live, its chip takes the colour and fill of the button you pressed (section 5): a dashed green chip is an Approve not sent yet. Once Claude has handled it, the chip shows the result, solid.

Under the colour, an item has one of four statuses:

  • open: still to settle.
  • decided: an answered question, a decision in force.
  • done: closed (fixed, passed, accepted). It stays on the page, not struck.
  • canceled: made moot by another decision. The one status drawn struck through.

The review label at the end of the line says the latest review step: "to do", "deferred", or "reviewed 10-03".

When Claude asks you to choose, the options sit side by side as cards, A, B ..., one marked recommended with a violet thumbs-up.

  • On any item: a question's own options, or the ones a judgement call or a reply weighs.
  • While you review, each card has a Choose pill. Picking one answers a question with it, or approves any other item with it.
  • Picked and applied: that card is green, its pill solid. Q8 above shows it (open its Choices).
  • An answered question can be revisited: its other cards take Choose pills again while its Revisit box is open, so "B instead, because ..." is one mark.
  • new A picked option keeps its Chosen marker through Send and after Claude applies it, so you can see what you've addressed.

An unfolded item reads in the order things happened. Every boxed card folds from its heading band.

  • Question: a question's text as first asked.
  • Original reply: the item's text, and its option cards.
  • Net effect: what concretely changes, one bullet each. Every question, issue and judgement call ends with one.
  • Answer: once a question is decided, in your colour (ivory).
  • More Details: what Claude added when you asked for more (Do Now with no note). It goes under the text, never over it.
  • Replies: your notes (ivory) and Claude's answers (violet), dated, each quoting what it answers.
  • Original Discussion: folded; any text Claude rewrote, as it was. Nothing is ever lost.
  • Commits: the commits that fixed it.
  • Status cards: last, under your note (below).

Revisit opens a box at the end of the item, in your colour (ivory), for what you want to say.

  • It saves as you type ("Saved 14:42"), so a reload keeps it. A draft is never sent by itself.
  • Revisit Later (blue): goes with the next Send, answered in the item.
  • Do Now (the wand): answered at once.
  • new Skip This (an x, grey): "nothing to do here", with your note, if any, kept as your reply. It replaces the box's Make Todo; the line keeps its own.
  • On a todo, the box matches its line: the plane, Revisit Later, the x.
  • Once sent, your note shows as a small bubble on the line, and later as your reply in the item.

/epic review <name> starts a session that waits on the doc's inbox and wakes when you send.

  1. It says it's listening (the review line changes) and waits in the background.
  2. Do Now and Review Now: a blue Underway card on each item at once, then a background agent answers into it.
  3. Send: approvals, picks, todos and new items are applied; each revisit is answered in its item, one at a time.
  4. Each answer done: the card turns green, your buttons clear, the chip shows the result.
  5. Then it waits again, until you say "stop reviewing".
  • It answers, never decides for you: when you must choose, the answer ends in option cards, and the item turns orange.
  • If it dies (a closed tab), the page notices within 90 seconds and says nobody is reviewing again.

new Choose what to do with an item, and it folds, its line staying where it is: the next item is right under it.

  • Folds after: Approve, Make Todo, Do Now, a todo's plane or x, any note box button, a Choose pick.
  • Not after: Revisit (it opens the box), typing, a mark cleared, a Do Now called off, an un-pick.
  • Only items fold: a phase, the summary and an Overview sub-section stay open.

Airplane mode lets you work on epic pages and guides with no internet and no Claude: everything saves on the laptop, and Claude takes it when you land.

  • Before the flight, in the main checkout: /airplane. It checks the laptop is ready (page server, offline pages, the extension), asks which running sessions to park, and turns airplane mode on.
  • On the plane: open an epic page from the Review tab's list button ("Review: Open Epic..."), any other page from "Review: Home Page".
  • Mark items as usual: Send isn't needed, since every mark is taken at landing, sent or not.
  • Do Now waits, dashed.
  • After: /airplane land. Claude gathers everything you left, applies the marks, answers the notes in the background, then starts /epic review on the epic with most waiting.
  • Text you typed and never submitted is asked about, never acted on.
  • Pages blank or unstyled: the Review tab's "Restart Page Server" button.
  • Wi-Fi that asks you to pay first makes requests hang: turn Wi-Fi fully off.
  • The Epics page's New epic pill (a seedling): a title, and what it's for.
  • It's written down at once as a future epic: a seedling card on the Epics page.
  • At landing, Claude asks which new epics to start, with checkboxes; each picked one opens in its own window.
  • On any page, epic pages too: the bullhorn beside a block (sections, tables, asides, code, items), or select text and press ⌘ I.
  • A docs page's comments wait in its own inbox file. Its header has a Send, as an epic page's: they go to the epic the page belongs to (its guide's epic, else an open epic whose See Also lists it), whose epic inbox wait <name> wakes with them. A page no epic owns: they go to the standing epic feedback the same way, its Send waking that epic's session, which gathers them one phase per page (epic epic-engine T9; /airplane land sweeps any never sent).
  • An epic page's wait in the epic's inbox; Claude answers them into the doc, as a revisit's note.
  • They replace the old page notes (the bubble and the Note pill); notes already written still show, read only.
