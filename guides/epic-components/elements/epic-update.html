<epic-update>

An update: a block titled Update: <summary>, its date at the right, what changed under it. A phase's plan change sits at the end of its phase's description; an update to prose, right after what it changed. Empty, it's the small orange UPDATE label on an item changed while a phase is active.

For anyone reading or writing a plan doc's markup, or changing this element.
Its vocabulary (every name it uses): EpicUpdate.en.ts.
Its component: EpicUpdate.tsx, with its style sheet and tests beside it.
Since 2026-10-11 (epic epic-workflow), it also replaces <epic-updated> and the Plan changes box.

First, the label in an item's title (hover it: "Changed during P6"). Then a plan change, as the end of a phase's description holds it, its text made up for this page: its title row folds it.

Its parents:

  • a plan change: <epic-phase>, at the END of its description (Owen, 2026-10-11): after Goal and Owen's kept notes, before Done, the commits, Files, Verify and To review
  • an update to prose: right AFTER the block it changed, anywhere prose goes. It's a prose element:
    • <epic-item>: among an item's own text, before its cards
    • <epic-field>: a phase's field, or a labelled block
    • <epic-section>: an Overview sub-section's prose (an update to the whole sub-section goes last inside it)
    • <epic-answer>, <epic-reply>, <epic-option>: the cards
  • a bare label: an item's title (the <span slot="title"> of an <epic-item>), or inline in any prose

Its children:

  • prose: what changed, and why
  • none: a bare label (or a block with only its summary)

A note that isn't an update (an item partly fixed elsewhere, a decision done) is an <epic-note> instead.

A slot is where the element's own children show.

Slot Holds
(default) What changed, and why; none for a bare label.

A part is a piece of the drawing a page's CSS can reach.

Part Is
base The block, or the bare label.
header A block's title row: the fold chevron, the title, the date.
toggle A block's fold chevron, a <button>, first.
title A block's Update: <summary>.
time A block's date, at the right of its title row.
body What changed; hidden (until-found) while folded.
label The bare orange UPDATE label.

A state is set by the element on itself; none is ever written in the doc.

State When
block It has a summary, a date or children: drawn as a block, not a bare label.
open A block, unfolded. It starts so.
  • Orange: changed since you looked. On every doc page, orange means only that, and warnings (plan-doc.md, "Colours").
  • A block: a faint orange ground and an orange hairline, rounded.
  • Its title row: the chevron, then Update: <summary>, free to wrap; the date pinned to the right of its first line, as a comment thread's message shows its time (smaller, tabular figures, muted). The date's tooltip: the time as written, and the phase under way then.
  • It folds by its title row and starts open. Folded, what changed is hidden="until-found", so find-in-page reveals it.
  • Owen comments on it with its own bullhorn (a block kind of its own, update), even inside an item or a field; the bullhorn moves left of the date.
  • The bare label: solid orange, small bold capitals, centred on the text's middle.
  • Restyled in one place, its own family (components/epic-update/): --epic-update-accent and --epic-update-background, or its parts.
  • A plan change: spell dev plan-doc updated <name> <N> "<html>" [--summary "..."], at the end of phase N's description (PlanDoc.addPhaseUpdate()). The phase's changes counts them, for the folded title's pen.
  • An update to prose: Claude writes it by hand, right after the block it changed.
  • A bare label: the tool adds one to an item's title when a command changes the item while a phase is active, once per item (PlanDoc.markUpdate()).
  • spell dev plan-doc phase <name> <N> done removes phase N's bare labels and its prose updates; its plan changes stay.
  • add-phase ... --before N renumbers phase with the phases.
  • Older docs are migrated on the tool's next write, by the step updates-as-epic-update (Migrations, in the tool's migrations/):
    • a phase's <epic-updated> lines become blocks at the end of its description, and their copies in the Phases section go
    • an UPDATE note, which stood BEFORE the prose it was about, moves after it, dated and summed up
