The Recipe Box

Recipes on a real local server. Every change shows at once and is saved in the background, and nothing on the page knows which kind of server it's talking to.

To see them all at once, showing that one set of recipes can have several ways in: every back-end, side by side.

  1. Start the servers, at the kit's root, and leave them running: 
    yarn tsx scripts/recipe-servers.ts
    REST and GraphQL keep their recipes in one CSV file, and the folder and the room in one folder. They print where both are, and both stay between runs.
  2. Add a recipe, or change one. "saving…" then "saved" shows at the top.
  3. For each person's own account: open this address, a fake of AT Protocol's network the servers run, with two people on two hosts. Sign in as alice.test, and in a second window as bob.test. Each sees everyone's recipes, what either adds shows in the other at once, and changing the other's is refused. Your own real account: this page, where everything saved is public.
  4. Open this page in a second window. What you change in one shows in the other within 5 seconds.
  5. For a clash, open this page in a second window, and change one recipe's title in both within 5 seconds: the second one's is kept, and a notice holds what the first wrote, with Use theirs. Change its notes in the other window instead, and both stand with no notice. Or edit the row in the CSV file: it's the same clash.
  6. Switch to GraphQL: change back-end="rest" to back-end="graphql" in this page, or open this address. It's the same recipes, in the same CSV file. GraphQL here has no versions, so a clash has no notice: the last save wins that field.
  7. Switch to a folder: change back-end="rest" to back-end="folder" in this page, or open this address. The servers print where the folder is: a list file, and one file per recipe. Open recipes/r-1.json in VS Code, change its title, and save: the page follows within 5 seconds. A change on the page is saved in that file. A file that isn't JSON yet is left alone until it is.
  8. Switch to a CSV file: change back-end="rest" to back-end="sheet" in this page, or open this address. It needs no server: the file, recipes.csv, is beside this page. It's read only, so there's nothing to type in or press. Change a line of the file in VS Code and save: the page follows within 5 seconds. Give two rows one id, or blank one, and it says which rows it left out.
  9. The same file with no id column named: this address. The rows still show, and it says they share no identity.
  10. Use a sample: press Use a sample, on the read-only CSV file or any other. The same recipes come up, and now you can change them, add to them and remove them. None of it reaches the real place, and recipes.csv doesn't change. Use the real place brings the real ones back, and Use a sample brings your changes back again, even after a reload: they're kept in this browser. It's what the editor's side bar will offer. With the servers stopped, it's how to see a place that can't be reached: on REST, with no servers running, a sample starts empty, as this page names no type to make records from.
  11. Your own Google Sheet, read only, with no account: share it with "anyone with the link", give it columns id, title, servings and notes, then add &sheet= and its address to this one, written as https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/<its id>/export?format=csv&gid=0.
  12. To write to it too, it goes through the servers, which hold the key: the epic page's manual test has every step (this address, once they're started with it). There are no versions in a Google Sheet, so the last save wins each cell.
  13. Airtable: this address, through the servers, which hold its key. It's a stub of Airtable until they're started with your own base: the epic page's manual test has every step. The last save wins each field.
  14. A room: open this address in two windows, side by side. What one window does shows in the other at once, with nothing polling. Change one recipe's title in both at the same moment: each may flicker, then both show one of them. Paste a long sentence into its notes in both: the one that lost keeps its words in a notice, with Use mine. Press Remove in one while typing in the same recipe in the other: it goes, with Restore. Press Up or Down in both, and One more serving in both: nothing doubles, and both additions count. Go offline, change something, and come back. A room trusts everyone in it: there are no per-person rules yet.
  15. Stop the servers and make a change. It stays on the page, says it's trying again, and goes through once they're back, even after a reload.
