Spell and AT Protocol Spaces

Communities will run themselves in Spell, in spaces they can reshape. AT Protocol will keep their data and publish what they choose, so Spell doesn't have to reinvent hosting.

Written for: anyone working on or reviewing the epic atmosphere Status: draft the plan as it stands; nothing is built Researched on: 2026-11-01, by reading the specs. Nothing here has been run (see Sources)

Role in a space Can
Member Read anything public in the org, make new posts and comments, and volunteer for an event. Members can edit or delete their own contributions.
Lead Read and modify anything in the private space. Make new pages and install apps in either space, change existing ones, delete but not edit anyone's posts and comments, manage who's in the space, make any current Member a Lead, and remove other Leads

Spell Lexicon What to do about it
a recipe is a thing A record, named like spaces.spell.x.recipe A name belongs to whoever controls its domain, and spell.spaces is ours
Text, date, list, one of String, datetime, array, known values Mostly direct
A decimal number None: floats are banned Store it as a string or a scaled integer
A thing inside a thing, saved once, then referenced A nested object, or an at:// link Each record is capped at 1 MiB, and whole-then-reference has no direct equivalent
Rename a field, upgrade on read Not allowed once published: new fields must be optional, with no renames or type changes Field names freeze only on publishing, and a breaking change needs a new name
What it is Where it lives Its versions
A file: Spell code, a document, a picture A blob, and one record with its name, type and size, pointing to that blob Git
The file you're editing One draft, saved over and over None: it's replaced each time
The rows an app saves One record per thing, with a schema generated from the app's declarations None: sign-ups and posts just change
An app many orgs use Published in public records, with a record in each org's space pointing to one version Git, then a published version
Setting up an org On an existing host On the org's own host
For Most orgs Privacy-focused orgs
What it asks Dead easy, with no knowledge of AT Protocol Simple, but it may take some knowledge of AT Protocol
What Spell does Makes the identity, the handle and the spaces The same, once the org's server is up
Who can read everything That host, and whoever runs Spell's servers The org, if it runs Spell's servers too
Provider What it offers Price Fit
eny.space Managed hosting in beta, with invite codes, updates and backups. Custom domains, unlimited accounts and a service agreement are "coming soon" €9 a month for one account, €24 for a community with 20 GB, ask for an organization The closest: its organization plan is what spell.spaces needs
Eurosky A non-profit running accounts and the services behind them, with handles on its own domains None found Good for an account today, and a possible partner
ElfHosted A managed host on its own subdomain $9 a month For one person's host, not handing out accounts
Northsky, Blacksky Community hosts people can join Not checked Somewhere to send people, not something to resell
Apple's rule What it means for Spell
More than "a repackaged website" Real alerts, sign-in and a list of your orgs, not just a wrapper round the pages
User-generated content needs filtering, reporting, blocking and published contact details These have to exist before the first submission. Leads answer the reports, and the contact details are Spell's, as the app's publisher
HTML5 and JavaScript mini apps are allowed, and you're responsible for them Spell programs can run in it, but people's programs become Spell's problem with Apple
Template apps are rejected, unless the content's owner submits them, or it's one app with a picker One Spell app where you pick your org
