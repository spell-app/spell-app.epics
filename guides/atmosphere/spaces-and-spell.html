Spell and AT Protocol Spaces

If social is Spell's killer app, a Spell group space should be an AT Protocol Space. This page says how the two line up, and what that would cost.

Written for: Owen, to comment on, before the epic's Overview is rewritten Status: draft nothing is decided or built Researched on: 2026-11-01, by reading the specs directly. Nothing here has been run (see Sources)
  • Your social idea fits Spaces far better than the cookbook did: a forum is what Spaces was designed for.
  • The schemas can be unified by generating Lexicon, AT Protocol's schema language, from Spell's declarations.
  • The word "space" sits one level differently: owen@spell is closer to an AT Protocol account, and a group is closer to a Space.
  • The biggest loss is shared rows: every record belongs to its author, so spreadsheets and docs need a merge of Spell's own.
  • A new risk comes with your platform idea: a stranger's program must not get everything you granted to Spell.
  • Align now, without depending on it: make a group space a Space, and keep a local host shaped the same.
  • This reverses what the epic page says today, in its questions Q1 and Q3.

"Imagine Facebook, but for your communit(ies), without ads, and where you can control the UI and the feed algorithm. Want to see more or less of one person or group's stuff? Love their art posts but hate their politics? Just change your feed algorithm. Want to add pages (spreadhseets, docs, sign-ups, organizer-specific pages) for an event you have coming up? Just add them to the group's space."

"I don't want to have to re-think everytying they have already figured out in terms of how to host/federate/etc. Our 'spaces' being fundamentally different than theirs would be a huge loose."

Their stated aims and your idea match closely.

  • Hosting and apps: hosting is generic and boring, and each app owns its own product, so Spell can leave hosting to them.
  • Control: the ethos post lists "skepticism of centralized control of any aspect of the user's experience" among its roots. That's your feed idea.
  • Spaces: Holmgren calls a space a "shared social context" and "mini atprotos". A 100k-person forum is his design case, and a community is that, smaller.
  • Pages in a space: a space can hold records of any type, so sign-ups, sheets and docs can all sit in it.
  • What Spell adds: they want the same data remixed across apps, but building one still takes a developer. Spell lets anyone write one.

Yes, by generating Lexicon, not by replacing either side. Spell's declaration stays the only thing anyone writes.

  • Spell already generates JSON Schema from a declaration this way, so Lexicon is one more output.
  • Importing other people's Lexicons as Spell types is what makes remixing work.
Spell Lexicon Problem
a recipe is a thing A record, named like spaces.spell.x.recipe A name's owner is whoever controls its domain, so you own this one now you've bought it
Text, date, list, one of String, datetime, array, known values Mostly direct
A decimal number None Floats are banned, so store them as strings or scaled integers
A thing inside a thing, saved once, then referenced A nested object, or an at:// link Each record is capped at 1 MiB, and whole-then-reference has no direct equivalent
Rename a field, upgrade on read Not allowed once published New fields must be optional, with no renames or type changes; breaking changes need a new name

What follows from the last row:

  • Spell's field names become permanent once shared, so the line between draft and published matters.
  • The spec suggests marking a name experimental (com.corp.experimental.newRecord) while it's a draft.
  • Not worked out yet: what a translated (Spanish) program's field names should be, on the wire.

The main catch: the word "space" sits one level differently.

  • A Spell space is a person's or org's container, with one owner.
  • An AT Protocol Space is one shared context, with many writers.
Spell today AT Protocol
owen@spell, a person An account (a DID, its permanent id), with a handle like owen.spell.spaces
@spell, an org A dedicated DID that owns spaces, and can change hands without any one person's account
owen@spell/, "your space" Closer to an account than a Space
A project's data folder, or a group A Space, named by its authority, type and key
Guest, member, admin A read flag and a write flag only
  • The rename: Spell's "space" should move down a level, so owen@spell becomes your account and a group becomes a Space. It's a real rename, and cheaper now than later.
  • Roles: they don't need protocol support.
    • A "managed space" asks an outside app about each person, and Spell could be that app.
    • Custom policies are explicitly allowed too.
  • Who may write: the write flag doesn't stop anyone writing to their own repo. It only decides whose writes get tracked, so apps have to ignore the rest.
  1. Shared rows. Every record belongs to its author, so Ana can't edit Bo's row, and atomic writes cover one person's repo only.
    • Posts, comments, likes and sign-ups are fine.
    • Spreadsheets and docs need per-person edit logs merged by the app: the crdt epic's kind of work.
  2. The no-setup start. Everyone needs an account.
    • Spell can make ana.spell.spaces for them, but that makes Spell a host: your sideline.
    • I found no email-link sign-in, so we may have to build it. That source is from 2025.
  3. Plain files. The stored form is binary archives, so export to plain JSON becomes something we write.
  4. Privacy by row. Read access is all or nothing per space.
    • Holmgren names sharing each recipe with a different audience as the poor fit.
    • "Love their art, hate their politics" is unaffected, because that's a filter on your side.
  5. Confidentiality and recall. Hosts and apps can read everything, and a removed member can keep what they already synced.
  6. Instant updates.
    • A notification says "something changed, go fetch", and goes to a reachable service, not a page.
    • Rooms stay separate.
  7. Offline. A data folder remains the local copy.
  • Consent is granted per app, for a type of space.
  • If every Spell program runs as one app, a stranger's program gets everything you granted.
  • Each program probably needs its own sign-in identity and web origin.
  • That looks possible, because a client's id is just a URL.
  • This needs a design before anything else.

Reuse rather than rethink: handles and DIDs, hosting, OAuth, account moves, member lists, sync and moderation.

Spell would run three things:

  • spell.spaces: the site, plus a sign-in identity for each program.
  • A syncer: it copies spaces and tells pages when something changed. It's a cache that can be rebuilt from people's hosts, so Spell isn't the keeper of their data.
  • An optional paid host: the sideline business, on standard host software.

The feed: running it in the browser over synced records is what makes "your algorithm" literal.

  • That should work for communities of hundreds.
  • I haven't tested how far it scales, and millions would need a server.

The window is open: they're aiming for a beta around the end of November, and shipping weekly until then.

  • Build what they asked for: Holmgren says the recipes case "deserves a blogpost & an example app of its own". The family cookbook could be that example.
  • Report back early, with a working app:
    • browser-first access, including push to pages
    • sign-up without a password
    • patterns for records people edit together
  • Offer Lexicon generation: Spell becomes an easy way to write schemas.
  1. Statusphere in Spell: Bluesky's example app. Public records only, so it needs no Spaces, and it tests the first real atmosphere write, OAuth against a real host, and using a schema someone else published.
  2. Trial: a throwaway host, plus one small Spell group app with posts, comments, a sign-up sheet and a feed you can edit, built from a Lexicon that Spell generates.
  3. Seam: a Spell host stays for local and offline use, shaped like a Space: same addressing, same member list, same per-author rows.
  4. Rename: a decision on moving Spell's "space" down a level.

Your call:

  1. The rename: does owen@spell become an account, and "space" mean the group? I'd say yes; everything else follows from it.
  2. The first app: a group space with an editable feed, or the cookbook? I'd build the group space, and use the cookbook as the blog-post example.

The readings above are mine unless quoted. A proposal "not the final specification", in an alpha that promises breaking changes.
