Spell and AT Protocol Spaces

If social is Spell's killer app, a Spell group space should be an AT Protocol Space. This page says how the two line up, and what that would cost.

Written for: Owen, to comment on, before the epic's Overview is rewritten Status: draft nothing is decided or built Researched on: 2026-11-01, by reading the specs directly. Nothing here has been run (see Sources)
  • Your social idea fits Spaces far better than the cookbook did: a forum is what Spaces was designed for.
  • The schemas can be unified by generating Lexicon, AT Protocol's schema language, from Spell's declarations.
  • Spell's word stays: each person has a space, each org has a space, and each is one AT Protocol Space.
  • A project is not a Space: it's records inside one.
  • Your idea of writing to an org's space through Spell removes the biggest losses: shared rows, needing an account, and trusting strangers' programs.
  • Its price: Spell's server becomes required, and holds the right to write for every org on it.
  • Make each person's and org's space a real AT Protocol Space, and write to an org's space through Spell.
  • Keep the way open for people with their own accounts to write from their own repos later.
  • This reverses what the epic page's question Q1 recommends.

"Imagine Facebook, but for your communit(ies), without ads, and where you can control the UI and the feed algorithm. Want to see more or less of one person or group's stuff? Love their art posts but hate their politics? Just change your feed algorithm. Want to add pages (spreadhseets, docs, sign-ups, organizer-specific pages) for an event you have coming up? Just add them to the group's space."

"I don't want to have to re-think everytying they have already figured out in terms of how to host/federate/etc. Our 'spaces' being fundamentally different than theirs would be a huge loose."

And what spell.spaces brings to mind: myspace, the first place you could establish any identity you wanted, back when making things on the web was fun because it wasn't so complicated.

Their stated aims and your idea match closely.

  • Hosting and apps: hosting is generic and boring, and each app owns its own product, so Spell can leave hosting to them.
  • Control: the ethos post lists "skepticism of centralized control of any aspect of the user's experience" among its roots. That's your feed idea.
  • Spaces: Holmgren calls a space a "shared social context" and "mini atprotos". A 100k-person forum is his design case, and a community is that, smaller.
  • Pages in a space: a space can hold records of any type, so sign-ups, sheets and docs can all sit in it.
  • What Spell adds: they want the same data remixed across apps, but building one still takes a developer. Spell lets anyone write one.

Yes, by generating Lexicon, not by replacing either side. Spell's declaration stays the only thing anyone writes.

  • Spell already generates JSON Schema from a declaration this way, so Lexicon is one more output.
  • Importing other people's Lexicons as Spell types is what makes remixing work.
Spell Lexicon Problem
a recipe is a thing A record, named like spaces.spell.x.recipe A name's owner is whoever controls its domain, so you own this one now you've bought it
Text, date, list, one of String, datetime, array, known values Mostly direct
A decimal number None Floats are banned, so store them as strings or scaled integers
A thing inside a thing, saved once, then referenced A nested object, or an at:// link Each record is capped at 1 MiB, and whole-then-reference has no direct equivalent
Rename a field, upgrade on read Not allowed once published New fields must be optional, with no renames or type changes; breaking changes need a new name

What follows from the last row:

  • Spell's field names become permanent once shared, so the line between draft and published matters.
  • The spec suggests marking a name experimental (com.corp.experimental.newRecord) while it's a draft.
  • Not worked out yet: what a translated (Spanish) program's field names should be, on the wire.

Each person has a space, and each org has a space: each is one AT Protocol Space.

  • That's your correction, and it keeps Spell's word where it is, with no rename.
  • Projects don't get a Space each: they're records inside the space.
Spell today AT Protocol
owen@spell, a person An account (a DID, its permanent id), with a handle like owen.spell.spaces
@spell, an org A dedicated DID that owns spaces, and can change hands without any one person's account
owen@spell/, "your space" One Space, on that person's account
Any space, a person's or an org's A Space, named by its authority, type and key
A project in a space Records in that Space, the project in each record's name
Viewer, editor, author, admin, in the org and again in each app A read flag and a write flag only
  • A new Space only where the audience differs: the protocol can't hide part of a Space from someone holding its credential, so a private project is either Spell's rule or a second Space.
  • Roles: they don't need protocol support.
    • A "managed space" asks an outside app about each person, and Spell could be that app.
    • Custom policies are explicitly allowed too.
  • A person's space needs controlled access too: "even in my 'personal space,' I might invite my partner to collaborate on a document, share a trip writup with my men's circle".
    • Below, "Sharing one thing from a space", says how.

This is the one case Spaces handles badly: read access is all or nothing, and Holmgren names a different audience for each recipe as the poor fit.

  • Each thing can have its own list of people; with none, it follows the space's.
  • A list can name a person, an org, or a friend list, which the people library already has.
  • The list's shape goes in now. Checking it comes after the group app, as you said of finer rules: "make sure it'll be easy to add but don't work on it now".
  • The price: your privacy is Spell's word, and only holds while reads go through it. Hosts can still read everything, either way.
  • Still to design: Holmgren's own warning, that two audiences on one thing raises whether they see each other's comments. With one list, they do.

Your idea: an org's records are all written by the org's own account, and Spell is the only one holding the right to do that, so it can enforce any rule.

Ana with no AT Protocol account Someone must make and host her one Fine: she only signs in to Spell
A stranger's program Gets what you granted, unless each has its own identity Asks Spell, which checks that program's rights

Is it still a real Space? Yes, as far as I can read.

  • The authority's own account is always allowed to read and write, under every policy.
  • It stays addressable, and can move hosts.
  • Holmgren suggests the same shape for recipes: put them in a space shared with applications, and "let those applications manage the access logic".
  • It does give up his "authority rests in the author's DID", so it's a Space with one writer.
  • People who bring their own account can write from their own repos later, because a Space allows many writers.

"Pretty straightforward, no?" The code is; the promise isn't.

  • The code: a few routes that sign in, check a level and write. The place server already carries data, and a room's referee already answers for who's in it.
  • Holding the right to write: a break-in at Spell can write to every org. Each org should keep its identity's recovery key, so it can leave.
  • Staying signed in: how long that right lasts is up to each host, says the OAuth spec. It's only ours to decide on a host we run.
  • Being needed: writes stop whenever it's down. It can be the same code anyone can run, so an org isn't tied to us.
  • Limits: one account writes for everyone.
    • Bluesky's hosts allow about 1,666 new records an hour per account, by the figure in our own code.
    • I don't know if that covers Spaces, and our own host could set its own.
  • Big spaces: a page can't ask for one project's changes, as the draft's change feed shows no such filter. So the server keeps a copy and answers pages from it.

The four levels, as I read your order: viewer reads; editor changes what's there; author also makes new apps and pages; admin manages people. These replace today's guest, member, admin.

Spell is the editing platform, and AT Protocol is where things are kept and published. That's your framing, with one addition, because AT Protocol does two jobs.

Layer Its job Example
Spell Making, editing, running, and enforcing the rules The sign-up sheet's program, and who may change it
A Space Keeping the shared data, for those allowed in The group's sign-ups
Public records Publishing to anyone: signed, and rebroadcast by the network A guide, or a public status like Statusphere's

What it gives us:

  • A real publish step: field names only have to freeze when published, which answers the Lexicon limit above. Until then, a draft can change freely.
  • Spell not holding people's data: this only stays true if shared data lives in a Space, not in Spell, with Spell keeping a copy.

Addresses are Spell's too. People see owen@spell, which reads like an email and is friendly.

  • at:// stays on the wire: nobody types it or reads it.
  • Spell keeps the permanent id behind the name, since handles change.
  • The catch: it looks like an email address, but mail sent to it goes nowhere, unless we make it work.

Writing through Spell removes several of these, as the section above says. Those marked "if own repos" only apply to people writing from their own.

  1. Shared rows, if own repos. Every record belongs to its author, so Ana can't edit Bo's row, and atomic writes cover one repo only.
  2. The no-setup start, if own repos. Everyone needs an account, and I found no email-link sign-in on the standard host: that source is from 2025.
  3. Plain files. The stored form is binary archives, so export to plain JSON becomes something we write.
  4. Privacy by row, unless reads go through Spell. Read access is all or nothing per space.
    • Holmgren names sharing each recipe with a different audience as the poor fit.
    • "Love their art, hate their politics" is unaffected, because that's a filter on your side.
  5. Confidentiality and recall. Hosts and apps can read everything, and a removed member can keep what they already synced.
  6. Instant updates, if own repos.
    • A notification says "something changed, go fetch", and goes to a reachable service, not a page.
    • Rooms stay separate either way.
  7. Offline. A data folder remains the local copy.
  • A stranger's program must not get everything you granted to Spell.
  • Through Spell, programs never hold your grant: they ask the server, which checks that program's rights.
  • Those rights still need a design, and each program still wants its own web origin.

Reuse rather than rethink: handles and DIDs, hosting, OAuth, account moves, member lists, sync and moderation.

Spell would run three things:

  • spell.spaces: the site.
  • A space server: it signs people in, checks levels, writes for each org, and answers pages from its copy. The copy can be rebuilt from the org's host, so Spell isn't the keeper of their data, but it is the way in.
  • An optional paid host: the sideline business, on standard host software.

The feed: running it in the browser over synced records is what makes "your algorithm" literal.

  • That should work for communities of hundreds, from the server's copy.
  • I haven't tested how far it scales, and millions would need a server.

The window is open: they're aiming for a beta around the end of November, and shipping weekly until then.

  • Build what they asked for: Holmgren says the recipes case "deserves a blogpost & an example app of its own". The family cookbook could be that example.
  • Report back early, with a working app:
    • browser-first access, including push to pages
    • sign-up without a password
    • patterns for records people edit together
  • Offer Lexicon generation: Spell becomes an easy way to write schemas.
  1. Statusphere in Spell: Bluesky's example app. Public records only, so it needs no Spaces, and it tests the first real atmosphere write, OAuth against a real host, and using a schema someone else published.
  2. Trial: a throwaway host, a small space server writing for one org, and one Spell group app with posts, comments, a sign-up sheet and a feed you can edit, built from a Lexicon that Spell generates.
  3. Seam: a Spell host stays for local and offline use, shaped like a Space: same addressing (owen@spell/recipes, never at://), same member list.

Your call:

  1. Write to an org's space through Spell? I'd say yes, reads too, keeping own-repo writers possible later.
  2. Are the four levels right, as I read them in "Writing to an org's space through Spell"?
  3. The first app: a group space with an editable feed, or the cookbook? I'd build the group space, and use the cookbook as the blog-post example.
