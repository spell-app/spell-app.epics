Spell Hosts Your Data

One line of Spell keeps a program's data on Spell's server, in a space of its own, shaped like AT Protocol Spaces so Bluesky's servers can take it over later.

## Family Cookbook:  your recipes, saved in the cloud
// The next line keeps them in your own space on Spell's server:  sign in on any device, and they're there.
// Until you sign in, they're kept in this browser, and the page says so;  once you do, they go up.
// What's drawn and done with them is in FamilyCookbook.spellml.
family cookbook is saved in the cloud

a "recipe" is a thing:
	- it lives in family cookbook
	- its "title" is text
	- its "servings" is a number
	- its "notes" is text
  • That line compiles to a project data folder of each person's own, on Spell's cloud server: your space's __data/family-cookbook.
  • Signed out, every change stays in this browser, and the sign-in bar says so.
  • Signed in, the changes go up within seconds, and every device you sign in on sees them.
  • In Spanish: recetario familiar se guarda en la nube.

A Firebase or Supabase app gets the same thing from an SDK, a console and a config file. Here it's the sentence, and the rest of the program doesn't change: the recipes read and save the way they did on this device.

Real today: Ana's story, checked headless and against a real cloud server on this machine.

  • Laptop and phone, then a cookbook shared with Bo.
  • Not yet: a public address. Going live waits on a machine, its https address, and a real mail service.

The goal was never for Spell to hold people's data, but for people to hold their own somewhere. AT Protocol Spaces, which holds private data with access control, is that somewhere, but it's an alpha.

  • So Spell runs a small server of its own, invite only, shaped like a Space.
  • A space is a person's (owen@spell) or a group's (@family); its project data folders hold a program's rows.
  • Writes go through Spell's server, as the space; programs read a space straight from its files.
  • When Spaces is ready, Bluesky's server can take over the same data.
  • family cookbook is a data folder at "owen@spell/recipes" keeps its rows in that space's folder, through one page-server route.
  • "recipes" alone is the program's own folder.
  • personal "recipes" is one per person (Spanish: carpeta de datos personal en "recetas").
  • A project data folder is asked at most every 5 seconds: check it every is raised to that, with a warning at the line.
  • The recipe box moves both ways between a project data folder and an atmosphere account.
  • Ana asks to sign in on her laptop, and gets an email with a link and a 6-digit code.
  • The link works on any device: its page first says where the request came from (browser, system, this machine or this network), with one button.
  • Opening it on her phone signs in the laptop that asked, too.
  • Invite only: an invite makes her Spell account, ana@spell, in the people library.
  • A wrong code says so, and it keeps waiting; 5 wrong codes end the request, and so do 20 minutes with nothing done (SPELL_SIGN_IN_MINUTES sets the time).
  • Until a mail service is picked, mail lands in a catcher on this machine, listed at /sign-in/mail, or read with spell app cloud mail.
  • A space's own folder (@family/cookbook) is written by its members; a program's own data is each row's owner's only.
  • Every record in a members' folder is the space's (owner: "@family"), with by naming who saved it: Ana and Bo editing one recipe keep one record.
  • Guests read, members write, admins manage the list. A member may always leave; the last admin can't.
  • Anyone else is refused, in English and Spanish: "Your role can't write here: you aren't a member of @family".
  • Members and guests read @family/cookbook at /api/reads/spaces/<address>/, with a credential for the whole space.
  • Reading keeps working while writes are stopped.
  • The credential lasts 30 days, in a cookie only that space's read address receives. Every read checks the member list again, so someone who leaves is cut off at once.
  • Never served: a folder's member list, dot files, nested folders.

The cloud is its own server, apart from the page server, on port 4949, for people outside this machine.

It serves sign-in, project data folders, the /terms page and the built-in programs, and nothing else.

  • Takedowns: a Spell admin hides, deletes or restores a record, a space or an account, with a reason: spell app takedown hide | delete | restore | list.
    • Each is a line in the folder's own log (who, when, what, why), never listed, read or copied out.
    • A hidden record drops out on both read paths at once; a restore brings it back, live pages too.
    • A hidden account can't sign in, be invited or get mail.
    • A refusal says "taken down" and where to ask, never why: HTTP 451, with the abuse contact (abuse@spell.spaces by default), which also shows on the sign-in bar.
  • Public read and public write: each space has both, off until an admin turns them on, as Spaces has them. Under public write, anyone signed in adds rows and changes only their own.
  • Limits: 1 MB a thing, 100 MB a space. A save over either is refused, says how big, and stays on the page.
  • Export: members download a space as a zip of plain JSON, or run spell app export; the folder runs the program with one line changed.
  • Delete: admins delete a space, or spell app delete a space or an account; the takedown log is kept.
  • Backups: every space copied nightly, a week kept, a missed night logged: spell app backup now | list | status | restore.
  • The promise to hand over a space's keys later is on the /terms page.
 publishing one guide · 2 lines 
ATMOSPHERE_APP_PASSWORD=... epic pages publish guides/atmosphere/spaces-and-spell.html \
  --site <url> --as <handle>
  • It writes a Standard.site record in your account: the title, description, address and a link back. The page itself stays ours.
  • Publishing again updates the same record.
  • The guide gets a back-link tag in its head, which is how Standard.site checks the record is the page's (--no-link leaves it alone).
  • --dry-run shows the record without signing in.
  • The app password comes from the environment, never a flag, so it's in no shell history.

Bluesky's example app runs in Spell: pick an emoji, and see everyone's statuses, newest first, live. Its status type was read in from Statusphere's own Lexicon.

// - "status" holds at most 32 bytes:  Spell says lengths in letters
a "status" is a thing:
	- it is published as "xyz.statusphere.status"
	- its "status" is required text, with maximum length 1
	- its "created at" is a required date
  • spell compile writes a Lexicon beside each library type's JSON Schema.
  • A type marked - it is published (Spanish - se publica) gets editor warnings about decimals and renames.
  • spell app lexicon publish writes the schema records, and prints the DNS record to make.
  • A length rule counts an emoji as one letter, and a new date is now.

A program never knows where its data lives: it talks to a place, and the line it was compiled from picks which.

  • Writes go to one route of Spell's server, which checks who's asking (one sign-in check, read by the page server's token, rooms, places, prefs and the page alike) and the space's member list.
  • Reads skip the server: the page's chunked reader asks for the folder's files directly, with the space's credential, asked for once when refused.
  • The cloud is the same project data folder on another server: signed out it asks nothing and keeps changes in the browser, through the same save queue a failed save uses.
  • An atmosphere account is one more place: records are written as the person, decimals kept as text ("12.50") and read back as numbers.
  • Spell's own server, shaped like Spaces, over waiting for Spaces: a Spaces host is 27 server calls on a full account server, and its proposal calls itself "very much a work in progress".
  • Writes through Spell as the space, reads direct, over each person writing their own records: Spaces never lets one person change another's record, so a shared recipe is simply the group's.
  • An emailed code as well as a link, over a link alone: a link opened on another device can't finish AT Protocol's sign-in, which belongs to the browser that started it.
  • The cloud as its own server, over the page server: the page server is for this machine, and the cloud is for everyone else.
  • A takedown path and an abuse contact first, over reports and appeals: those come before Spell opens to everyone.
  • A guide published by an app password, over the browser sign-in: a command has no browser to come back to.
  • No public address yet, and no real mail: codes are read from the catcher.
  • One real atmosphere write, and the Spaces trial, are still to do; the trial waits for Spaces' beta.
  • Drafts still belong to this machine's person, whoever's signed in.
  • Pending sign-ins live in memory: a restart loses them. Nothing limits how often one email can ask.
  • A hidden account stays signed in where it already was.
  • Shared rooms and the editor's files route don't check takedowns yet.
  • Downloading a space needs a sign-in or the run token; deleting your own account is a command, not a page.
  • Backups cover spell.spaces only: not the sign-ins folder, nor the built-in spaces git keeps.
  • Spell's record keys aren't Statusphere's timestamps, so Bluesky's own Statusphere may skip Spell's statuses.
  • Behind a tunnel, the sign-in link's page says "this machine" for everyone.
  • A Standard.site record is a loose document: no publication record, no text or tags.
