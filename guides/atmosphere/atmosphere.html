Spell Hosts Your Data

One line of Spell keeps a program's data on Spell's server, in a space of its own, shaped like AT Protocol Spaces so Bluesky's servers can take it over later.

## Family Cookbook:  your recipes, saved in the cloud
// The next line keeps them in your own space on Spell's server:  sign in on any device, and they're there.
// Until you sign in, they're kept in this browser, and the page says so;  once you do, they go up.
// What's drawn and done with them is in FamilyCookbook.spellml.
family cookbook is saved in the cloud

a "recipe" is a thing:
	- it lives in family cookbook
	- its "title" is text
	- its "servings" is a number
	- its "notes" is text
  • That line compiles to a project data folder of each person's own, on Spell's cloud server: your space's __data/family-cookbook.
  • Signed out, every change stays in this browser, and the sign-in bar says so.
  • Signed in, the changes go up within seconds, and every device you sign in on sees them.
  • In Spanish: recetario familiar se guarda en la nube.

A Firebase or Supabase app gets the same thing from an SDK, a console and a config file. Here it's the sentence, and the rest of the program doesn't change: the recipes read and save the way they did on this device.

Real today: Ana's story, checked headless and against a real cloud server on this machine.

  • Laptop and phone, then a cookbook shared with Bo.
  • Not yet: a public address. Going live waits on a machine, its https address, and a real mail service.

The goal was never for Spell to hold people's data, but for people to hold their own somewhere. AT Protocol Spaces, which holds private data with access control, is that somewhere, but it's an alpha.

  • So Spell runs a small server of its own, invite only, shaped like a Space.
  • A space is a person's (owen@spell) or a group's (@family); its project data folders hold a program's rows.
  • Writes go through Spell's server, as the space; programs read a space straight from its files.
  • When Spaces is ready, Bluesky's server can take over the same data.
  • It has now been tried on Bluesky's own Spaces alpha, with two accounts there, and it worked: below, "Trying it on the Spaces alpha".
  • family cookbook is a data folder at "owen@spell/recipes" keeps its rows in that space's folder, through one page-server route.
  • "recipes" alone is the program's own folder.
  • personal "recipes" is one per person (Spanish: carpeta de datos personal en "recetas").
  • A project data folder is asked at most every 5 seconds: check it every is raised to that, with a warning at the line.
  • The recipe box moves both ways between a project data folder and an atmosphere account.
  • Ana asks to sign in on her laptop, and gets an email with a link and a 6-digit code.
  • The link works on any device: its page first says where the request came from (browser, system, this machine or this network), with one button.
  • Opening it on her phone signs in the laptop that asked, too.
  • Invite only: an invite makes her Spell account, ana@spell, in the people library.
  • A wrong code says so, and it keeps waiting; 5 wrong codes end the request, and so do 20 minutes with nothing done (SPELL_SIGN_IN_MINUTES sets the time).
  • Until a mail service is picked, mail lands in a catcher on this machine, listed at /sign-in/mail, or read with spell space cloud mail.
  • A space's own folder (@family/cookbook) is written by its members; a program's own data is each row's owner's only.
  • Every record in a members' folder is the space's (owner: "@family"), with by naming who saved it: Ana and Bo editing one recipe keep one record.
  • Everyone on a space's list is a member, with one of three roles:
    • viewer: reads, and posts and comments where the space allows it
    • editor: also adds rows and files, and changes any of them; removes only their own
    • manager: also makes new pages and apps, publishes, removes anything, and adds people
  • Changing a program's own pages is a right of its own, design: managers have it, and an org gives it to anyone else ("Ana designs the picnic pages").
  • A member may always leave; the last manager can't.
  • Anyone else is refused, in English and Spanish: "Your role can't write here: you aren't a member of @family". A refusal names the role it takes: "it takes an editor".
  • Signed in by email, Ana links her Bluesky account from the sign-in bar; after that, "Sign in with Bluesky" signs her in too.
  • Spell asks Bluesky only who she is, never for the right to post as her.
  • Still invite only: an account nobody linked, or one taken down, is refused, in her language.

An org, like RS, is set up with one command, and its roles decide who reaches each of its spaces.

Setting up an org 
spell space org create rs --name "RS" --admin ana@example.org --team "Events Team"
  • It makes the org's file, its Admin space (@rs/__admin), its first Admins and teams, and their spaces, and invites each Admin. Run again, it makes nothing twice.
  • The roles: the Board (the org's Org Managers), the Admins, and each team's and project's Managers, each held by a person or a whole team, from a day until a day.
  • Two spaces each: the org, the Admins, every team and every project have a shared space everyone in the org reads (events_team@rs) and a private one only their Managers reach (events_team@rs/__private).
  • Lists follow the roles: nobody types a space's member list. A role that ends takes the person off at once; spell space org audit rs says where anything differs.
  • The Admin page, the built-in Org Admin (and its Spanish twin, Administración de la Org): people, teams and projects, who manages what, who designs what, approved apps, event stages, and the log of every change.
  • Recovery: if the Admins are gone, the Board names new ones; if the Board is lost too, recovery codes split among the org's people bring it back (spell space org codes, spell space org recover).
  • Members' own spaces: Ana has one in RS, ana@rs, which only she writes. Leaving the org (spell space org leave) hands it to her.
  • Drafts: each person's drafts are their own; in an org they're kept in the private space, and only a space's Managers publish.
  • Everyone in an org has an inbox, @rs/__inbox to a program: sending a message writes a copy into each recipient's. The Board and the Admins can read inboxes too, and the page says so.
  • A message can point to one of the org's pages, which opens with the facts it was sent.
  • Each person picks how they hear of it: a mail per message, a daily digest (the default), or none. Every mail has a one-click stop link.
  • Anyone can follow a shared space: each new post, row or file there arrives in their inbox as an update.
  • Real mail waits on a mail service: until then, it lands in the catcher on this machine.
  • See it in the built-in Inbox.
  • @rs/__feed is each person's feed: the posts of every space in the org they can read, quiet events and inboxes left out.
  • Each person ranks it in their own copy of the program: Bo's shows more of Ana's art and none of her politics.
  • With - keep a copy on this device, opening it again asks only for what changed. See it in the built-in Feed.
  • Every team, project and event has an open thread in its shared space and an organizers' thread in its private one, with nothing to set up: team chat is a room at "events_team@rs/messages".
  • A message shows on everyone's page at once; everyone deletes their own, Managers anyone's.
  • The Board and the Admins start threads of their own. 200 messages take about a second.
  • See it in the built-in Chat: every thread you can read down its side, like Slack's channels, the one you picked beside it.
  • Anyone in the org proposes an event; the Events Team's Managers approve it, which makes it a project with its organizers as Managers. It moves from Planning through Open and Analysis to Quiet, when its spaces stop taking writes.
  • A sign-up sheet is a data folder with a few lines:
A sign-up sheet's lines 
- open to sign-ups from "picnic@rs/sheet"
- only its attendees add rows
- at most 2 rows for each shift
- each person asks for at most their "invitations" tickets
- confirmed tickets join "picnic@rs/__attendees"
  • Attendees add their own rows to a master they can't read; when two ask for the last place, one gets it.
  • The letter shows each reader the day, their own shifts and how many invitations they have left.
  • See them in the built-in Org Events, Sign-Up Sheet and Picnic Letter.
  • A projection writes part of one space's data into another, where its readers can see it.
    • It's a sentence: the published sheet is a projection of the sign up sheet.
    • Its lines say where it goes, which fields it shows, and which rows.
    • A command lists them and rebuilds one: spell space projection.
  • Files (a picture, a program's source) go in a space as their bytes and one record naming them, as a Space keeps them, within the space's 100 MB.
  • Handles: ana@rs is ana.rs.spell.space to AT Protocol, and Spell answers its handle look-up.
  • Members and guests read @family/cookbook at /api/reads/spaces/<address>/, with a credential for the whole space.
  • Reading keeps working while writes are stopped.
  • The credential lasts 30 days, in a cookie only that space's read address receives. Every read checks the member list again, so someone who leaves is cut off at once.
  • Never served: a folder's member list, dot files, nested folders.

The cloud is its own server, apart from the page server, on port 4949, for people outside this machine.

spell space cloud --help · what it printed 
Commands:
  serve [options]    run it on this machine, for whatever gives it its address
                     (a tunnel, a proxy), until Ctrl-C
  status             is it running, where, and its backups;  exits 1 when it
                     isn't, or a night was missed
  invite <email>     make an account for an email, and send its mail:  the
                     running cloud's own invite
  mail [email]       the newest mail it caught, with its link and code:  until a
                     mail service is picked
  service [options]  print the file that starts it with this machine, and again
                     if it stops:  launchd's, or systemd's

It serves sign-in, project data folders, the /terms page and the built-in programs, and nothing else.

  • Takedowns: a Spell admin hides, deletes or restores a record, a space or an account, with a reason: spell space takedown hide | delete | restore | list.
    • Each is a line in the folder's own log (who, when, what, why), never listed, read or copied out.
    • A hidden record drops out on both read paths at once; a restore brings it back, live pages too.
    • A hidden account can't sign in, be invited or get mail.
    • A refusal says "taken down" and where to ask, never why: HTTP 451, with the abuse contact (abuse@spell.spaces by default), which also shows on the sign-in bar.
  • Public read and public write: each space has both, off until an admin turns them on, as Spaces has them. Under public write, anyone signed in adds rows and changes only their own.
  • Limits: 1 MB a thing, 100 MB a space. A save over either is refused, says how big, and stays on the page.
  • Export: members download a space as a zip of plain JSON, or run spell space export; the folder runs the program with one line changed.
  • Delete: admins delete a space, or spell space delete a space or an account; the takedown log is kept.
  • Backups: every space copied nightly, a week kept, a missed night logged: spell space backup now | list | status | restore.
  • The promise to hand over a space's keys later is on the /terms page.
 publishing one guide · 2 lines 
ATMOSPHERE_APP_PASSWORD=... epic pages publish guides/atmosphere/spaces-and-spell.html \
  --site <url> --as <handle>
  • It writes a Standard.site record in your account: the title, description, address and a link back. The page itself stays ours.
  • Publishing again updates the same record.
  • The guide gets a back-link tag in its head, which is how Standard.site checks the record is the page's (--no-link leaves it alone).
  • --dry-run shows the record without signing in.
  • The app password comes from the environment, never a flag, so it's in no shell history.

Bluesky's example app runs in Spell: pick an emoji, and see everyone's statuses, newest first, live. Its status type was read in from Statusphere's own Lexicon.

Status.spell, written by spell space lexicon read · 6 lines

// - "status" holds at most 32 bytes:  Spell says lengths in letters
a "status" is a thing:
	- it is published as "xyz.statusphere.status"
	- its "status" is required text, with maximum length 1
	- its "created at" is a required date
  • spell compile writes a Lexicon beside each library type's JSON Schema.
  • A type marked - it is published (Spanish - se publica) gets editor warnings about decimals and renames.
  • spell space lexicon publish writes the schema records, and prints the DNS record to make.
  • A length rule counts an emoji as one letter, and a new date is now.

The recipe box was saved to a real Space on Bluesky's alpha host, on 2026-11-05: the test org's account made the space, and a second account joined it as a member. Every step passed.

  • Making it: the org made a members-only space of Spell's own type, app.spell.data.space. The alpha took it, though nothing publishes that type yet.
  • Reading: a read credential lasts 10 minutes, and is tied to a key the program holds. Each read is signed with that key, and each record's checksum is checked.
  • A member: added with a read and a write flag, the second account read the org's recipe, wrote one of its own (kept in its own account), and joined the list of writers.
  • Someone outside: asking for a credential, they're told the space isn't found (SpaceNotFound), so a private space doesn't give itself away.
  • Only the owner manages: the member trying to add someone was refused (NotSpaceOwner).
  • Removing someone: they leave the writers at once, and a new credential is refused. The catch: the one they already hold still reads, until its 10 minutes are up.
  • Public read: turned on, a signed-out reader got in; turned off again, the box was copied in (2 recipes) and listed back, then the records cleared and the space deleted.

Not yet: each member's own host has to run Spaces. An ordinary bsky.social account could be added to the list, but its host answered "not implemented" (501) when it asked to read or clear its records. The alpha also took an app password's sign-in for every call, though Spaces' proposal says OAuth only, so a later release may not.

Running the trial again

It's a test that skips itself unless the alpha accounts are given; it writes a line per step to its log. The code: AtProtoSpace, AtProtoSpacePlace and spacesTrial.test.ts.

A program never knows where its data lives: it talks to a place, and the line it was compiled from picks which.

  • Writes go to one route of Spell's server, which checks who's asking (one sign-in check, read by the page server's token, rooms, places, prefs and the page alike) and their role in the space: one table of roles, and an org's roles work each list out.
  • Reads skip the server: the page's chunked reader asks for the folder's files directly, with the space's credential, asked for once when refused.
  • The cloud is the same project data folder on another server: signed out it asks nothing and keeps changes in the browser, through the same save queue a failed save uses.
  • Rooms (live pages, chat) write the space as each page's person, so the same checks hold.
  • One retry queue, on the server's disk, carries every write that has to go somewhere else: a real Space, a projection's target, an inbox's copy, a mail.
  • An atmosphere account is one more place: records are written as the person, decimals kept as text ("12.50") and read back as numbers.
  • Spell's own server, shaped like Spaces, over waiting for Spaces: a Spaces host is 27 server calls on a full account server, and its proposal calls itself "very much a work in progress".
  • Writes through Spell as the space, reads direct, over each person writing their own records: Spaces never lets one person change another's record, so a shared recipe is simply the group's.
  • An emailed code as well as a link, over a link alone: a link opened on another device can't finish AT Protocol's sign-in, which belongs to the browser that started it.
  • The cloud as its own server, over the page server: the page server is for this machine, and the cloud is for everyone else.
  • A takedown path and an abuse contact first, over reports and appeals: those come before Spell opens to everyone.
  • Lists worked out from roles, over lists typed per space: a change to a team reaches every project's spaces at once, and nothing drifts.
  • A separate right to design, over one role for both: building an org's pages and managing its people are different jobs.
  • A guide published by an app password, over the browser sign-in: a command has no browser to come back to.
  • No public address yet, and no real mail: codes are read from the catcher.
  • Spaces is tried, not used: Spell's data stays on its own server until Spaces leaves its alpha, and a removed member reads for up to 10 more minutes there.
  • A Spaces member needs an account on a host that runs Spaces: a bsky.social account can't join yet.
  • Pending sign-ins live in memory: a restart loses them. Nothing limits how often one email can ask.
  • A hidden account stays signed in where it already was.
  • Shared rooms and the editor's files route don't check takedowns yet.
  • Downloading a space needs a sign-in or the run token; deleting your own account is a command, not a page.
  • Backups cover spell.spaces only: not the sign-ins folder, nor the built-in spaces git keeps.
  • Spell's record keys aren't Statusphere's timestamps, so Bluesky's own Statusphere may skip Spell's statuses.
  • Behind a tunnel, the sign-in link's page says "this machine" for everyone.
  • A Standard.site record is a loose document: no publication record, no text or tags.
  • The organizers' forms on a sign-up sheet show to everyone; the server refuses anyone else's write.
  • A write refused because its event is Quiet says nothing on a page without notices.
  • The built-in pages are English only, but Org Admin, which has a Spanish twin.
  • The phone app, and real mail, are still to come.
