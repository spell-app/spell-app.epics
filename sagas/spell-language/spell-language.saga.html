How spell reads and what it can say: precedence, types, outlines, mixins, things with ids.

Spell reads better and says more: operators bind as they should, types are checked in spell's own words, and a type can be written as an outline.

  • Precedence and Types, built 2026-10-04 and merged 2026-10-05: operators bind by a precedence table, and every match carries a datatype.
    • So signatures are paren-free, calls are checked by type, and multi-word members resolve through their type.
    • "A pile is an exclusive list of cards" owns its cards.
  • Outline Spell, built on its branch, not merged: a type's heading line, then a bulleted list of what's true of it, compiling to exactly what the sentence style does.
    • Solitaire's cards rewritten as outlines (the OutlineSolitaire example), and a "Reads as" hover.
  • Markdown on the parser, merged: GitHub-flavoured markdown on the generic parser, proving it isn't tied to spell; it serves docstrings and a live preview.
  • People and Orgs, all 18 phases done: every spell thing gets an id and is saved in its own readable file, and lists point at things by address.
    • Person and Org written in spell, one address scheme (owen@spell/solitaire), and a small "You" panel.

The next language ideas are a future epic and the parked goals' topics.

  • Mixins, a future epic: "a card is a drawable, sortable thing" takes behaviour from several sources at once, and Spell UI elements would compose the same way.
  • The TypeScript check of Precedence and Types is its own later epic (its T1).
  • From the parked goals, Thingverse: standard libraries of everyday ideas (people, bank accounts, invoices, calendars) that teach spell new words.
  • From the parked goals, Translation: spell in Spanish first, then Norwegian and Portuguese. Spanish is built (): Solitario, Solitaire in Spanish, compiles and runs; Norwegian and Portuguese aren't started.
  • From the parked goals, AI: spell as "the language AI writes for you, so you can read it".
